## 1. Why is Samsung handing cash back as dividends while SK hynix cancels shares?

**Why it matters: **What gets decided here is whether the money earned at the top of the memory cycle goes back to shareholders now or stays for the next cycle's fabs. It comes out of one account, so both cannot happen in full.

Samsung Electronics is expected to hold a board meeting before the end of August to approve a shareholder return package that includes a special cash dividend, and the total is reported to exceed 100 trillion won.
Reuters carried the same reporting as a return programme of more than $72 billion, and the shares rose more than 9% on August 20.

@@REF@@Samsung jumps 9% on record shareholder return hopes (Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-08-20)|https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/08/20/samsung-jumps-9-percent-on-record-shareholder-return-hopes|https://wimg.sedaily.com/news/cms/2026/08/20/news-p.v1.20260820.04b8191431824653a8cf7e61a9e58447_P1.jpg

None of this has been confirmed in a company filing yet. It is at the reporting stage.
The size of the number is what makes the story legible.

## Where 100 trillion won comes from

@@CHK@@Net cash, end of June|167.59 trillion won|Second-quarter operating profit|89.5 trillion won|First-half capital spending|28 trillion won@@The first box is what a year of rising memory prices left inside the company. It doubled in twelve months. Paying that out would not empty the account, but it comes out of the same wallet as the third box.@@Retrieved 2026-08-20. Net cash per Korea Herald, 2026-08-11; operating profit from Samsung's second-quarter 2026 results, 2026-07-30; capital spending per Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-08-14.

Samsung has run its returns in three-year programmes.
It returned 33.5 trillion won from 2015 to 2017, 39.6 trillion won from 2018 to 2020, and 29.4 trillion won from 2021 to 2023.

All three together come to 102.5 trillion won.
This would do that in one move.

## What separates a dividend from a cancellation

There are two ways to hand money back.
A cash dividend moves money from the company's account to the shareholder's account.
A share cancellation has the company buy its own stock and destroy it, so fewer shares remain and each surviving share owns more.

The difference that matters here is the ownership percentage.
When the share count falls, a holder who kept the same number of shares sees their percentage rise on its own.

A day earlier, on August 19, SK hynix said it would buy back and cancel 40 trillion won of its own stock, some 24.07 million shares, or about 3.3% of those outstanding.

@@REF@@SK hynix launches record $29b share buyback and cancellation after stock loses half its value (Korea JoongAng Daily, 2026-08-19)|https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/sk-hynix-launches-record-29b-share-buyback-and-cancellation-after-stock-loses-half-its-value/12832819|https://image.koreajoongangdaily.com/12816584.jpg?imageId=12816584&x=0&y=0&cropw=100&croph=100&panox=0&panoy=0&panow=100&panoh=100&width=1200&height=683

SK hynix's largest shareholder is SK Square, which holds 20.1%.
Korean fair trade law requires a company under a holding structure to keep at least 20% of a listed subsidiary.
That 20.1% leaves a tenth of a percentage point of room.

Cancellation works in the direction of widening that room.
Samsung has no such percentage constraint, which is why the original post reads dividends as the easier instrument for its governance structure.

## The price of tripling the payout

@@CMP@@The case for paying out|This cash piled up at the top of the memory cycle. A company that does not return it now will have nothing to return in the downturn.|The case for holding|The same cash is what pays for the US fabs and the next process node. Take it out now and the next expansion has to be borrowed.

The split is whether the same cash is money to spend now or money already spoken for.

First-half capital spending ran 28 trillion won, which points to roughly 60 trillion for the year.
On the foundry side the second Taylor fab in Texas is set to break ground at year-end, and on the memory side the Pyeongtaek lines keep expanding.
This payout shares an account with those plans.

There is a policy backdrop as well.
Korea moved dividend income tax to a progressive schedule in January, from 14% below 20 million won to 30% above 5 billion won.
Larger dividends therefore carry a tax bill for controlling shareholders, so choosing dividends anyway says the governance reason outweighs the tax one.

## What the August board meeting settles

A 9% move on an unconfirmed number means the board resolution either confirms the expectation or trims it.
If the total clears 100 trillion won with a special dividend inside it, the reporting was right. If it lands below that, or if no resolution comes in August, the market ran ahead.
After that, watch the mix. How that total splits between dividends and cancellations shows whether this company is returning cash for reasons that sit in the ownership register or in the cash flow statement.

## 2. On the evening of August 19, Trump attached a warning to the countries that trade with Iran

**Why it matters: **When the target of a sanction moves outside Iran, so does the party that pays for it. Whether Chinese refiners and Gulf settlement windows actually land on the list moves crude flows and shipping costs together.

A warning aimed at third countries that trade with Iran came on the evening of August 19.

Declaring an "economic D-Day" against Iran, President Trump said that any country letting its financial institutions, businesses, airports or government entities provide a lifeline to Iran would itself pay a price.

@@REF@@Trump announces 'most crushing economic operation ever' against Iran (Al Jazeera, 2026-08-19)|https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/19/trump-announces-most-crushing-economic-operation-ever-against-iran|https://www.aljazeera.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/ap_6a863c2b9f2cd-1787182123_e60870-1787182171.jpg?resize=1200%2C630&quality=80

Al Jazeera notes the announcement carried no implementation detail.
No new Iran designation appeared on the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control list that day.

## Six thousand measures are already in place

Iran is the second most sanctioned country in the world.
The National reports that more than 6,000 measures are already attached to it.

Since April an operation called Economic Fury has been running.
It stacks a US naval blockade of Iranian ports on top of the sanctions already covering oil, shipping and finance.

This announcement adds to that, and what is newly in the frame is not Iran but the parties that trade with it.
That is the logic of secondary sanctions: to isolate a country you have to move the countries that deal with it.

## 3. What stopped in Russia is not the crude but the plants that boil it

**Why it matters: **When refineries stop, crude and refined fuel move in opposite directions. Crude that cannot be processed is pushed out as exports while gasoline and diesel get scarcer, so reading the whole energy complex off the crude price misses what is happening here.

What has stopped in Russia is not the wells that lift crude but the plants that turn crude into fuel. Bloomberg's tally of Russian refinery runs fell to 3.6 million barrels a day in July 2026, the lowest since May 2002. Ukrainian drones now reach refineries more than a thousand kilometres from the border.

@@REF@@Russian oil refining falls to 24-year low after Ukrainian drone strikes (The Moscow Times, 2026-08-03)|https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/08/03/russian-oil-refining-falls-to-24-year-low-after-ukrainian-drone-strikes-bloomberg-a93404|https://static.themoscowtimes.com/image/og/ff/93404__ff3faa8a3a1cbf69dd7995191750a478.jpg

## The denominator decides whether it is half

Steve Hanke wrote that half of Russia's refining capacity has been knocked out. That share depends entirely on what sits in the denominator.

@@CMP@@Hanke's reading|Half the nameplate capacity is down. Repairs run into months and more strikes are coming, so stay long gasoline and diesel.|The throughput reading|Russia normally ran 5.3 to 5.6 million barrels a day. At 3.6 million it has lost roughly a third, which is short of half.

The same damage produces two numbers depending on whether you count against the capacity written on paper or against what the plants actually processed in normal times. Russia's nameplate refining capacity is put at roughly 6.8 million barrels a day. Measure against that and the idled share approaches half; measure against recent operating rates and it lands near a third. Ukraine's general staff claimed in early July that 42.7% had been disabled, which is also a nameplate figure and comes from a party to the war.

@@REF@@Ukraine's drones reach 1,500 km into Russia, setting two major oil and petrochemical sites ablaze (Kyiv Post, 2026-07-14)|https://www.kyivpost.com/post/80209|https://static.kyivpost.com/storage/2026/07/14/fa71b1c30b62c3a1d1a429a8b3bd397d.jpg?w=1200&q=90&f=jpg

## Crude piles up while diesel runs short

A refinery is where crude is boiled and separated into gasoline and diesel. When the plant stops, the crude does not vanish. It leaves the country as crude instead.

Prices therefore split in two directions.

More crude looks for buyers and its price is held down, while less refined fuel is made and its price rises. Put simply, the wheat is still there and the mill has burned; what gets expensive is flour, not wheat. On July 8 the US Gulf Coast diesel crack reached 64.58 dollars a barrel, close to a record, and European refining margins were above 60 dollars a barrel in the same stretch. Brent settled at 92.42 dollars a barrel on August 18.

The repair timeline follows from the same physical fact. What burns is the crude distillation unit or the catalytic cracker, equipment that takes a long time to build, and the spare parts and catalysts for it are hard to buy under Western sanctions.

## The door Russia shut until January 2027

@@TIME@@2026-06-28|Ukraine strikes two Russian refineries in one night|2026-07-14|Salavat petrochemical complex hit, 1,500 km from the border|2026-07-30|Russia extends its gasoline and diesel export ban to end-January 2027|2026-08-03|Bloomberg puts refinery runs at 3.6 million barrels a day, a 24-year low|>2027-01-31|Russian fuel export ban scheduled to end@@The strikes are not one event but six weeks of accumulation, and in the middle of it Russia closed its own export door.@@The Moscow Times, Kyiv Post, retrieved 2026-08-19

An export ban is a government conceding that fuel is short at home. Russian domestic gasoline stood at 77.89 roubles a litre on July 27, up 19% from the start of the year. If refinery runs climb back toward 4.5 million barrels a day, the months-long repair estimate was too pessimistic; if they stay below that, the bid under refined product prices carries into winter. Either way, treating crude and refined fuel as if they move together misreads this particular episode.

## 4. Why SK hynix is retiring 40 trillion won out of a 69 trillion won cash pile

**Why it matters: **The 40 trillion won equals 58% of the company's net cash. A memory maker that still has to fund the Yongin and Cheongju build-out has chosen to send that money to shareholders first, so capital spending and the return promise now draw on the same wallet.

SK hynix will buy back 40 trillion won of its own shares and cancel every one of them. The board approved the plan on August 19. Based on the previous day's closing price of 1,662,000 won, that is roughly 24.07 million shares, or about 3.3% of shares outstanding. Buying starts on August 20 and runs for about three months, after which the shares are retired.

@@REF@@SK hynix unleashes W40tr buyback amid AI windfall (The Korea Herald, 2026-08-19)|https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10845792|https://wimg.heraldcorp.com/news/cms/2026/08/19/news-p.v1.20260819.7ff4dceaf0ae4cdd95de88e1cba44180_T1.png

## Buying and retiring are not the same

A buyback is a company purchasing its own stock in the market. Shares bought that way can sit in the corporate treasury, and from there they can be resold later, handed to employees as compensation, or swapped for a stake in another company. A buyback on its own therefore does not shrink the share count.

Cancellation erases those shares from the register.

Retired shares never come back, so the number of shares outstanding falls permanently, and the same profit is divided among fewer of them. That is also why Korean policymakers treat the two steps differently. Lawmakers have been debating an amendment to the Commercial Act that would require repurchased shares to be cancelled within a set period, because treasury stock parked indefinitely can protect a controlling shareholder's voting power instead of enlarging what minority holders own. The fact that this announcement is written as a cancellation rather than a purchase is the part that matters.

@@REF@@SK hynix to buy back and cancel $29 billion in shares (Korea JoongAng Daily, 2026-08-19)|https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/sk-hynix-to-buy-back-cancel-40-trillion-won-worth-of-sharesnbsp/12832890|https://image.koreajoongangdaily.com/12814388.jpg

## How much comes out of the cash pile

The reason the board gave is the cash pile. Net cash reached about 69 trillion won at the end of the second quarter, and the board said the current share price does not reflect the company's competitiveness or its ability to generate cash.

@@BAR@@Net cash, end of Q2|69tn won|69|This cancellation|40tn won|40@@The decision hands back 58% of the accumulated cash in one move. The remaining 29 trillion won still has to carry the Yongin and Cheongju build-out.@@SK hynix board resolution, 2026-08-19

One smaller change went almost unremarked. The company had promised to return "up to 50%" of the free cash flow accumulated between 2025 and 2027, and it has now rewritten that as "at least 50%." Two words apart, the two phrases point in opposite directions. The first is a ceiling the company will not exceed; the second is a floor it must clear. Turning a ceiling into a floor removes discretion the company had reserved for itself.

## What SK hynix fills in by November

The test this card set on August 7 was whether the company could name a return figure while holding its expansion schedule. Half of that is now answered and half is not. SK hynix says the size and form of further returns, including a higher fixed dividend and a possible special dividend, will be announced alongside third-quarter results. If the purchases are executed at the announced scale by late November and a dividend increase arrives with them, the claim about an undervalued share price is supported by an execution record. If the amount falls short or the cancellation slips, this resolution reads more like an early claim on money the fabs will need.

## 6. Higher rates don't shrink what tech companies will earn, they shrink what that money is worth today

**Why it matters: **Because rates hit tech through two channels, the second one keeps working even if yields stop rising. Valuation can snap back in a day; borrowing costs reset at each maturity, one refinancing at a time.

The 30-year US Treasury yield has climbed to 5.3%, its highest level since 2007.
On the same day CoreWeave fell 8.3% and Fabrinet fell 10%,
while SK Hynix, SanDisk and Western Digital each dropped more than 5%.

@@REF@@Rising Yields, Oil Interrupt Rally, Hurting Chips (Charles Schwab, 2026-08-18)|https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/stock-market-update-open|https://www.schwab.com/sites/g/files/eyrktu1071/files/329122355_2x1.jpg

None of that means these companies will earn less money this year.
What shrank is the rate used to convert that money into today's value.

## Pushing 10 million won ten years out

The discount rate is the rate at which money you will receive later is marked down to what it is worth now.
Put simply, it is an interest rate turned around and run backwards.

Say you are due 10 million won a year from now.
At a 4% rate you only need to put 9.61 million won on deposit today to end up with 10 million,
so that future money is worth 9.61 million won today.
At 5% you need only 9.52 million won.
The same future sum just lost 90,000 won of present value.

Stretch the wait to ten years and the gap changes shape.
At 4% you need 6.76 million won today; at 5% you need 6.14 million.
The same one percentage point now costs 620,000 won, close to seven times the one-year figure.
Compounding does that: interest earns interest, and ten years of it piles up.

## Where banks and tech part ways

A bank that pays a steady dividend hands you money that behaves like one-year money.
The profits an AI company promises sit several years out.
So the same one percentage point moves the two share prices by very different amounts.

That is the first shock, and it arrives fast.
Nothing about the company's earnings changed. Only the price tag attached to those earnings did.

## When the spread is thin, the Treasury yield passes straight through

The second shock is slower.
Companies borrow at the Treasury yield plus a spread that reflects their own credit risk.
A wide spread absorbs part of a rise in Treasury yields. A thin one lets it through untouched.

That is the author's premise, so we went and looked the numbers up.

@@CHK@@30-year Treasury yield|5.31%|10-year Treasury yield|4.72%|10-year real yield|2.44%|10-year breakeven inflation|2.28%|Investment-grade corporate spread|0.81%|High-yield corporate spread|2.70%@@What moved was the real yield, not breakeven inflation. Money itself got more expensive, and this is not an inflation scare. At 0.81% the investment-grade spread is far too thin to absorb the move in Treasury yields, which supports the author's premise. It is still wider than the 0.53% of October 1997, so it is not a record low.@@2026-08-17 · FRED (DGS30 · DGS10 · DFII10 · T10YIE · BAMLC0A0CM · BAMLH0A0HYM2)

When borrowing costs rise, the businesses hurt first are the ones that spend heavily up front and recover it later through usage fees, which is exactly how a data centre works.
The rent has to rise as much as the funding cost did to break even, and when competition stops that from happening, the difference comes out of profit.

@@REF@@Dow, S&P 500 slip on higher oil prices and Treasury yields (TheStreet, 2026-08-18)|https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-aug-18-2026|https://www.thestreet.com/.image/NDA6MDAwMDAwMDAzMTQ3MTM5/nyse-traders-2.jpg?profile=share16-9

As that report notes, yields were not the only weight on the market that day.
Oil rose too, and the two pushed in the same direction on the arithmetic of data-centre investment.

## What Nvidia can answer on August 26

The rule that higher rates sink tech stocks does not always hold.
Rates were high all through 2026, and tech went to record highs anyway.
A discount rate is only the measuring stick. If earnings grow faster than the stick lengthens, the stick loses.

So the question now is not the direction of yields but the speed of earnings.
Nvidia reports its fiscal 2027 second quarter on August 26, and that number is the speedometer.
Revenue and next-quarter guidance above expectations would be evidence that the sector can carry a heavier stick.
Falling short would undercut the very reason the market has been willing to pay these prices at these yields.

## 7. What Nvidia stood behind is not the eight gigawatts

**Why it matters: **The money in AI data centers is now decided by land and power, not by the chips. When the chipmaker starts guaranteeing somebody else's building and power bill, it means the market is no longer funding those sites on its own terms.

Nvidia has agreed to provide up to $105 billion of credit support for the land, the power and the building shell of a data center campus in Pike County, Ohio.
OpenAI will lease the campus, and only Nvidia chips will run inside it.

@@REF@@Nvidia guarantees SB Energy's PORTS-Pike technology campus in Ohio to exclusively host Nvidia AI compute (Nvidia Newsroom, 2026-08-17)|https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-guarantees-sb-energy-s-ports-pike-technology-campus-in-ohio-to-exclusively-host-nvidia-ai-compute|https://iprsoftwaremedia.com/219/files/202608/091eea2d2fde579be40f8220fdb42526/6a829bf63d633298f4b3f8cd_partner-promo-pack-logo-lockup-openai-sbenergy-5590550_-press-1920x1080-1/partner-promo-pack-logo-lockup-openai-sbenergy-5590550_-press-1920x1080-1_4485e282-071f-4027-a12f-440e901bb193-prv.png?v=4485e282-071f-4027-a12f-440e901bb193

This is not a loan.
SB Energy, a SoftBank company, builds and owns the land, the generation and the buildings.
Nvidia's role is to stand behind that company when it borrows from lenders.

A guarantee lowers the risk the lender carries, and cheaper debt follows.
Think of a parent co-signing a child's mortgage.
The parent does not buy the house; the parent makes the bank lend at a lower rate.

## Between 4.25 and 8

The scope Jensen Huang wrote out himself on LinkedIn is narrow.
The support covers roughly four gigawatts over a 20-year term, and it runs to defined portions of lease and power payments plus a specified residual-value commitment.
He also wrote what it is not: not the full cost of the site, and not all of the tenant's obligations.

Eight gigawatts is the compute OpenAI intends to lease.
What has been backed today is the 4.25 gigawatts of the first phase, and the remaining 3.75 gigawatts sits as an option Nvidia may or may not take.

@@IMG@@datacenter-inside|This is where the $105 billion points. What is guaranteed is not the chips that go into these racks but the building and the electricity that arrives here.

## Why land and power of all things

The campus rises on the decommissioned Portsmouth gaseous diffusion plant, a Department of Energy site in Pike County that once enriched uranium for naval reactors.

The electricity is being built from scratch.
Ten gigawatts of new generation is planned, 9.2 gigawatts of it natural gas, and SB Energy and SoftBank are putting at least $4.2 billion more into regional grid infrastructure through AEP Ohio.

@@IMG@@natgas-flame|Before eight gigawatts of compute there has to be this much fire. Of the ten gigawatts of new generation, 9.2 gigawatts burns natural gas.

The gas plant alone is estimated at around $33 billion, and gas plant construction costs have risen 66 percent in two years.
That is the price of making a place to plug the chips in, before anyone buys a chip.

## From 2028, when Pike County switches on

Huang wrote that the guarantee becomes effective in phases.
As each data center is placed in service between 2028 and 2030 the matching slice of the guarantee comes alive, and as OpenAI makes lease payments Nvidia's remaining exposure declines.

So the weight of this promise is set in 2028, not today.
If OpenAI keeps generating the cash to pay rent, the guarantee stays paper.
If it does not, Nvidia inherits somebody else's building and somebody else's power bill.
Rather than argue about whether the structure is circular financing, watch when Nvidia puts its name on the remaining 3.75 gigawatts.
Filling that slot means it has taken the full eight gigawatts onto its own risk; leaving it empty means 4.25 gigawatts is the limit Nvidia chose.

## 8. Financial conditions are the easiest since 1997, and 2.4 million people walked out of the labour market

**Why it matters: **Easy financial conditions reach the real economy only through asset prices. At the far end of that channel, households are missing debt payments and workers are leaving the labour force. If easing does not warm the real economy, all it leaves behind is concentration in assets.

The US Index of Financial Conditions has climbed to 1.29 points, the easiest reading since 1997.
Borrowing and deploying money has not been this cheap in three decades.
Yet the labour and household numbers released the same week pointed the other way.

@@REF@@US Index of Financial Conditions at 1.29 points, easiest since 1997 (The Kobeissi Letter, 2026-08-14)|https://x.com/KobeissiLetter/status/2088255853052780874

## What "easy" actually means

A financial conditions index bundles interest rates, corporate bond spreads, share prices and the dollar into a single number that answers one question: how hard is it to get money right now.

Think of it as the height of a doorstep across the whole market.
When the step is low, borrowing is easy, and buying something with that borrowed money is easy too.

Two things pushed the index up.
Equities hit all-time highs, and corporate bond spreads stayed near their tightest since 1998.
A spread is the extra yield a weaker borrower must pay above government debt, so a low spread means lenders are barely pricing in the risk of not being repaid.

Participation measures the share of working-age Americans who either hold a job or are looking for one.
People who stop looking drop out of the unemployment count too, so the unemployment rate never shows them.
July saw 264,000 leave, June 720,000, and since last November the total exceeds 2.4 million.

Households are moving the same way.
Credit card delinquencies of 90 days or more stood at 12.9% in the second quarter, the second-highest reading since the first quarter of 2011.
The post-crisis peak was 13.7%, so this is not far from it.
Student loan delinquencies are at 10.6%, the highest since early 2020, and auto loans at 5.5%, the second-highest on record.

## JOLTS, measured by a 30% response rate

One more thing sits underneath all of this.
The surveys that produce those labour numbers are not being answered the way they once were.
Kobeissi reported on 15 August that only about one in four businesses now agree to take part in the Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey, and that roughly 30% file in time for the first estimate.

@@REF@@Federal Statistics: Stakeholders Said Jobs Report Generally Meets Their Needs, but Opportunities Exist to Improve Data Quality (US Government Accountability Office, GAO-26-107538, 2026-06-02)|https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107538|https://www.gao.gov/assets/extracts/3634bdab0eb31cd0d8c37cb3b0d3f3a9/rId25_image6.png

The Government Accountability Office confirmed on 2 June that response rates fell for both the household and the establishment surveys from October 2015 through September 2025.
Its conclusion, though, is more careful than Kobeissi's.
For the household survey it recorded internal assessments finding limited effects on accuracy.
For the establishment survey it recorded that the Bureau of Labor Statistics has not yet released its assessment.
Participation comes from the household survey; job openings come from the establishment survey.
The side without a published assessment is the side markets stared at hardest this week.

So the contradiction runs two layers deep.
Easy money against strained households is one layer.
One of the two instruments measuring those households having no published verification is the second.
If participation slips further from here into the September FOMC and card delinquencies climb with it, easy financial conditions are running on a separate track from the real economy; if either one turns back, this exit reads as a single summer.
