# This week on Stacks (08.09 ~ 08.15)

## 1. Half the copper left in London's warehouses already has an owner waiting

**Why it matters: **Today's copper price is being set by where the metal is parked ahead of a tariff decision rather than by the balance between mines and factories. When location sets the price, a single line of policy moves more than physical supply does.

About half of the copper still sitting in London Metal Exchange warehouses has already been claimed for withdrawal.
Stockpiles have fallen for 42 straight trading days,
and over that stretch anyone who needs metal today has ended up paying far more than someone willing to wait a month.

@@REF@@Copper price holds near record as London warehouse bidding war looms (Mining.com, 2026-08-14)|https://www.mining.com/copper-price-holds-near-record-as-london-warehouse-bidding-war-looms/|https://www.mining.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/AdobeStock_588953684-e1770662316198.jpeg

## Why the date matters more than the price

In commodity markets the later delivery is usually the dearer one.
Storage and financing costs get added on top.

When that order flips and the immediate delivery becomes the expensive one, the market is in backwardation.
Put simply, there is not enough metal in the warehouse, so whoever cannot wait pays a premium to jump the queue.
The binding question stops being what copper costs and becomes when you can actually take hold of it.

A front position trading hundreds of dollars a tonne above the one behind it says a great many buyers cannot afford to be without metal right now.@@Figures as reported by Mining.com, 2026-08-14, accessed 2026-08-15

## The copper did not disappear, it moved

@@FLOW@@Tariff signalled|The market comes to expect a US duty on refined copper|Metal relocates|Producers and traders ship copper into US warehouses before any duty lands|London stocks drain|What is left in London shrinks while withdrawal requests pile up|Front premium|Buyers who need metal now pay far above those content to wait a month@@The copper was not consumed, it crossed the Atlantic. That is why this squeeze will not be undone by mines digging faster, and why it turns first on how the tariff is written.

There is one thing the Kobeissi Letter did not mention.

@@REF@@Indonesia smelter failure extends copper record rally as global stockpiles near crisis (TechTimes, 2026-08-11)|https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323984/20260811/indonesia-smelter-failure-extends-copper-record-rally-global-stockpiles-near-crisis.htm|https://d.techtimes.com/en/full/472271/indonesia-smelter-failure-extends-copper-record-rally-as-global-stockpiles-near-crisis.jpg

On August 8 a boiler water leak shut the Gresik smelter in East Java, Indonesia, for checks and repairs.
The plant makes about 342,000 tonnes of copper cathode a year and takes its ore solely from Grasberg, the second largest copper mine in the world.
It is owned jointly by Freeport Indonesia and Japan's Mitsubishi Materials.

If the pre-tariff shipments pulled existing copper to one side of the ocean, the outage cut the amount coming out of the ground into metal for several weeks.
The original post covers the first force. The second arrived at the same moment and pushed the same way.

## The numbers on the table are 15 percent in 2027 and 30 percent in 2028

What the market is really pricing is not copper supply and demand but a decision in Washington.
A phased duty is being discussed, with talk of 15 percent from 2027 rising to 30 percent from 2028.

If those numbers are confirmed, the copper already moved into the United States turns out to have been bought before the price of entry went up, and emptying the London sheds looks like a gain rather than a loss.
A lower or delayed duty flips it.
The metal parked in US warehouses gains a reason to come back out, and today's premium on immediate delivery goes away quickly.

So the thing to watch here is not the daily copper price but how fast the booked metal actually leaves the warehouses, and when the tariff decision lands.
Which of those two moves first will settle whether today's price was justified.

## 2. The Chinese memory Washington is blocking is not cheap memory

**Why it matters: **When a government tells a buyer to drop a supplier in a market that is already short, the volume moves to Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron. This is a stretch where rules do not cut demand but decide where it goes, which raises the pricing power of whoever is left.

The US Commerce Secretary said he had told Apple directly not to buy Chinese memory.
Howard Lutnick, speaking after touring an Apple manufacturing site in Houston on August 13, said "the Trump administration is not in favor of that", and confirmed he had put that to Apple plainly.

@@REF@@Commerce Secretary says US opposes Apple buying Chinese memory chips (9to5Mac, 2026-08-14)|https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/14/commerce-secretary-says-us-opposes-apple-buying-chinese-memory-chips/|https://i0.wp.com/9to5mac.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2022/10/Working-for-Apple.jpg?resize=1200%2C628&quality=82&strip=all&ssl=1

The Chinese memory in question comes from two firms, CXMT and YMTC.
CXMT makes DRAM and YMTC makes NAND.
Both are designated by the Pentagon as Chinese military companies and already sit under US export restrictions.

@@IMG@@dram-module|A DRAM module of the kind that goes into servers and PCs. This is the sort of part Apple wanted to buy, and switching who makes it takes little redesign, which is where the argument starts.

Apple chief operating officer Sabih Khan has said memory needs "not a lot of customization".
That means it is an easy part to re-source, and that is exactly why politics has room to reach into it.

## Price was never the reason

This is where the usual reading of the story turns over.

CXMT signed a contract with Tencent worth about $3 billion over three to five years.
The piece above reads that deal as a move to secure volume rather than the result of a discount.
A supplier with adequate technology, steady output and negotiable pricing beats a better but supply-constrained one in the competition that actually happens.

## The years when Apple held the whip are over

Serenity adds one line to that.
Apple once held extreme leverage over its component suppliers, the argument runs, and that was before the AI build-out arrived.

As memory capacity has been pulled toward high bandwidth memory for data centres, the volume left for PCs and phones has been pushed into the queue that takes what remains.
In that queue the two Chinese firms were a card Apple could still play, and Washington is now telling it to drop that card.

## What August 21 decides

A date is already fixed to this.
On July 30 a bipartisan group of senators led by Chuck Schumer wrote to Apple demanding it commit to using no CXMT or YMTC memory in any Apple product sold anywhere in the world, with a reply due by August 21.

If Apple gives that commitment on the date, Chinese memory loses one of its largest Western customers and Apple has to find replacement volume in a market where prices are climbing.
If the date passes without one, the matter stays between Washington and Apple, and the share the two Chinese firms hold gets settled by supply rather than by rules.

## 3. What Nvidia's next-next generation is waiting on is not 2nm

**Why it matters: **What sets the schedule for the next accelerator generation is shifting from the process node to back-end capacity. When the bottleneck moves, so does the moment the equipment, material and assembly firms sitting at that step get to book revenue.

Nvidia's Feynman platform, the generation due after Rubin Ultra, is reported to skip TSMC's 2nm node and go straight to A16, the upgraded version of it.
Taiwan's DigiTimes carried the report on August 14,
and in the same piece the bigger movement was not in the process name but in the packaging that wraps the chip.

@@REF@@Nvidia's next-gen Feynman AI platform is ramping up, forcing TSMC to race ahead (Benzinga, 2026-08-14)|https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/08/61201714/nvidias-next-gen-feynman-ai-platform-is-ramping-up-forcing-tsmc-to-race-ahead-report

## One rack, one petabyte a second

Nvidia treats an entire server rack as a single computer.
NVLink is the dedicated path the chips inside it use to hand data to one another.
If that path is narrow, adding more chips buys nothing, because they spend their time waiting on each other.

@@BAR@@Blackwell NVL72|130TB/s|130|Vera Rubin|260TB/s|260|Rubin Ultra|520TB/s|520|Feynman|over 1,040TB/s|1040@@Each generation is exactly double the last. There is a point where copper wiring can no longer carry that width, and switching to light at that point is what CPO is.@@Figures as reported by CloudNews, 2026-08-15, accessed 2026-08-15

@@IMG@@gpu-cluster|Accelerators bundled at rack scale. What grows in Feynman is not only the performance of a single chip but the width of the paths running inside this bundle.

## Packaging binds before the process does

A16 is TSMC's improved node sitting above 2nm.
It offers 8 to 10 percent more speed at the same voltage, or the same speed at 15 to 20 percent less power.

That part is familiar. Getting Feynman out of the door needs what comes after it.
SoIC stacks dies vertically instead of laying them side by side, and the shorter distance between them widens the path.
The catch is that capacity for this step runs on its own schedule, separate from capacity for the chips themselves.

@@TIME@@2026-08-14|DigiTimes reports Feynman on A16 with a larger share of 3D stacking|>2H 2027|Part of AP7 P2, set aside for SoIC, may be turned over to CPO and CoWoS lines|>End of 2027|TSMC targets monthly SoIC capacity of 50,000 wafers|>2H 2028|Feynman expected to launch@@The first three lines decide the last one. If the packaging lines are not standing first, a ready process still ships nothing.@@As reported by DigiTimes 2026-08-14 and CloudNews 2026-08-15

@@REF@@NVIDIA Feynman targets 2028 with A16, 3D chiplets, custom HBM and CPO optical (CloudNews, 2026-08-15)|https://cloudnews.tech/nvidia-feynman-targets-2028-with-a16-3d-chiplets-custom-hbm-and-cpo-optical/|https://cloudnews.tech/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/jensen-nvidia-chips.jpg

That piece puts Feynman's arrival in the second half of 2028 and lays out the same doubling of interconnect width shown above.
Put the two together and it explains why TSMC is hurrying construction at AP7 in Chiayi and AP8 in the Southern Taiwan Science Park.
The bottleneck sits in where things get joined, not in how they are designed.

## Where AP7 P2 ends up

This is the line Jukan flags as interesting.
Some of the AP7 P2 space planned for SoIC may be handed to CPO and CoWoS instead.
If that firms up, sending signals as light enters the real production calendar earlier than the industry had assumed.

Two branches are left.
Hold the 50,000 wafer a month SoIC target through the end of 2027 and Feynman's 2028 schedule largely holds with it, pulling forward when the optical component suppliers get to book revenue.
Let that target slip and the finished part slides even with A16 ready, confirming that this generation's bottleneck was back-end assembly rather than the process node.

The number to watch on this card is TSMC's back-end capacity, not Nvidia's announcements.

## 4. Musk is building a chip fab, and he says he will not buy the light that draws the circuits

**Why it matters: **Lithography is the one bottleneck in the chip supply chain with no substitute. If the light source at the heart of it can be built another way, tool prices, power budgets and the question of which countries can reach the leading edge all move together.

A signal came out of Terafab, the chip plant Musk is building in Texas, that he intends to make the light that draws the circuits himself.
Only one company in the world can make that light today, ASML of the Netherlands.

@@REF@@Musk signals interest in FEL-EUV for Terafab, potentially cutting power versus ASML's current light source (TrendForce, 2026-08-12)|https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/08/12/news-musk-signals-interest-in-fel-euv-for-terafab-potentially-cutting-power-vs-asmls-current-light-source/|https://img.trendforce.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/11135042/Terafab-SpaceX-on-X-20260811-624x293.jpg

It started with a night rendering of Terafab that began circulating on X on August 6.
A large ring runs under the building in the picture, and people asked whether that was a machine for pushing electrons to near the speed of light.
Musk answered with three letters and one more: "FEL FTW".

FEL stands for free electron laser.
It is a machine that accelerates electrons very hard and then pulls light out of them.
FTW is internet shorthand for "this is the way", so putting the two together says the light source is going to be an FEL.

## How is EUV light made

Chip circuits are drawn with light.
Drawing them finer takes light of a shorter wavelength, and what the industry uses now is extreme ultraviolet at 13.5 nanometres, EUV.

@@FLOW@@Tin droplets|fired fifty thousand times a second|High-power laser|hits each droplet and burns it into plasma|Curved mirrors|collect the light and send it to the wafer@@It burns metal to get light, which is why it drinks electricity. What Musk wants to replace is the first two steps.

An FEL has no metal to burn.
Light comes out as long as there is force pushing the electrons, and the force given back by decelerating electrons can be returned to push the next ones.
Meru writes that this is why Musk describes the route in terms of free energy.

## Changing the source changes the power bill

@@BAR@@Today's route (tin plasma)|4.4 megawatts|4.4|Free electron laser (theoretical)|0.7 megawatts|0.7@@This is the input power it takes to get one kilowatt of EUV light out. The right-hand bar is a theoretical figure, and no production lithography tool has yet shown that gap in practice.@@Cited from TrendForce reporting of 2026-08-12, accessed 2026-08-15

Electricity is the resource that runs out first for anyone building AI chips right now.
If the power behind the same light falls that far, the question of how many lithography tools fit inside one plant gets a different answer.

@@REF@@Elon Musk's Terafab enters the EUV lithography race with FEL technology (KuCoin News, 2026-08-11)|https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/elon-musk-s-terafab-enters-euv-lithography-race-with-fel-technology|https://assets.staticimg.com/@kudos/runtime/0.6.6/assets/og-ea52b6f9440e857c.jpg

That piece frames the move as a challenge to ASML's monopoly while noting in the same breath that no one has ever run production lithography off an FEL.

## Ten metres and one kilometre

Size is the price.
An ASML EUV tool is a little over ten metres long, while a machine that accelerates electrons to pull light out of them runs past a kilometre when it is built for research.
Korea has one, the fourth-generation light source accelerator run by the Pohang Accelerator Laboratory, and Meru notes that it too is more than a kilometre end to end.

Meru's reading is that the arithmetic still has a chance.
One FEL can feed light to dozens of lithography tools, and a machine built to make lithography light rather than research light can be far shorter.

## The number 2028

Musk did not raise the idea of making EUV light with an FEL first.
A US startup chaired by former Intel chief executive Pat Gelsinger is developing the same route, with the US government among its stakeholders.
What that company has promised, though, is also one prototype in 2028.
There is no FEL supplier anywhere that can feed light to volume production.

So the thing being decided now is not whether an FEL can make the light.
It already can.
What is being decided is whether that light is even and steady enough to draw circuits on a wafer, and when that gets confirmed.
If one FEL source clears production lithography inside 2028, ASML's monopoly gets a deadline attached to it for the first time; if the route is still a prototype by then, Terafab joins the queue at ASML like everyone else.

## 5. How did the $15 billion Jane Street lost in July ever become public?

**Why it matters: **When the firm quoting the price is stuck with the inventory, spreads in that market widen. A hedge fund blowing up and a market maker getting caught are different things for how much liquidity is left in the next drawdown.

Jane Street lost $15 billion in the month of July, the Financial Times reported.
Reuters carried the same figure the same day, describing the loss as tied to Situational Awareness, the hedge fund that concentrated on AI infrastructure names.
Jane Street had not commented by the time the reports ran.

@@REF@@Exclusive: Jane Street took $15 billion hit in July tied to Situational Awareness, sources say (Reuters, 2026-08-14)|https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusivejane-street-took-15-billion-hit-in-july-tied-to-situational-awareness-sources-say-4861577|https://content-media.investing.com/trkd-images/LYNXMPEM7D1PB_L.jpg

That report puts another number in the same paragraph.
Even after the July setback, the firm has generated more than $40 billion of trading revenue so far this year.

## Why it surfaced now

The firm is private and owes the public no earnings statement.
The Kobeissi Letter picked out how the number got out anyway.

@@TIME@@July 2026|Situational Awareness loses 67% in a single month|Late July 2026|The fund's listed equity book is moved across to Citadel|July 2026|Jane Street is reported to have taken a large loss in the same month|August 14, 2026|The FT and Reuters report the loss@@The number surfaced through a refinancing, not an earnings release. It came out at the moment the firm opened its book to lenders.@@FT and Reuters reports of 2026-08-14 and earlier Stacks cards, accessed 2026-08-14

Jane Street disclosed the July loss to lenders while working on a deal to move an $11 billion pile of public debt across to private investors.
Public debt is a bond anyone can buy and sell in the market; private debt is money held directly by a named handful of investors.
Narrowing the lender group buys room to negotiate terms, and the price of that room is showing those lenders more of the book.
Crypto Briefing puts the transaction at $14.6 billion, led by JPMorgan, with investors including Pimco.
That meeting was the channel the number came through.

## The side that carries the inventory

Jane Street is a market maker.
It quotes a price to buy and a price to sell at any hour and carries the inventory in between so that trades can happen.
Think of a currency kiosk. The business is not calling the exchange rate, it is skimming a sliver on every transaction.

Inventory is where it goes wrong.
You cannot quote without holding the thing, and in a market that has crowded to one side that inventory becomes a directional bet on its own.

@@REF@@Jane Street's $15B loss claim doesn't square with the firm's record-breaking quarter and fresh investment-grade rating (Crypto Briefing, 2026-08-14)|https://cryptobriefing.com/jane-street-15b-loss-claim-debunked/|https://static.cryptobriefing.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/14174708/a-group-of-jane-street-staff-posing-for-a-photo-at-jane-stre-800x420.png

That piece reads the same facts the other way.
It points to $16.1 billion of net trading revenue in the first quarter and argues a July loss on that base does not shake the firm.

@@CMP@@Read as an accident|Fifteen billion dollars in one month. Money crowded into AI names has now caught a firm that makes markets, not just a hedge fund, and the hole was large enough to be shown to lenders.|Read as a results line|The same firm booked $16.1 billion of net trading revenue in the first quarter alone. July was absorbed inside the run of the year.

The same number splits depending on whether the denominator is a month or a year.

## The two numbers Jane Street leaves behind

So was this an accident or a cost.
Taken as July alone it is an accident; taken across the year it is a cost the firm carried.

The place it gets settled is the reporting early next year.
If full-year 2026 trading revenue clears the roughly $40 billion of 2025, July stays a bump in one month; if it falls short, the year goes down as the one in which market making itself got caught by the crowd in AI.

## 6. The old GPUs everyone expected to get cheaper are renting for more, and Wall Street started calling them collateral

**Why it matters: **Once a chip counts as collateral, the money for data centres shifts from corporate cash to the bond market. From that point the pace of the industry is set by the rental curve rather than by chip demand.

The price of renting a GPU launched in 2020 is going up.
For two years the market took it as given that old chips get cheaper every year, and that assumption has now turned over.

On the Silicon Data figures Marc Rubinstein cites, an hour of Nvidia H100 time has gone from $1.96 at the end of November to $2.71 now.

@@REF@@H100 Price Spike: Understanding the 10% Surge in GPU Rental Costs (Silicon Data, 2026-01-09)|https://www.silicondata.com/blog/h100-price-spike

A post from the outfit that compiles those figures shows the climb had already begun early this year.
An hour that cost $2.00 on December 9, 2025 cost $2.20 four weeks later.
At that point A100 and B200 rates held steady, so it looked like a one-generation story.

## At renewal the rate doubles

The gap opens widest at the moment a contract is rewritten.

@@BAR@@What Baseten pays now per B200 hour|$2.63|2.63|The rate after the October renewal|$5.10|5.10@@One renewal lifts the rate by this multiple. What went up in price is not a new chip but a chip already in use.@@Net Interest, 2026-08-14, citing Baseten's disclosure, accessed 2026-08-14

Baseten sells software and computing capacity to companies running lower-cost AI models.
A tenant absorbing an increase of that size is telling you the same machine is hard to find elsewhere.

What actually changed here is not the level but the tenor, the length of time a price is quoted for.
Silicon Data now compiles forward rates for delivery in 2027 and 2028, and that curve slopes upward.
A forward rate is not what something costs today but what two parties agree today to exchange it for later.
It is the equivalent of writing next summer's electricity bill into a contract this morning.
The starting point of the article is that a curve exists, not that a price moved.

## A curve turns a chip into collateral

Once a curve exists, finance follows.
If you can write down what the rent will be for several years, you can lend against that stream.

@@FLOW@@Buy the chip|A GPU is installed in a data centre|Collect an hourly rate|The longer the lease, the more of the cash is fixed in advance|Pledge the stream|The chip and its contract become the security for a loan|Bring in outside capital|Pensions and private credit buy the data centre paper@@What Nvidia announced on August 10 is that last box. The company selling the chips is also assembling the money to buy them.

@@REF@@NVIDIA Partners With Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to Mobilize Over $500 Billion of Third-Party Capital (NVIDIA Newsroom, 2026-08-10)|https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-partners-with-apollo-blackrock-blackstone-brookfield-goldman-sachs-and-kkr-to-establish-ai-compute-infrastructure-financing-platforms-to-mobilize-over-500-billion-of-third-party-capital|https://iprsoftwaremedia.com/219/files/20224/6296531eb3aed3741918b6b5_DH1L4415-HDR-20220527-r5/DH1L4415-HDR-20220527-r5_dba29dde-5a9b-4ac8-bf58-a0b78da89547-prv.jpg?v=dba29dde-5a9b-4ac8-bf58-a0b78da89547

In that release Jensen Huang gives broad adoption, flexibility across models and workloads, and transferability as the reasons his compute suits the role.
Transferable, in finance, means priceable.
If the next tenant will take the machine at the same rate when the first one walks away, the lender can sell it on and get the money back.

Jon Gray of Blackstone reached for houses and planes at the launch.
When you buy a house, he said, the bank underwrites you but it also looks at the value of the home.

## The one who reached for the 1970s was Larry Fink

What this turns on is whether the asset really lasts.
Larry Fink of BlackRock said this is the very beginning, like the mortgage-backed securities market he entered in the 1970s.
Mortgage-backed securities bundle many home loans into bonds and sell them on, and that market did collapse once.

The comparison reads both ways, and that is where the article leaves it.
Keep the rental curve sloping up and the chip settles in as collateral like an aircraft, let the curve bend and it is inventory again.
The one thing confirmable today is that the curve still slopes up.

## 7. Of the 269,000 homes due to break ground around Seoul this year, 65,204 did so in the first half

**Why it matters: **The supply target slid backwards while the borrowing ceiling was pulled forward. Prices are set by the money available now rather than by announced volume, which leaves six months in which the two clauses pull in opposite directions.

The Korean government put out two property packages together on August 13.
One is a fast-track housing supply plan meant to steady the rental and resale markets, the other a comprehensive finance package aimed at the property market.

What sits at the very top of the announcement is not a new target but an old one.
It is a line promising to improve delivery on the 1.35 million housing starts in the Seoul metropolitan area set out in the September 7, 2025 package.

## 9. The 30-year bond just sold at its costliest in 25 years. Why did it disappear the last time rates were here?

**Why it matters: **The government is not the only borrower in the queue that sets the price of long money. When the hyperscalers' purchase commitments turn into cash, two demands meet at the same window, and yields start reacting to a corporate issuance calendar as well as to fiscal news.

The US Treasury sold $25 billion of 30-year bonds on August 13, and the high yield was 5.2%.
That is the highest since 5.5% in August 2001.

@@REF@@U.S. set to pay most for 30-year debt in quarter of a century (Fortune, 2026-08-13)|https://fortune.com/2026/08/13/us-debt-treasury-30-year-highest-25-years/|https://fortune.com/img-assets/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/GettyImages-2287747676-1-e1786633388665.jpg?resize=1200,600

The auction itself cleared without difficulty.
A portfolio manager quoted in that piece noted that a successful auction should not be confused with strong structural demand for long-duration assets.

## Why the bond disappeared last time

The circumstances behind the previous record, in 2001, were the reverse of today's.
The budget was in surplus, the government did not need to borrow that much, and in the autumn of that year the Treasury announced it would stop issuing the 30-year bond altogether.

@@REF@@30-year Treasury bonds to return Feb. 9 (NBC News, 2006)|https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna11133022|https://media-cldnry.s-nbcnews.com/image/upload/v1696280772/newsgroup-logos/nbcnews/social/primary-white-1680x840.png

Selling resumed on February 9, 2006, and that first auction back was $14 billion.
What goes out in a single sale today is far larger.

@@TIME@@2001-08|A 30-year auction sets the previous high in yield|2001-10|The Treasury announces it will stop issuing the bond, citing budget surpluses|2006-02-09|Issuance resumes after more than four years|2026-08-13|The bond sells at the highest yield since that record|>2026-11|The next quarterly refunding announcement@@The same level of yield has now been passed twice. ## The bill at $3 billion a day

Why the long end holds where it does becomes easier to see from the interest bill.

@@CHK@@Interest on the debt, fiscal year to date|$1.17 trillion|Change from a year earlier|+15%|Treasury debt outstanding|$31 trillion@@Maturing paper keeps rolling over at higher rates. Put simply, the balance on the card has not merely grown. The rate on it went up while the balance kept revolving.
The Treasury has leaned issuance toward shorter maturities to defer that burden, which brings each rollover around more often.

## What El-Erian posted that same morning

Hours before he relayed the auction result, El-Erian wrote that there is a second queue.
Citing the FT's Alphaville column, he reported that the hyperscalers' purchase commitments have reached $1.5 trillion, and said these sit on top of large government financing needs.
A purchase commitment is money already contracted to buy chips, compute, and the power and equipment that run a data centre.

Not all of that reaches the bond market.
But a commitment has to turn into cash eventually, and one route to that cash is corporate issuance.
When a government and the technology companies look for long money at the same window, the borrower is not the side that sets the price.

The thing that decides this is the November refunding.
If the Treasury lifts the share of long-dated issuance, today's yield is confirmed as a price made by supply, and if it keeps leaning on bills, the price of long money will swing more on the issuance calendar of corporations.

## 12. One percent of ETF assets makes 16% of the daily trading value

**Why it matters: **America's rare earth independence rests less on mines and plants than on a floor price and loan terms written by the Pentagon. If that support wavers, or dysprosium supply slips, the whole schedule moves back.

MP Materials swung to a profit in the second quarter of 2026 not because magnets sold well, but because the U.S. Department of Defense paid it cash for the gap between the market price and a floor set in advance.

@@REF@@MP Materials reports second quarter 2026 results: 1,006 tonnes of NdPr sold, adjusted EBITDA turns positive (Investing News, 2026-08-11)|https://investingnews.com/mp-materials-reports-second-quarter-2026-results/

A year earlier the same quarter showed a loss of $12.5 million.
One of the lines that made the difference was $17.6 million of price protection income.

## The floor at $110

The Pentagon has put a floor of $110 per kilogram, running to 2035, under NdPr, the neodymium-praseodymium feedstock that MP lives on.
When the market price falls below that line, the difference arrives in cash each quarter.

Think of it as a guaranteed purchase price for a crop.
Even if the market collapses, what you harvest still fetches the agreed number.

Why the device was needed sits in how China has cleared out Western rare earth producers before.
It pushed prices below the cost of digging the ore, waited for rivals to close, and took the space they left.
A floor makes that method stop working.

At the roundtable, chief executive Jim Litinsky said the magnet was mined and refined in California and built in Texas.
The heavy rare earth dysprosium, which keeps a magnet from losing its pull as it heats up, is understood to still come from China.
The hotter the job, an EV traction motor or defense hardware, the worse its absence hurts.

Japan hit this wall first.
In the 2010 Senkaku dispute it had no answer, but when shipments were cut in January 2026 it already had production lines for magnets that use no dysprosium or terbium, and a plant in Vietnam.

## Making the magnet without the rare earth

The Niron Magnetics announcement Trump made the same day is a different kind of bet.
Niron builds magnets from iron nitride, and both elements are everywhere.

You sell a building you own, pocket the cash, and rent the same building back on a long lease.
Swap the store for a data center and the tenant for an AI buyer such as OpenAI, and only the cast changes.

What the lender actually holds is not the building. It is the tenant's ability to keep paying every month.

## The take-or-pay contract has holes in it

The tool used to close that risk is a take-or-pay contract.
It says you pay for the agreed amount whether or not you actually use it.
Revenue holds up even if demand softens, so credit committees treat such a contract almost as collateral.

But in both the United States and South Korea, an administrator in a court restructuring can reject contracts that have not yet been performed.

The life of the contract was set by the tenant's condition, not by the term written into it.
Bankruptcy is not even required for this to bite.
Once a tenant's credit wobbles, renegotiation on softer terms usually arrives before any default.

## 19. The Bank of Korea bought gold after thirteen years, without spending a single dollar

**Why it matters: **Central bank buying is the least price-sensitive slice of gold demand. Whether that slice is growing or shrinking sets the floor under the price right now.

The Bank of Korea started buying gold again on August 3, 2026.
It had not added a single gram since February 2013.

@@REF@@Bank of Korea resumes buying gold after a 13-year pause (Korea JoongAng Daily, 2026-08-03)|https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/bank-of-korea-resumes-buying-gold-after-13year-pausenbsp/12806039|https://image.koreajoongangdaily.com/12806158.jpg?imageId=12806158&x=0&y=0&cropw=100&croph=100&panox=0&panoy=0&panow=100&panoh=100&width=1200&height=683

## What stopped the buying was not the market

The bank began in July 2011, taking 25 tonnes at $1,544 an ounce, and filled out 90 tonnes over five purchases through February 2013.

## A different wallet this time

Korea exports gold.
Smelters such as LS MnM and Korea Zinc recover 40 to 45 tonnes a year as a by-product of processing copper and zinc.
The slice that used to leave the country as exports is what the bank is now buying.

Buying domestic metal in won means no dollars have to come out of the reserve pool.
It is like paying for something made in your own house with money already in the house: nothing leaves.
Storage moves home as well.

Gold parked in London is not risk-free either.
The Bank of England lends out part of the metal it safekeeps to global investment banks and earns a fee.
The Bank of Korea has used its share of that fee to cover storage costs.
If the lent metal does not come back on time when it is called, there is a hole.

Whether domestically refined bars count as reserve assets is already settled.
LS MnM sits on the London Bullion Market Association's Good Delivery list.
The metal clears the international standard, so moving storage onshore does not disturb how it is counted.

## Where 1.1 percent and 3.2 percent part ways

The original post puts gold at 1.1 percent of Korea's reserves.

The gap appears to come from whether the metal is carried at what it cost or at what it is worth today.
Either way the number sits far below the average for central banks.
That is the reason to check the basis before reading a headline share.

## What the World Gold Council counted in the first half of 2026

The original post says China raised the size of its purchases for five straight months from March 2026.
Bloomberg counts a buying streak of 21 months, with 640,000 ounces added in July, the largest monthly purchase since October 2023.
The two figures do not clash.
One counts months in which the purchase grew, the other counts months in which buying never stopped.

@@REF@@China's central bank adds 20 tons to gold reserves in July (Bloomberg, 2026-08-07)|https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/china-s-central-bank-adds-20-tons-to-gold-reserves-in-july

The same body's full-year 2026 range is 700 to 900 tonnes.
The 900-plus figure in the original post is the top end of that range.

So Korea breaking a thirteen-year pause and global central bank buying shrinking in the first half now sit side by side.
That is where the least price-sensitive slice of gold demand is decided.
Net buying above 555 tonnes in the second half reaches 900 for the year and the whale story holds; anything below that leaves 2026 as a smaller year than 2025.

## Bandwidth grew, latency did not

To compute anything a GPU launches a small unit of work called a kernel, tears it down when it finishes, and launches the next one.
Each launch and teardown takes a very short time.

The trouble is that once the time to produce a single piece of the answer drops below a millisecond, that very short time becomes a large share of the whole.

## Launch the kernel only once

What TileRT does is compile the whole answer-generating path ahead of time and freeze it into a single kernel.
With the launching and tearing down gone, computation, memory reads and communication overlap each other.

On a single B200 server it produced 500 tokens a second, which SemiAnalysis reports is about three times faster than a much larger GB300 NVL72 running conventional engines.

The software comes from the team behind the TileLang language and sits on GitHub under an MIT license.

The figure Bloomberg reported first covers Nippon Life, Dai-ichi Life, Sumitomo Life and Meiji Yasuda together, and all four saw their paper losses grow.
Nippon Life alone accounts for 6.28 trillion yen.

An unrealized loss is the gap between the book value of a bond you have not sold and what it would fetch today.
When rates rise, bonds bought at lower rates are worth less, so a loss shows up in the numbers even though nothing has been sold.

## Why the ultralong bonds belong to insurers

A life insurer is a company that has promised to pay out decades from now.
To line its assets up with that promise it has to hold assets with very long maturities, and in Japan the 30-year government bond fills that slot.
The 30-year was first issued in 1999, and life insurers are close to the only natural buyer for it.

That 30-year yield rose above 4% on May 15 this year, the first time since the bond was created.
Worries that Prime Minister Takaichi's cabinet may widen fiscal spending were named as the reason.
At the August 6 auction it priced at 3.9% on an average basis.

@@TIME@@2026-05-15|The 30-year JGB yield tops 4% for the first time since 1999|2026-07-31|Japan and the US buy yen together for the first time in 15 years|2026-08-06|The 30-year auction prices at an average 3.9%|2026-08-07|The four insurers disclose 15.13 trillion yen of unrealized bond losses|>2026-09-18|Bank of Japan policy meeting@@Three months after rates touched 4%, the paper losses have not shrunk, and a BOJ meeting arrives before the next quarterly figure does.@@Retrieved 2026-08-10 · Ministry of Finance auction result (2026-08-06) · Bank of Japan meeting calendar

## The yardstick changed at the end of March

Since the end of March this year, Japanese insurers' solvency has been measured on a new standard.
It marks not only assets to market but also liabilities, meaning the obligation to pay policyholders.

That is where the direction flips.
A life insurer's liabilities run longer than its assets, so when rates rise the present value of the liabilities falls by more than the value of the assets.
Capital is assets minus liabilities, so on this yardstick a rise in rates pushes capital up.

@@REF@@Japan's insurers clear the bar under new economic solvency rules (Insurance Business Asia, 2026-07-28)|https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/breaking-news/japans-insurers-clear-the-bar-under-new-economic-solvency-rules-583838.aspx|https://cdn-res.keymedia.com/cms/images/us/024/0381_639207652516035815.png

@@CMP@@Reading the paper losses|15.13 trillion yen grows further if rates keep rising. A wave of cancellations would force sales at a loss.|Reading the new yardstick|Liabilities run longer than assets. Higher rates cut liability values more, so the ratio improves.

The same rise in rates lands on opposite conclusions depending on whether you look only at the bond side of the book or at the liabilities alongside it.

The bridge between the two readings is cancellations.
That is what the original post was worried about. If policyholders surrender in bulk, bonds meant to be held to maturity have to be sold at a loss, and at that moment a paper loss becomes a real one.

The numbers on hand do not point that way yet.
The Life Insurance Association of Japan puts the lapse and surrender ratio for individual policies at 5.6% in fiscal 2024, down 0.3 points from the year before.
Figures covering the first half of this year, when yields jumped hardest, are not out.

So whether Japan's life insurers are weaker or sturdier is settled by the surrender rate rather than by bond prices.
If surrenders stay near 5%, the losses stay a number on a page; if that rate starts climbing in earnest, the same rise in rates works against what the solvency ratio is saying.
The end-September figures land in November.

The first award sent out 45.2 million barrels and secured 55 million barrels in return. The difference was about 9.8 million barrels, or 21.7%. A May award exchanged 53.3 million barrels for an additional 15.1 million, implying about 68.4 million barrels back and a 28% premium.

## Contract assets are not oil in a cavern

Doomberg's accounting is sound, but it does not mean the short-term buffer is unchanged. EIA's weekly data put SPR stocks at 311.4 million barrels on July 17, about 104 million below the February level. A return claim cannot be delivered to a refinery today, so physical inventory matters if another shock arrives before repayment.

The opposite risk also matters. If oil remains scarce or becomes more expensive next year, companies must buy costly barrels to return principal and the premium. Concentrated repayments can withdraw supply from the market, while any DOE delay would postpone the reserve's rebuild. The cost has not vanished; it has moved to private companies and a future date.

## The real test is the 200-million-barrel return

The question is whether roughly 200 million barrels actually return by March 2027. Returns of at least 200 million would validate Doomberg's crude-interest framing in measurable terms, while less than 172 million would fail to replace even the principal. Investors should hold both facts at once: lower inventory today and a larger contractual return tomorrow.

## 34. Lumber's four-year high came from shrinking supply, not a housing boom

**Why it matters: **Builder costs can rise when lumber supply falls faster than home sales. Even lower tariffs may not remove price pressure if closed mills do not return. That is why demand data alone cannot explain margins in builders or lumber producers.

A four-year high in lumber would normally suggest that more homes are being sold. This time the opposite is true. US new single-family home sales were lower than a year earlier in June, while framing-lumber prices rose.

## Housing demand and lumber prices split apart

The industry framing-lumber composite reached $535 per thousand board feet in July. That was the highest since September 2022 and 23% above a year earlier. The US Census Bureau, meanwhile, reported June new-home sales at a 628,000 annual rate, down 5.6% from a year earlier.

@@CHK@@Framing-lumber composite|$535 per thousand board feet·up 23% year over year|June new-home sales|628,000 annual rate·down 5.6% year over year|Current trade-remedy duties for most suppliers|35.2%|Separate product tariff|10%@@This is not a scene in which stronger home sales pulled lumber higher. When demand is weak and price still rises, supply deserves the first look.

@@REF@@The framing-lumber composite reached $535, its highest since September 2022 (Wall Street Journal, 2026-08-05)|https://www.wsj.com/finance/commodities-futures/home-building-is-sputtering-but-lumber-prices-havent-been-so-high-in-years-a2ef1cf2

@@REF@@June US new-home sales ran at 628,000, down 5.6% from a year earlier (US Census Bureau, 2026-07-24)|https://www.census.gov/construction/nrs/current/index.html

## Tariffs are not the whole explanation

Hanke points to tariffs on Canadian lumber. Most Canadian suppliers currently face 35.2% in antidumping and countervailing duties, with a separate 10% Section 232 tariff on softwood lumber since October 2025. A simple addition puts the border burden at 45.2%.

Tariffs are not the whole explanation. West Fraser permanently closed four US and Canadian sawmills in late 2025, removing 300 million board feet of annual lumber capacity. Its reasons included weak demand, timber-supply problems, and elevated duties. Once low prices close a mill, supply does not instantly return just because the market tightens.

@@FLOW@@Weak lumber demand|Low prices and mill losses|Closures and curtailments|Less operable supply|Tariffs on Canadian lumber|Higher import costs and lower volumes|Price rises despite weak demand|Different from a housing boom@@Tariffs reduce imports while closed mills reduce domestic and foreign capacity. Both paths meet in the same price.

@@REF@@West Fraser permanently closed four sawmills, removing 300 million board feet of annual capacity (West Fraser, 2025-11-06)|https://www.westfraser.com/investors/news/news-releases/west-fraser-reduces-lumber-capacity

## A 45% wall may become a 35% wall

Preliminary results from the seventh US review would lower the standard antidumping and countervailing rate from 35.2% to 24.8%. The result is not yet effective and can change in the August final decision. The separate 10% Section 232 tariff remains, so the preliminary figures would still imply a roughly 34.8% total burden.

@@REF@@The seventh-review preliminary rate is 24.8% for most suppliers but is not effective before the final decision (Government of British Columbia, 2026-04-09)|https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/competitive-forest-industry/softwood-lumber-trade-with-the-u-s

@@REF@@The US imposed a 10% Section 232 tariff on softwood lumber imports (White House, 2025-09-29)|https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/09/adjusting-imports-of-timber-lumber-and-their-derivative-products-into-the-united-states/|https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/WH47-Presidential-Actions-Social-Share-Card.jpg

## Investors should watch supply return, not only home sales

For builders, weak home sales combined with rising material costs are uncomfortable. It is hard to raise selling prices while lumber squeezes margins. For lumber producers, higher prices help, but closed mills and expensive timber can prevent a quick volume response.

If the final August tariff falls as indicated but the all-in burden remains around 34%, a large supply barrier survives. If it drops below 30% and imports recover, Hanke's tariff explanation weakens. The next move in lumber depends less on a housing rebound than on Canadian volume and how quickly shuttered mills can return.

## 37. Copper jumped on Congo's ban, but the target is concentrate, not refined metal

**Why it matters: **Export controls by the country supplying most of the world's cobalt and a large share of copper can move prices. Separating concentrate from refined metal reveals how much physical supply is actually at risk.

The government of the Democratic Republic of the Congo immediately banned exports of copper and cobalt concentrate in an order dated June 29. Concentrate is an intermediate product in which ore has been upgraded but not yet smelted into metal. The government wants that intermediate processed at home so more value stays in the country.

After Reuters reported the order, three-month copper on the London Metal Exchange rose as much as 1.8% to $14,369.50 a ton. The order nevertheless allows one-year export waivers in strategic circumstances.

@@REF@@Congo bans copper and cobalt concentrate exports while allowing one-year waivers in strategic circumstances (Reuters, 2026-08-06)|https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/congo-bans-exports-copper-cobalt-concentrates-official-order-says-2026-08-06/

## What the price read and what was actually blocked

Concentrate and refined copper are different products even though they contain the same metal. In the first quarter of 2026, Congo exported 696,725 tons of refined copper cathodes. It exported 53,926 tons of concentrate containing 18,863 tons of copper metal.

@@BAR@@Refined copper cathode exports|696,725 t|696725|Copper contained in exported concentrate|18,863 t|18863@@The copper volume directly targeted by the ban is far smaller than the headline may suggest. Both figures cover the first quarter of 2026.@@Reuters (2026-08-06), citing official Congolese export data

The fact that most material is already refined locally limits the immediate hit. It also concentrates the burden on projects such as Kamoa-Kakula that had export exemptions. Congo introduced similar bans in 2013, 2019, and 2023, granting waivers when domestic smelting capacity was insufficient. The latest order repeals the 2023 order and its exemptions and replaces them with a wider framework.

## More smelters change the policy's weight

There is one important difference from earlier rounds. Ivanhoe Mines built a 500,000-ton-a-year on-site smelter at Kamoa-Kakula and produced its first copper anodes in December 2025. The company said it expected to reduce on-site copper-in-concentrate inventory from 37,000 tons to 17,000 tons during 2026. That is evidence that domestic processing capacity is genuinely growing.

@@REF@@Kamoa-Kakula produces its first copper anodes at a 500,000-ton-a-year on-site smelter (Ivanhoe Mines, 2026-01-02)|https://www.ivanhoemines.com/news-stories/news-release/ivanhoe-mines-announces-first-anode-production-from-kamoa-kakula-copper-smelter/|https://www.ivanhoemines.com/wp-content/themes/ivanhoemines/img/OG.jpg

One project's smelter cannot process every concentrate stream in the country. The ban's staying power is a race between new processing capacity and the pace at which the government grants waivers.

## Cobalt has a more direct valve

Congo's leverage is greater in cobalt. The US Geological Survey estimates that in 2024 the country produced 75% of the world's cobalt, 13% of mined copper, and 9% of refined copper.

@@REF@@Congo's shares of world cobalt, mined-copper, and refined-copper production (US Geological Survey, 2024 data)|https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center/congo-kinshasa|https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/s3fs-public/styles/teaser/public/thumbnails/image/augelite%20and%20quartz.JPG?itok=H4dnIqg8

Cobalt also has a separate export quota already in force. The International Energy Agency's policy record puts the 2026 ceiling at 96,600 tons: an 87,000-ton base quota plus a 9,600-ton strategic quota that can be adjusted with market conditions.

@@REF@@Congo's 2026 cobalt export quota is 96,600 tons (International Energy Agency, updated 2026-05-28)|https://www.iea.org/policies/29138-drc-arecoms-decision-no-0042025-cobalt-quota-system

Copper's first move was quick to price the word ban. The policy's real weight will show up in quarterly concentrate exports, refined-copper exports, cobalt shipments, and waiver approvals. If those four numbers do not fall, much of the rally will have priced caution before a physical supply loss.
