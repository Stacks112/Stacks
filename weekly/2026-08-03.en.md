## 1. Why did China build a 2008 model when it already held ASML's 7nm scanner?

**Why it matters: **A lithography scanner is the one seat in the chip supply chain with no substitute. If China fills that seat on its own, the leverage behind US export controls disappears, and if it cannot, the controls keep working. That this tool sits at 2008 level says the leverage is still there.

The immersion scanner China says it has built sits at the same level as the NXT:1950i, a tool ASML released in 2008.
It prints 28nm circuits in a single exposure, and the generation above it, the 2015 model 1980Di, is still out of reach.

When a state-backed firm in Shanghai was reported on July 27 to have started volume production of the tool, ASML shares closed down 5.7% and fell as much as 7% intraday.

@@REF@@China begins mass production of homegrown immersion DUV machines, first units to SMIC, Hua Hong and CXMT this year (Tom's Hardware, 2026-07-27)|https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/china-begins-mass-production-of-domestic-immersion-duv-lithography-machines|https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/7ZotSVaMnrDA7FNSEBa4BN-970-80.jpg

Tom's Hardware reports the tool prints 28nm-class features in one exposure and reaches 7nm-class when exposures are layered on top of each other.
Deliveries run to about five units this year and roughly 20 next year.

## Why a 2008 model of all things

A scanner is the machine that writes circuits onto a wafer with light.
That single step eats 60% of the time and 35% of the cost of making a chip.

Start with the light itself. The domestic source built by Keyihongyuan runs at 6kHz and 60W.
ASML's part delivers 90W at the same frequency.

For the optics, Maolai is said to have passed validation on a 300mm large-aperture lens.
Its precision lands around 30nm, against under 10nm for Germany's ZEISS lenses, so ZEISS lenses were reportedly imported in the end.

@@REF@@China's DUV lithography still lags ASML by four generations (Asia Times, 2026-07-31)|https://asiatimes.com/2026/07/chinas-duv-lithography-still-lags-asml-by-four-generations/|https://asiatimes.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/SSA800.jpg

Asia Times names the tool the SSA800 and reports it was built by Shanghai Aishengna Electronic Technology Group, founded in 2023, which absorbed development teams from Yuliangsheng and SMEE.
Roughly 70% of the parts are domestic, while mirrors for the 193nm excimer laser, ZEISS lenses, vacuum chambers and synchronization control algorithms are still imported.
Turn that 70% around and it says 30% has to be bought from outside before the machine can be built at all.

## Why the 1980Di could not be copied

Huawei already held the generation above.
HSMC in Wuhan brought in an ASML 1980Di for a 7nm fab and then went bankrupt, and the Chinese government took over the company and handed it to Huawei.

In October 2025, China asked ASML to repair that machine.
The engineer who went on site reportedly found it taken apart and never put back together.

Calibration is what stands in the way.
It is a precision setting that aligns the lenses, mirrors and wafer stage down to the nanometer, fixed at the factory over weeks against hundreds of reference points.
Opening the machine throws that alignment off, and the software key that restores it sits only with ASML.

Even holding the finished product, you lose it the moment you look inside.

## What ASML actually stands to lose

China moved fast because of the calendar.
EUV was blocked from 2019, the NXT:2000i and above from late 2023, and the 1970i and 1980i from September 2024.
Ninety units of the 1980i were stockpiled just before the rules landed, but servicing that lifts performance by more than 1% was banned too, which made upkeep hard.

## So what settles it

The question comes down to one thing.
Whether this is the first crack in ASML's moat, or a detour that export controls forced open.

The generation is what settles it.
While the tool stays at 1950i class, it is a detour.
The moment a 1980Di-class machine comes out of domestic parts alone, it is a crack.

ASML took seven years to move from 28nm to 7nm.
There is no visible reason the side walking that road again on a domestic light source and domestic lenses gets less time than that.
A scanner is the one seat in the chip supply chain with no substitute, and whoever holds it is what gives export controls their leverage.
That this tool sits at 2008 level says the leverage is still there.

## 2. The US bought yen for the first time since 2011, and it sold euros rather than dollars to do it

**Why it matters: **Interventions are usually read by how much was spent, but the point here is where the money came from. Japan's dollar wallet is more than a trillion dollars of US Treasuries, and selling them lifts the long rate Washington cares about most. If a route to raise dollars without selling those bonds is genuinely open, the limit on defending a currency stops being Japan's reserves and becomes Washington's consent.

On July 31 the Federal Reserve Bank of New York sold euros and bought yen on behalf of the US Treasury.
It is the first time since 2011 that Washington has stepped into the currency market on the buying side of the yen.

@@REF@@Bessent joins Japan to help reverse months of yen losses (Fortune / Bloomberg, 2026-08-01)|https://fortune.com/2026/08/01/scott-bessent-treasury-japan-yen-dollar-exchange-rate/|https://fortune.com/img-assets/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/GettyImages-2286452776-e1785596222971.jpg?resize=1200,600

Bloomberg reports that Japan spent 8.45 trillion yen, about $52.8 billion, on July 30 alone.
That is likely the largest amount Tokyo has ever spent in a single day.
The yen closed Friday at 157.40 to the dollar, its strongest since early May.

## Why did one notepad become the story

It started with a sheet of paper.
A Reuters photograph taken at a cabinet meeting on July 31 caught the notepad of Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.
Under the words 'To Do' it read 'Buy Japanese Yen (JPY) $5-10 bil.'

@@REF@@Exclusive: Bessent's 'to do' list, buy $5-10 billion worth of Japanese yen (Reuters, 2026-07-31)|https://finance.yahoo.com/news/exclusive-bessents-list-buy-5-225415289.html|https://s.yimg.com/lo/mysterio/api/C8F5ECDB88AEC917D1C3C05F2C99E95565B7C15A7FBC36B6029FEE539C424998/subgraphmysterio/resizefill_w1200_h800;quality_80;format_webp/https:%2F%2Fmedia.zenfs.com%2Fen%2Freuters.com%2F5c009aaee3c711409ee15d0354c70e83

Reuters reports that the Treasury had alerted banks to a possible intervention that morning.
The Treasury did not comment on the contents of the memo.

## Why do US Treasuries come up when you buy yen

To push the yen up you have to sell dollars and buy yen.
That means you need the dollars first, and where those dollars come from is the whole question here.

The money Japan spends on the exchange rate comes out of a special account run by its Finance Ministry.
The account holds $1.3747 trillion, and $1.0087 trillion of it, most of what can be turned into cash quickly, sits in US Treasuries.

Put simply, Japan's dollar wallet is stuffed with US government debt.
Opening the wallet means selling those bonds, and the moment they are sold there are more of them around, so prices fall and yields rise.
The one number Bessent has said he watches most closely is the 10-year Treasury yield.

## So where did the dollars come from this time

What the US sold this time was not dollars but euros.
The Treasury keeps a separate pocket called the Exchange Stabilization Fund, and euros and yen sit inside it.
Selling euros to buy yen leaves US Treasuries untouched.

What Japan will use has not been confirmed.
The original piece expects Japan to use a window called FIMA repo.
Created in March 2020, it lets a foreign central bank pledge the US Treasuries it already keeps in custody at the New York Fed, borrow dollars for a short period and buy the bonds back later.

@@REF@@Federal Reserve announces the establishment of a standing FIMA repo facility (Federal Reserve, 2021-07-28)|https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20210728b.htm|https://www.federalreserve.gov/images/social-media/social-default-image-opengraph.jpg

The Fed's announcement fixes the collateral as Treasuries held in custody at the New York Fed and the limit at $60 billion per counterparty.
Selling a bond and pledging it are very different things in the market.
The seller puts paper into the market. The borrower does not.

@@CMP@@The common reading|Washington has taken the same side for the first time. What the US put in looks more like a signal than money.

The same fact reads differently depending on whether you price the signal or the money.

## So what will tell us which it was

In the end the question is one.
Whether a route has really opened that defends a currency without selling US Treasuries, or whether this was a signal that lasts a few days.

The place to look is the balance sheet the Fed publishes every Thursday.
If the dollars borrowed by foreign monetary authorities against Treasuries move off zero, Japan has opened that route.
If that number stays at zero while the yen slides again, Japan either went back to selling bonds or gave up on intervening.

Two governments can agree to move an exchange rate for a few days.
What they cannot settle by agreement is where the dollars behind it come from.
The record this intervention leaves will not be how far the yen went, but whether there was a way to hold the line without selling the bonds.

## 3. Trump says Iran asked him to call off the strike. So why does Tehran say it never did?

**Why it matters: **The war premium in oil is set by whether ships move through Hormuz, not by whether a strike happens. Trump cancelled the attack and put a complete opening of the strait in the deal, but Iran, which controls permission to transit, says there was no request and no deal. The price fell 5% before the agreement was confirmed to exist, and whatever is unconfirmed can come back.

President Trump has called off the planned US attack on Iran.
His stated reason was that Iran and other Middle Eastern countries had asked him to hold off, and that the perimeters of a deal had been agreed.
That deal, he said, would include the immediate and complete opening of the Strait of Hormuz.

Oil fell about 5% within a day of the post.
West Texas Intermediate traded at $79.77 a barrel and Brent at $83.47.

@@REF@@Oil Prices Plunge 5% as Trump Halts Iran Strike Plans (OilPrice.com, 2026-08-02)|https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Oil-Prices-Plunge-5-as-Trump-Halts-Iran-Strike-Plans.html|https://d32r1sh890xpii.cloudfront.net/news/1200x675/2026-08-02_8klqup39sh.jpg

The same report notes that the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency logged three more tanker attacks over the weekend.
Brent was under $72 at the start of July and closed above $90 last week, so this move gave back part of that run rather than all of it.

## Why one strait is this big

The Strait of Hormuz is the way out of the Persian Gulf into the Indian Ocean.
Crude loaded in Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait and the UAE leaves through that single passage.

Put simply, the strait behaves like a valve, not a dial.
It does not taper off. It either flows or it stops.
So a single sentence about who may pass moves the price 5% in a day.

## What exactly did Iran deny

Tehran tells it differently.
Iran's Mehr news agency said it never asked the US to hold off, and Fars said reports of an agreement to split control of the strait were false.

The official quoted by Fars attached conditions.
As long as the US continues what Iran calls hostile actions, the strait stays closed, and a ship may pass only along a route Iran designates and with permission from the Revolutionary Guards navy.

@@REF@@Iran denies it asked Trump not to strike or agreed to deal to split control of Hormuz (The Times of Israel, 2026-08-02)|https://www.timesofisrael.com/iran-denies-it-asked-trump-not-to-strike-or-agreed-to-deal-to-split-control-of-hormuz/|https://static-cdn.toi-media.com/www/uploads/2026/08/2026-08-02T091335Z_1478686883_MT1ANADL0007YG4ZC_RTRMADP_3_ANADOLU.jpg

The same report describes a compromise floated by Israel's Channel 12.
Ships would enter through Iranian-controlled water and leave through Omani water, and transit fees were reportedly left out of the outline.
Oman is said to have asked for assurances that the Guards would honour it.

Both sides are describing the same two days, and they disagree on whether an agreement exists at all. What splits them is who sets the terms of passage.

## So what settles this

The question comes down to one thing.
Is this the start of the war premium unwinding, or a pause before it goes back on.

The place to look is the ships.
If tankers begin transiting without clearing it with the Revolutionary Guards first, the deal is working. If passage stays permission-based, the announcement was words.

Oil has already answered half of it. It gave up 5% and did not return to where it sat at the start of July.
The attack being cancelled is a confirmed fact. The strait being open is not one yet.
The market has priced itself somewhere in between.

## 4. Q2 earnings are beating estimates by a record 31%. Take out two companies and it is 9%.

**Why it matters: **This surprise record reflects what an AI company was priced at, not how much AI earned. The gains Alphabet and Amazon booked came from the carrying value of stakes in private AI firms like Anthropic, and that value is set by the next funding round. Reading the season through the index average alone makes it impossible to tell AI demand arriving as revenue apart from AI assets being repriced.

US companies are beating Q2 estimates by an unusual margin.
S&P 500 earnings have come in 31.4% above analyst estimates in aggregate, the widest gap since FactSet began tracking the figure in 2008.

The next passage of the same report carries a caveat.
Strip out Alphabet and Amazon and the number falls to 9.2%.

## What happened at those two companies

Most of what those two booked was not money earned by selling anything.
It was the carrying value of stakes they already held moving up.

When an asset a company has not sold rises in value, accounting rules require that increase to be recorded as income in that quarter. That is an unrealised gain.
No cash arrives, and the net income line grows anyway.

A painting on your wall getting more valuable does not raise your salary that year. Corporate accounting still writes the increase into the profit line.

Amazon added $53.4 billion pre-tax to income this way from its Anthropic stake.
Operating income in the same quarter was $27.5 billion, so the mark-up on the stake was close to twice what the business itself earned.

@@REF@@Amazon's $200B Quarter: Ads, AWS, and the Anthropic Gain (Digital Applied, 2026-07-31)|https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/amazon-q2-2026-earnings-ads-aws-anthropic-gain|https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/amazon-q2-2026-earnings-ads-aws-anthropic-gain/article-image

Alphabet is the same shape.
As covered in an earlier card, Alphabet's pre-tax unrealised gain was $99 billion, from its SpaceX and Anthropic holdings.
Anthropic is the name that appears in both.

## So is the unprecedented AI earnings claim right

Half right.
Companies that converted AI demand into actual revenue, chipmakers and cloud operators, did post strong quarters again.

But that is not what produced the index-level surprise record.
What produced it was the book value of two companies' holdings in an AI startup.

@@CMP@@How Kobeissi reads it|AI-exposed companies are delivering unprecedented earnings, and a 27% aggregate beat is the evidence.|How the FactSet data reads|Most of the record is non-operating gains at Alphabet and Amazon. Take those out and it is 9.2%, high but not unprecedented.

Neither reading disputes that earnings are good. They split on whether the profit was earned by selling products or recorded because an AI company got more expensive on paper.

## So what settles this

The question comes down to one thing.
Is this record the size of the profit AI created, or the price someone put on an AI company.

The place to look is next quarter.
If Anthropic is not marked up again, this quarter's gain does not repeat, and if the valuation falls the same rule cuts net income in the other direction.
If the 9.2% ex-Alphabet-and-Amazon figure holds up next quarter, that supports the reading that AI demand is arriving as real revenue.

The record is a fact. Roughly two-thirds of what made it is the value of stakes nobody sold.
A number written in by a rule can be written out by the same rule.

## 5. Nomura's three-year profit forecast for Samsung, 1,800 trillion won, tops its market cap by 131 trillion

**Why it matters: **The price of a memory company has always been set by how early the next downturn is pulled into it. A three-year profit forecast above the market cap also means the market has already put that downturn into the price. If long-term agreements really delay it, the discount loses its footing, and if they do not, the forecast is what breaks.

Nomura has raised its operating profit forecasts for Samsung Electronics to 391 trillion, 635 trillion and 774 trillion won across three years.
The three add up to 1,800 trillion won, 131 trillion more than the 1,669 trillion won market cap at the July 31 close.

The same note puts free cash flow at 296 trillion, 456 trillion and 563 trillion won, with shareholder return yields of 11.2%, 17.6% and 22.3% attached.
The source lists the three years in order without naming them. The first is taken here as this year.

## Where does that yield come from

Free cash flow is the cash left in hand after the money spent on plants and equipment is taken out of what the business earned.
A shareholder return yield is the money going back to shareholders through dividends and buybacks, divided by the market cap.

@@REF@@Samsung's chip unit alone earns 88 trillion won, focus turns to a 120 trillion won shareholder return (Seoul Economic Daily English, 2026-07-30)|https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/07/30/samsung-chip-unit-alone-earns-88-trillion-won-focus-turns|https://wimg.sedaily.com/news/cms/2026/07/30/rcv.YNA.20260707.PYH2026070703230001300_P1.jpg

Seoul Economic Daily reports that a 120 trillion won shareholder return covering 2027 through 2029 is under discussion, built on the existing policy of returning 50% of free cash flow.
So the climb from 11.2% to 22.3% is not a number Nomura imagined on its own.
Put the cash flow forecast into a payout policy the company has already stated and the yield falls out mechanically.
Which also means the yield is wrong the moment the cash flow forecast is wrong.

## Why anyone can write down a number three years out

Memory used to be an industry where even next quarter's price was hard to call.
Prices rose, everyone added capacity, and that capacity came back as a glut a few years later.

What changed is the contract.
On its Q2 earnings call Samsung said it is locking 60% to 70% of memory capacity into five-year long-term agreements.
When two thirds of the volume is priced in advance, profit three years out becomes something you can write down.

That is why a profit forecast above the market cap does not read only as evidence of undervaluation.
Whether the contracts actually break the cycle is still unconfirmed, and the market appears to be holding that unconfirmed part out of the price.

## So what settles it

The question comes down to one thing.
Whether the 131 trillion won between the market cap and the forecast is the market's mistake, or a fair discount on the forecast.

The first year is what settles it.
If second-half operating profit clears four times the Q2 run rate and lands near 391 trillion won, the remaining two years carry the same weight.
If it does not, the 635 trillion and 774 trillion figures were already off in year one.

The price of a memory company has always been set by how early the next downturn is pulled into it.
If the long-term agreements really do delay that downturn, today's discount loses its footing, and if they do not, the forecast is what breaks.
A market cap that slipped under $1 trillion within a month and then recovered more than 20% in a single day says the market is swinging on this one assumption.

## 6. Why did a single SK hynix share printing at the daily floor close out 960 accounts?

**Why it matters: **The assumption that a stock trades only where you know it trades no longer holds. One price on a listed company becomes the liquidation reference for a market that neither the company nor its home exchange knows about, and the rules of that market are set by whoever opened it. Korea's daily price limit did not stop this single share, and it was never the thing that could.

At 8:00 on the morning of July 28, one share of SK hynix traded at 1,272,000 won.
That was almost 30% below the previous close, the lowest price the stock was allowed to reach that day.

The single share filled instantly and the price was back above 1.7 million won shortly after.
For the Korean market, that was the end of it.

@@REF@@SK hynix single-share limit trade triggers 83 billion won crypto futures liquidation (Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-07-30)|https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/07/30/sk-hynix-single-share-limit-trade-triggers-83-billion-won|https://wimg.sedaily.com/news/cms/2026/07/30/news-p.v1.20260730.9a7844d0e68446278f0d4efc939778e7_P1.png

Seoul Economic Daily reports that the share filled not on the Korea Exchange but in the pre-open session of Nextrade.
The stock recovered into the 1.7 million won range afterwards, so the domestic impact was limited.

## Why did that share trade at exactly eight in the morning

The timing matters.
The Korea Exchange opens its regular session at nine.
Nextrade, which opened in 2025 as a second venue where the same Korean stocks can be bought and sold, runs its own pre-open session from 8:00 to 8:50.

So that single share filled during the thinnest hour of the Korean trading day.
One price printed in a window with almost no buyers and no sellers became the reference for the day.

## Who was reading that price

There are places outside Korea where people bet on SK hynix.
They trade perpetual futures, futures with no expiry date.
Nothing changes hands. You are only betting on up or down, so a market can be opened anywhere, whatever exchange the stock is actually listed on.

One of the places where such markets are opened is Hyperliquid.
It is not an exchange run by a company with staff, but a program written in advance that executes its own rules.

Whoever opens a market is a separate party.
The SK hynix market was opened by trade.xyz, and trade.xyz also chose where its price would come from.
What it chose was Nextrade.

## The line where a 5% drop closes you out

One of the rules the operator set was leverage of up to ten times.
A million won of your own money can carry a ten million won bet.

Leverage comes with a line called maintenance margin.
Once your money falls below that line the venue does not wait, it closes the position there and then.
At ten times leverage that line sits around 5%, so a 5% fall is enough to start liquidations.

The reference price fell from $1,127.90 to $917.25, about 18.7%.
At a venue that cuts you off at 5%, an 18.7% fall left only one outcome.

@@REF@@Hyperliquid explains $57 million SK Hynix perp liquidations after a price feed anomaly (BeInCrypto, 2026-07-28)|https://beincrypto.com/hyperliquid-sk-hynix-perp-oracle-liquidations/|https://assets.beincrypto.com/img/9bSbOmyA4HHmwqtR8v-GH2122BQ=/1200x630/smart/filters:format(jpeg):quality(82)/2dcb42bf99444cbc8e0debfdfd30acc2

Hyperliquid drew a line, saying it supplies the infrastructure while the party that opens a market sets the rule for where prices come from.
The safeguard trade.xyz had in place allowed a 10% move at once, plus one reset, capping the fall at about 19%.
Because the actual move stayed inside that band, the team says the safeguard worked as designed.

Working as designed is not the same as nothing having happened.
About $57.4 million was liquidated across 960 accounts, with realized losses estimated near $17.3 million.
On the other side, 100 accounts betting on a fall had $10.8 million cut automatically.

## Something went wrong, so where does responsibility sit

Opening a market requires staking 500,000 HYPE with Hyperliquid, worth about $27.4 million.
Validators can vote to burn that stake.
The burnt money, however, does not go to the traders who lost.

This time trade.xyz said it would fully compensate the losses from the forced liquidations and would fix the way it calculates its price.

## 7. The 12,800Mbps on CXMT's LPDDR6 is China's lithography limit written into a spec sheet

**Why it matters: **How far Chinese memory can climb is settled by the standard, not by price. ## What LPDDR6 is and why the speed matters

LPDDR is the low-power DRAM soldered directly onto the board of a phone or a laptop, and LPDDR6 is its newest generation.
Unlike the stick that slots into a server, it is fixed inside the device, so it cannot be swapped later and the speed chosen at purchase stays for the life of the machine.

## Why the top of the standard stays out of reach

@@REF@@CXMT nears LPDDR6 production: the DUV ceiling shows up in the 12.8 Gbps vs 14.4 Gbps gap (TechTimes, 2026-08-02)|https://www.techtimes.com/articles/322700/20260802/cxmt-nears-lpddr6-production-duv-ceiling-shows-128-gbps-vs-144-gbps-gap.htm|https://d.techtimes.com/en/full/471043/photo-shows-view-changxin-memory-technologies-factory.jpg

Chinese memory has taken the route of entering on price and climbing toward the standard.
This number is the first time that road has been measured to the decimal.

## 10. The won came back 115 against the dollar in a month, and not because the dollar weakened

**Why it matters: **An exchange rate is usually read as the dollar getting stronger or weaker, but the cause here sits inside Korea rather than outside it. That the rate slid while a record surplus piled up means the dollars on the ledger and the dollars at the window are not the same thing, and what closed the gap was official pressure rather than the market. This is a moment to watch what stopped rather than what improved.

The won, which was past 1,550 to the dollar in early July, had come back to 1,435 by the end of the month.
That is 115 won in four weeks.

Over the same stretch the dollar index fell only 0.2%.
The dollar did not get cheaper. The won got more expensive.

@@REF@@Korean won likely to extend gains in H2 after hitting 11-week high (KED Global, 2026-07-24)|https://www.kedglobal.com/foreign-exchange/newsView/ked202607240002|https://www.kedglobal.com/data/ked/image/2026/07/24/ked202607240008.jpg

## Why did the won weaken while the surplus grew

Korea has been running its largest current account surplus on record this year.
The January to May total of $141.3 billion passed the $123 billion full-year surplus of 2025, itself a record, in five months.

A current account surplus is an amount booked as earned. It is not a statement that those dollars came home.
When an exporter leaves the dollars it earned sitting in the US, the surplus grows and nothing is converted into won.

When a foreign investor sells Korean shares and leaves, the dollars going out are cash.
By the Bank of Korea's count, $110.2 billion left through equity sales between January and June.

Put simply, the ledger showed a surplus while dollars were walking out the door.

In the third week of July the foreign selling stopped.
This looks less like heavy buying than like fast-moving money pausing.
Once the cash stopped leaving, the surplus that had been piling up finally started to show in the rate.

Rates came in on top of that.
On July 16 the Bank of Korea raised its base rate from 2.50% to 2.75%.
It was the first increase in three and a half years, since early 2023.

It reads more accurately as a question of what stopped than of what improved.

**Why it matters: **What changed is less the rate than the way you read it. ## 17. The Fed swapped its inflation yardstick from CPI to PCE, and accuracy was not the only reason

**Why it matters: **The Fed's 2% target looks like a neutral benchmark, but the index it is measured against was chosen alongside a fiscal problem. As long as a single inflation print moves markets, how that print is built is part of the price.

The US personal consumption expenditures price index fell 0.1% in June from the month before.
That is the first monthly decline in this index in six years.

Against a year earlier it ran 3.7%, down from 4.1% in May.
The biggest single mover was energy goods, including gasoline, which fell 9.2%.

@@REF@@Inflation rate fell by 0.1% in June, first decrease in six years (UPI, 2026-07-30)|https://www.upi.com/Top_News/US/2026/07/30/personal-consumption-expenditures-june-2026/4151785423293/|https://cdnph.upi.com/sv/ph/og/upi/4151785423293/2026/1/6002c295230d40502dff7b0b8abb84de/v1.5/Inflation-rate-fell-by-01-in-June-first-decrease-in-six-years.jpg

UPI reports that the 9.2% drop in energy goods was the largest since August 2022.
What pushed the index negative for a month, in other words, was energy rather than prices in general.

The reason markets cheered the number sits two days earlier, at the Fed meeting.
Rates were held 9 to 3, and all three dissents were for a hike rather than a cut.
Data showing prices cooling takes the wind out of the side arguing for higher rates.

## Why the Fed does not watch the CPI

Here is the part that snags.
The number we call inflation in the news is usually the consumer price index, the CPI.
But the yardstick the Fed measures its 2% target against is not the CPI. It is the personal consumption expenditures price index, the PCE.

The two are built differently.
The CPI fixes more than a hundred thousand items into a basket and prices that basket.
The PCE keeps changing what is in the basket according to what people actually bought.

When beef gets expensive, households buy more chicken.
A fixed basket cannot follow that move, so it charges the full beef increase to the index.
That failure to see shoppers switching to whatever got cheaper, which makes measured inflation run high, is called substitution bias.

## Why this statistic was a political problem

The homework in front of Congress in 1995 was the deficit.
More than half of federal spending was mandatory, programs like Social Security, and both those increases and the income tax brackets were indexed to the CPI.

Indexed means one CPI print moves spending and tax revenue at the same time.
So a finding that the CPI overstated inflation changed the arithmetic.
It would slow the growth of outlays without anyone having to vote to cut pensions.

The commission Congress set up delivered its verdict on December 4, 1996.
The CPI was overstating inflation by about 1.1 percentage points a year, and substitution bias was the largest single piece of it.

@@REF@@Final Report of the Advisory Commission to Study the Consumer Price Index (US Social Security Administration archive, 1996-12-04)|https://www.ssa.gov/history/reports/boskinrpt.html

The report itself spells out why a statistic was a money question.
If the CPI overstated the cost of living by one percentage point a year for a decade, the report calculated, the resulting addition to the deficit would reach roughly $135 billion by 2006, making it the fourth largest federal program behind Social Security, health care and defense.

The Fed's official yardstick changed from CPI to PCE in February 2000.

## Does that yardstick really read lower

The effect of swapping yardsticks is not the kind of thing a single month confirms.
Averages running lower and this month running lower are different claims, and this month was inverted.

## How we will know

In the end there is one question.
Is this cooling the start of a trend, or a one month energy effect.

The place it splits is oil.
What pulled June down was the drop in crude that came out of a temporary truce with Iran, and in July that truce broke and crude is rising again.
If energy turns positive again in the July print, June was a one month affair. If core prices, with food and energy stripped out, keep coming down alongside it, the trend reading holds.

The side that changed the yardstick and the side that asked for the change each had their own arithmetic.
The Fed got a target that is easier to hit, and Congress got a way to shrink the deficit without losing votes.
It is worth knowing how the number markets are cheering today was chosen.

## What a total return swap is

A total return swap is a contract where someone else buys the stock in their own name and you collect only the gain or loss.
Put simply, the name stays with the lender and only the outcome comes back to you.

Say a Chinese private fund wants to own SK Hynix but capital controls keep it from sending dollars abroad.
It signs this contract with a foreign broker such as Goldman Sachs or Morgan Stanley, the broker buys and holds the shares under its own name, and only the price difference is settled inside China.

The money never crosses the border while the offshore gain or loss lands anyway.
With Chinese equities and property both weak, demand reportedly poured through this channel into US, Japanese and Korean technology stocks.

## Why something blocked in June is landing now

These contracts are short.
They come in one, three and six month terms, mostly three, and the way they lasted longer was by being rewritten each time they matured.

Block the renewal and the position has to be sold the moment it comes due.
Because the contracts were struck on different days, the maturities are scattered across June 23 to September 22.

Then July brought a sharp drop in AI and memory names, and with it demands for more collateral.
Anyone who could not wire dollars in time, because of the same capital controls, reportedly had no option but to sell what they held.

When DRAM prices jump, what the company gives up to make HBM instead grows too, and so does its ground for charging more.

## 49. Why Nvidia is guaranteeing $250 billion of OpenAI's debt: the same reason showed up in the fiber-optic bubble 25 years ago

**Why it matters: **Because the loop links Nvidia, OpenAI and Oracle as investor, supplier and customer all at once, whether it ends like the fiber-optic bubble decides the direction of the whole AI investment cycle.

## Why this story is back
A Bloomberg-sourced report today could move Korean markets too.

The same dollars move from Nvidia to OpenAI, to Oracle,

Add Nvidia's stakes in CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN, and this circular financing tops $540 billion.

## The same structure showed up 25 years ago
In the late 1990s, telecom firms raced to lay fiber-optic cable,
betting the internet would make data traffic explode.

Cash-poor startups borrowed from equipment makers to buy that equipment,
and the loans were structured so the money flowed straight back as the lender's own sales.
Nearly $30 billion in such vendor loans went out in 2000 alone.

@@IMG@@datacenter-inside|Today's equivalent of the fiber build-out. This is where the money actually goes.

and the fiber already in the ground turned out to be more than enough.
One new technology was all it took to pop the bubble.

The Nasdaq, which had peaked at 5,048 in March 2000, fell 78% by 2002,
and $2 trillion of telecom-stock value vanished.
Roughly 95% of the fiber laid at the time was never lit even once.

## Where it differs this time
The fiber bubble was built on demand that was overestimated from the start.
Today's AI data-center demand is, at least so far, backed by real contracts and revenue.

By scale, though, this round is already bigger.
As of 2025, Nvidia's vendor financing equaled 67% of its own revenue,
2.8 times larger relative to revenue than Lucent's vendor loans at the telecom bubble's height.

## 57. The inside story of how Samsung is lifting DRAM output 15% at Hwaseong without a new fab

## What exactly is an end-fab
Chipmaking splits into two broad stages.
The main fab etches transistors and circuits onto a wafer, and the end-fab connects those components into a finished chip.
The end-fab is the last step of front-end processing, where the memory becomes something that can actually be used.

Samsung's Memory Manufacturing Technology Center formed a task force this month to build a new commodity DRAM end-fab at H1, the first complex of its Hwaseong campus.

## 59. With 0.6% share in China, why is Samsung weighing Chinese DRAM for its phones?

**Why it matters: **This is a fork in the road for Samsung's phone business: absorb the memory-cost hit, or use rivals' retreat to grab share. Q2 earnings will be the first clue.

Samsung Electronics is weighing whether to use cheap Chinese-made DRAM to chase market share in China's budget smartphone segment, semiconductor analyst Jukan reported on July 27, citing Korean industry sources.
The idea centers on Galaxy A-series budget phones, not the flagship line.

## Why Chinese DRAM, and why now
The AI data-center buildout has companies like Nvidia buying up so much memory that prices are spilling over into phone makers' costs too.
When rising chip prices push up the price of finished devices, the industry calls it "chipflation."

Apple already raised MacBook and iPad prices by about $100 in June, and is expected to raise iPhone prices this fall.
Chinese makers like Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo and Honor don't have Apple's brand pricing power, so they can't pass the cost increase to buyers.
Several have voluntarily cut shipments by 15-20%, reasoning that selling more would only widen losses.

## Why this looks like an opening for Samsung
Samsung currently holds just 0.6% of China's smartphone market.
If cheaper Chinese DRAM lowers the cost of Galaxy A-series phones, Samsung could use that price edge to grab the share its rivals are voluntarily giving up.
Shim Woo-jung, a research fellow at the Korea Institute for Industrial Economics and Trade, said Samsung "could meaningfully increase share by expanding supply of lower-priced models like the Galaxy A series, given that major Chinese rivals have sharply cut production."

## Where the worry comes in
The same chipflation is squeezing Samsung's own Mobile eXperience (MX) division.
Brokerages have slashed second-quarter estimates: Hana Securities projects a 200 billion won loss, Samsung Securities 584.1 billion won, Eugene Investment & Securities 1 trillion won, and iM Securities 800 billion won.
Samsung Securities' swing is the starkest, going from a 3.41 trillion won full-year profit forecast to a 5.841 trillion won loss forecast, a multi-trillion-won reversal from black to red.

SoftBank's energy unit, SB Energy, is building a 10-gigawatt data center campus in Piketon, in southern Ohio.

OpenAI would sign a long-term lease on the facility, and Nvidia would guarantee $250 billion of the construction financing.

A backstop means that if OpenAI can't repay, Nvidia steps in instead.

That profile makes it hard to raise long-term debt directly for a $500 billion campus.
Nvidia's guarantee is the workaround.

## 61. China's CXMT Surges 470% on Debut, Becoming a $489 Billion Chipmaker Overnight

**Why it matters: **China's chip self-sufficiency rally has spread to the IPO market, setting a new reference point in the memory valuation debate.

China's DRAM maker ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) surged 472% on its trading debut on Shanghai's STAR Market on July 27.
Its market cap jumped from $85.5 billion to $489 billion in a single day.
Market watchers, including the X account Serenity, reacted with disbelief to what was Asia's biggest IPO of the year.

## How unusual was this listing
CXMT priced its IPO at 8.66 yuan and closed its first day at 49.50 yuan.
It raised up to 66.6 billion yuan, beating the previous STAR Market record set by chipmaker SMIC in 2020 (53.2 billion yuan).

Reports say the surge pushed CXMT's market cap past Intel's, making it China's largest listed company by that measure.

@@IMG@@dram-module|What CXMT actually makes: DRAM modules like this one.

## Was the 472% really about demand
Demand for AI server DRAM is running well ahead of supply, and that part is real. It's lifting every memory maker's valuation.
But this specific spike has a mechanical cause too.

Only 6.73% of CXMT's total shares were actually tradable at listing.
The rest is locked up until January 27, 2027.

When the sellable float is this thin, a rush of buyers moves the price much further than it would on a normal-sized float.
That's not unique to CXMT. It's a common pattern in early STAR Market debuts.
