Blockade first, toll later: reading Trump's Hormuz play

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

The US military blockaded Iran's entire coastline, effectively an act of war under international law.

How far does the blockade reach

The notice to mariners reported by USNI News covers far more than the strait itself. Any ship suspected of carrying Iranian contraband is a target no matter where it sails, as far off as the Indo-Pacific, and neutral vessels bound for non-Iranian ports are still subject to visit and search. Ships that do not comply may be met with destructive or disabling fire. This is not one waterway closed off, it is the whole flow of goods in and out of Iran placed under guard. The grounds for the card calling this an act of war are sitting in the paperwork itself.

U.S. Reinstates Naval Blockade in Strait of Hormuz (USNI News, 2026-07-13) ↗

Why 20% was never payable

Right after, Trump declared himself 'Guardian of the Hormuz Strait' and floated a 20% fee on all cargo passing through, then dropped it within a day. Meru shows with simple math why the fee was never real: a supertanker carries $160M of crude, so the US fee would be $32M, versus roughly $2M in tolls to Iran. Nobody pays a toll sixteen times more expensive than what they'd pay the enemy. CBS News reached the same place by a different route. It priced a single transit at $24M for a fully laden very large crude carrier and about $17M for a large gas carrier. The International Maritime Organization said the idea would open "a very dangerous Pandora's Box," and Lloyd's List said flatly that there is no legal basis for charging vessels exercising their right of transit passage. That is an objection to the ground the fee stands on, not to its size, which fits the card's reading that the money was never the point.

Trump's Strait of Hormuz fee would have cost millions, opened "a very dangerous Pandora's Box," experts say (CBS News, 2026-07-14)

So what was the real play

So why float it? Meru's read: it's a negotiating card. Trump labels each strike a 'skirmish' rather than war, hits in controlled bursts, and throws out absurd demands only to withdraw them, all to push Iran back to the table. And the oil price rising along the way isn't a bad side bet for the world's largest oil producer.

Why it matters · Energy is the input cost of the AI buildout. Hormuz risk is now a line item in every datacenter power model.
