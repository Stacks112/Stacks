TSMC's Kumamoto fab restarted within a day. So why is it still too early to call this over?

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

A day after the quake, TSMC's Japan fab, JASM, began returning to normal operation in stages. TSMC said its employees were safe and the fab structure was unaffected by the quake, and with that confirmed, production is being brought back step by step, while the detailed equipment inspection and damage assessment are still under way. The construction site of the second fab next door was not affected either, though part of the work was paused over possible aftershocks.

Japan's Meteorological Agency, meanwhile, asked for about a week of caution against another quake of this size. Seismic activity is still elevated, and three more tremors have already been strong enough to shake objects off shelves, a lower 5 on Japan's intensity scale.

Why one week specifically

Ten years ago in this same Kumamoto, a strong quake at intensity 7 came first. People took it for the mainshock. Two days later a larger one, the real mainshock, hit. The first tremor had not been the main event but the leading edge of a bigger one, what seismologists call a foreshock. That is why the agency says this quake looks like the 2016 sequence.

Where this quake came from

Most earthquakes happen where one slab of the earth's crust grinds under another. That boundary is called a subduction zone, and the very large quakes tend to originate there. This one did not. It came from a crack inside Kyushu itself. A crack that can move again is called an active fault, and the one that moved here appears to be the Hinagu fault zone.

The Kumamoto earthquake (M7.1) of July 28, 2026 (Japan Society for Active Fault Studies, 2026-07-28) ↗

The Philippine Sea plate slides under the Eurasian plate here at four to six centimeters a year, loading that fault for centuries. Like a balloon that keeps taking air until it bursts, a fault that has been holding finally slips all at once, and that slip is the earthquake. This epicenter sits about 20 kilometers south of the 2016 epicenter. If the 2016 quake released the stress stored in the northern stretch of the fault, this one appears to have released the southern stretch.

What happened outside the fab

Twenty-eight sections of stone wall collapsed at Kumamoto Castle. Ten people are unaccounted for after a gas leak at an Aeon mall. At a paper plant in Yatsushiro, two people were found in cardiac arrest and nine are missing. Kikuyo Town holds JASM's two fabs along with Sony and Fujifilm facilities inside the same 20-kilometer radius. One plant holding up is not the same as the region holding up.

So what actually settles this

It comes down to one question. Was this the end of it, or the leading edge of something larger the way 2016 was. If no bigger quake arrives within the week and the aftershocks fade, JASM's staged restart holds. If another quake of this size or larger hits, everything resets: the building checks that looked finished, the detailed equipment inspection, the restart schedule. When a fab stops and starts repeatedly, the loss does not scale with downtime alone. Each restart drags the inspection and requalification steps along with it again.

In three lines
  • A day after the magnitude 7.1 quake, TSMC's Japan fab, JASM, began returning to normal operation in stages.
  • On the surface the seismic design and the evacuation drill did their job, and the whole thing looks settled within a day.
  • But Japan's Meteorological Agency asked for about a week of caution against a quake of the same size. Ten years ago in this same place, a bigger mainshock arrived two days after the first strong tremor.
