Jensen Huang's Letter Backs Open AI Models. Anthropic Didn't Sign It

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang posted on X for the first time on July 24, using it to share a letter titled Open Weights and American AI Leadership, arguing open models strengthen safety and cybersecurity and protect national AI sovereignty.

Who actually signed

Per Fortune and Tom's Hardware, the letter launched with roughly 25 signers, Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, Palantir, Hugging Face, IBM, Mistral, Dell, and a16z among them. OpenAI and Google weren't on it. Forbes reported the signer list roughly doubled to about 50 within a day, as OpenAI, Google, AMD, Cisco and GitHub joined late. Anthropic and Amazon never signed at all.

Jensen Huang just used his first ever X post to warn the AI industry not to make the mistake that software narrowly avoided in the 1980s (Fortune, 2026-07-24)

Why did Anthropic sit this out

Per Axios, July 27, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei declined to join, citing a safety concern that open-weight models cannot be recalled once released. That's a substantive safety objection, not just competitive positioning. The timing is notable too. Four days earlier, reports surfaced that the White House was reviving a push to restrict Chinese open-weight models, including Moonshot AI's Kimi K3, released July 16, accused of distilling US models, and days after Xi Jinping made his own openness pitch at China's World AI Conference on July 17, announcing a rival body, the World AI Cooperation Organization, with 29 countries signed on.

Big Tech lines up in support of open-weight AI (Axios, 2026-07-27)
출처: axios.com

Why is Nvidia the one pushing this

Per Yahoo Finance, Nvidia controls roughly 81% of the data-center AI chip market, and more open models spreads chip demand across many more buyers instead of concentrating it in a few efficiency-optimizing closed API providers, a commercial interest baked into the position. Huang himself is quoted saying more AI use means you'll have to sell more Nvidia computers. Forbes summed it up as every signatory argues for openness in someone else's yard. Nvidia itself owns proprietary CUDA and is a $26 billion investor in OpenAI.

So what's the thing to actually watch

Whether to read this letter as industry-wide consensus or a coalition that joined at different times for different self-interested reasons comes down to who signed when, and who didn't. What actually matters is whether this shapes Washington's real export and procurement rules, and how the signatories answer the cannot be recalled safety concern Anthropic raised by staying out.

  • Jensen Huang's first X post shared a letter backing open AI models.
  • That sounds like the whole industry, including Meta, OpenAI and Anthropic, lining up behind it.
  • But the letter launched with about 25 signers, OpenAI and Google joined a day later, and Anthropic never signed at all. CEO Dario Amodei has argued open weights cannot be recalled once released.

Why it matters · Jensen Huang's open-models letter launched with 25 signers before OpenAI and Google joined late, and Anthropic never signed over a safety concern. That Nvidia's chip-sales interest sits behind its open-model advocacy matters for how to read this letter.

Worth asking · Anthropic stayed out over a safety concern that open weights can't be recalled, while Nvidia stands to sell more chips either way. Can this letter be read as a genuine policy conviction, or does it just reflect where each company's business interests land?
