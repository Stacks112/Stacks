The root of the rumor that Micron's Taiwan union is weighing a strike is a 200% bonus cap

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

There is talk that Micron's labor union in Taiwan is considering a strike. What is named as the reason is not pay but how the bonus is calculated.

Micron's Taiwan union reportedly studies Samsung's worker strikes, circulates handout for meeting to discuss the 200% bonus cap (Wccftech, 2026-08-01)
출처: wccftech.com

Wccftech reports that the Taoyuan union held an information session on July 30. It was arranged after workers on the floor asked to learn about unions and strike procedures, and it shared the results of research into strikes in Korea.

Micron bonus cap200% of salary
Samsung's former cap50% of salary
Samsung after the deal10.5% of chip division operating profit, no cap

The three numbers do not measure the same thing. Micron and the old Samsung capped the top against salary, while post-deal Samsung switched to sharing profit and opened the top. In a stretch where profit multiplies, that difference multiplies with it.

Retrieved 2026-08-03 · Micron and Samsung bonus terms, as reported by Wccftech

Why this talk surfaces now

Memory companies' profits have risen many times over within a year. But when a bonus is capped at a percentage of salary, the amount received stops at that line no matter how far profit runs. Two hundred percent of salary means someone earning $100,000 can receive at most another $200,000 as a bonus. In absolute terms that is not small. What it is not is responsive. The ceiling does not move with what the company earns. In a year profit rose thirty-fold and in an ordinary year, the upper end sits in the same place. Samsung ran into this first.

A 45,000-person labor strike at Samsung's memory chip plants could throw a wrench into the AI boom (Fortune, 2026-05-17)
출처: fortune.com

Fortune covered the large strike in Samsung's semiconductor division in May. Samsung then moved its bonus from a salary-based cap to sharing 10.5% of chip division operating profit with no ceiling. The talk now circulating in Taiwan comes after watching that result.

Where this reaches the share price

Before the strike itself, the thing to look at is the bonus structure. Tie the bonus to profit and boom-time labor cost rises with profit, shaving the top of the margin. Cap it against salary and the margin holds, but demands like this one come back from the labor side. Neither choice is free, and in a stretch where memory profits are at records, the price of that choice grows.

So what settles it

The question comes down to one thing. Whether this is one plant in Taiwan, or the start of bonus structures moving to profit-sharing across the industry. Micron's response settles it. If the company keeps the 200% cap, it stays a dispute at one plant, and if it switches to profit-linked terms as Samsung did, labor cost rising with profit becomes the industry standard. The author wrote this down as a rumor and said he has asked the company to confirm. What is established is that the union held an information session. Whether there is a strike, and when, is not decided.

In three lines
  • There is talk that Micron's labor union in Taiwan is weighing a strike, with bonuses less generous than Samsung's or SK hynix's named as the reason.
  • On the surface it reads as a pay dispute at one plant.
  • But what separates them is the method, not the amount. Micron caps the top at 200% of salary, while Samsung, after a strike, moved to sharing 10.5% of chip division operating profit with no ceiling.
