The Fed cut 175 basis points, so why is the 30-year above 5 percent?

Charlie Bilello@charliebilello

The longest duration bond ETF is now down 63% from its peak in March 2020.

How is that possible?

The 30-Year Treasury yield has moved from an all-time low of 0.8% in March 2020 up to 5.3% today.

Long duration + Rising interest rates from all-time low levels = Pain

August 16, 2026

The Federal Reserve cut its policy rate by 175 basis points, and over the same stretch the 30-year Treasury yield went the other way. It was below 4 percent when the cuts began in September 2024. It is above 5 percent now.

Long bond rout drives TLT ETF to lows unseen since 2004 (Benzinga, 2026-08-14) ↗

The policy rate and the 30-year bond share a name and little else. What the Fed sets is the price of money banks lend each other overnight. The 30-year is the price of money lent today against a promise to repay in three decades. The Fed sets the first. Buyers and sellers set the second.

So what actually moved

30-year Treasury5.21%
10-year Treasury4.63%
2-year Treasury4.15%
10-year breakeven inflation2.27%
30-year real yield2.97%
Fed funds upper bound3.75%

The upper bound of the policy rate is 3.75 percent and the 30-year sits at 5.21 percent. Breakeven inflation stays on the low side, so what pushed the long bond up is the real yield rather than the inflation outlook. That points somewhere other than the cause Bilello names.

Checked 2026-08-17 · FRED (DGS30, DGS10, DGS2, T10YIE, DFII30; observations 2026-08-13 to 08-14) · Fed funds upper bound from FRED DFEDTARU

The real yield is the nominal yield minus expected inflation. When it rises, borrowing money for a long time has become expensive on its own terms, whatever prices are doing. Bilello reads the rise as inflation refusing to let the Fed go. Yet the ten-year breakeven the market is pricing sits in the middle of the 2 percent range. If the inflation outlook is quiet and only the long bond is climbing, what went up is not the price of inflation but the price of tying money up for a long time. That price is called the term premium. The New York Fed's estimate of the ten-year term premium was reported at 0.8 percent in May 2026. During the zero rate years the same estimate was negative.

We could not verify the 63 percent

The longest duration bond ETF Bilello refers to appears to be a fund holding very long Treasuries that pay no coupon along the way. We could not verify the claim of a 63 percent decline from the March 2020 peak against source data. What we could verify is the fund next to it. TLT, which holds Treasuries of 20 years and longer, is down about 52 percent from its 2020 peak at $81.80, its lowest since 2004. The record low yield is also slightly off. Bilello wrote 0.8 percent for March 2020. On FRED's daily close the lowest day is March 9, 2020, and the value that day fell just short of 1 percent.

    The 30-year yield hit 0.99 percent, a record low on a daily close basis

  • 2024-09-18

    The Fed began cutting. The 30-year was below 4 percent

  • 2025-12-10

  • 2026-08-13

    A $25 billion 30-year auction cleared at 5.216 percent, the highest since 2001

The policy rate came down while the 30-year rose more than four percentage points across these four dates.

FRED (DGS30, DFEDTARU) · US Treasury 30-year auction result, 2026-08-13 · checked 2026-08-17

The reason the drawdown is this large is duration. Duration measures how far a bond's price moves when yields move one percentage point, and it grows with maturity. A bond that pays no interest along the way has all of its repayment stacked at the very end, which gives it the longest duration of all. Put simply, the same rise in yields cuts deeper the longer the maturity. Counting reinvested interest narrows the loss. TLT's five-year total return is reported at minus 27.4 percent.

What the August 13 auction left behind

So is a 30-year above 5 percent a signal that inflation is unbeaten, or that tying money up for a long time has become expensive? The August 13 auction shows half of the answer.

Treasury pays 5.216% on 30-year bond, costliest long debt since 2001 (PrimeRates, 2026-08-14)
출처: primerates.com

The bid-to-cover ratio was 2.39, below the recent average of 2.43 to 2.44, and dealers were left with 11.5 percent against an average of 10.6 percent. Dealers taking more means fewer other buyers showed up. Indirect bidders, the usual proxy for foreign demand, took 66.8 percent against an average of 67.0 percent. What went missing was domestic, not foreign. If breakeven inflation stays where it is from here and the 30-year still rises, what lifted it is the term premium and not prices. If breakevens rise alongside it, Bilello's reading is the right one. Whether the Fed's cuts ever reach mortgage rates and corporate long-term funding costs lies on the far side of that fork.

In three lines
  • The Fed has cut six times since September 2024, while the 30-year Treasury yield went from below 4 percent to 5.2 percent.
  • Bilello reads it as the Fed being done with inflation while inflation was not done with the Fed.
  • The numbers we pulled point elsewhere. Ten-year breakeven inflation is quiet in the low 2 percent range, and what moved is a 30-year real yield close to 3 percent.
