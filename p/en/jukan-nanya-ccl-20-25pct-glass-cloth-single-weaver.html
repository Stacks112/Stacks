Nan Ya Plastics Raises CCL Prices, Citing “Glass Fabric” Shortage

August 14, 2026

A price notice that Nan Ya Plastics' electronic materials division sent to customers in August has been made public. CCL and prepreg prices go up 20 to 25%, applied to shipments from September 1. The notice says that amid a broad rise in raw material costs the shortage of glass cloth in particular has been severe, that the supply gap widened month by month, and that procurement costs ran beyond control. Copper is not what it leads with.

Why glass and not copper

CCL is the board that sits underneath a printed circuit board. Glass fibre woven into cloth is impregnated with resin and cured, then faced with copper foil, and that is CCL. The half-finished state before curing is prepreg. Put simply, the glass cloth is the skeleton of the board and the copper is the wiring drawn on top of it. Without the skeleton there is nowhere to draw the wiring.

That report says a Seoul board maker placed advance orders with two Taiwanese suppliers worth more than five times its typical monthly usage. That is not the behaviour of a buyer negotiating a price. It is the behaviour of one reserving a place in line.

One company does the weaving

Boards for AI servers move to glass cloth with a lower dielectric constant as signals get faster. Japan's Nittobo holds roughly 90% of that T-glass segment, and 60 to 70% of the NER-glass grade below it.

Glass Fiber Cloth: The Underlying Material Shortage in AI Infrastructure (TrendForce Insights, 2026-04-30)

The same analysis expects that company's new capacity to start running by the middle of 2027 at the earliest. Meanwhile consumption keeps climbing, as Nvidia's Rubin generation raises both substrate area and layer count and cableless designs add midplane and backplane demand. Reuters reported in November 2025 that the two companies had joined forces to expand special glass cloth production. The side sending the increase notice is also the side that has decided to make the material itself. When supply is fixed for more than a year while demand grows, raising the price does not produce more material. Being unable to buy because it is expensive and being unable to buy because there is none left are different problems, and money does not solve the second one.

The gaps between increases got shorter and the steps got larger. That shape is what separates passing on a cost from rationing a material.

DigiTimes and TrendForce reporting · retrieved 2026-08-14

If board makers pass the increase straight through to set makers, it ends as a cost transfer. If they cannot, margins take it, and from that point orders go to whoever secured glass cloth. What decides this is not the size of the increase but when Nittobo's new line runs. If it starts in the middle of 2027 this increase stays a one-year cost wave. If that date slips again, the schedule of every product that uses a circuit board is tied to a single kind of woven glass.

In three lines
  • Taiwan's Nan Ya Plastics has told customers it is raising the price of the base material under printed circuit boards.
  • On the surface it is one more increase driven by the price of copper.
  • But the reason the notice gives is a shortage of glass cloth, and one Japanese company holds 90% of the market that weaves the high-end grade.
