Why is Taiyo Yuden named the winner when AI is soaking up the capacitors?

August 17, 2026

Lines were retooled to make more of the high-grade capacitors that AI servers need, and the ordinary capacitors that go into phones and laptops started running short. Serenity expects that squeeze to bite in earnest late in the second half of 2026 or early in 2027.

Capacitors come in grades

An MLCC is a fingernail-sized part built from stacked ceramic layers. It holds a little charge and releases it, smoothing the power that runs through a circuit. MLCCs are graded by how little their capacitance drifts as temperature swings. X5R drifts more but is cheap and made in volume, which is why it fills phones and laptops. X6S and X7R hold steadier across a wider temperature range, are harder to build, and go onto AI server boards. That is the crux. The two grades mostly come off the same lines rather than separate plants. Every hour spent printing the upper grade is an hour not spent on the lower one.

What dries up in the space left behind

Serenity reported that channel checks in China's electronics market found 22 microfarad and 47 microfarad parts out of stock, and that some manufacturers had stopped taking orders altogether. The post also noted Walsin of Taiwan citing 22 microfarad among the high-volume specifications used in smartphones and PCs.

That report confirms the same movement from a different angle. Channel inventory for general-purpose parts from 1 to 22 microfarad has fallen below the 30-day warning line, and consumer-grade X5R capacity is being converted to the high-end specifications AI demands. The shortage, in other words, has not stayed inside the AI grade.

Where the benefit splits

Murata, share of the whole MLCC marketabout 40%
Samsung Electro-Mechanics, whole marketabout 18%
Taiyo Yuden, whole marketabout 10%

Across the whole market Taiyo Yuden is a quarter of Murata's size. Isolate AI server parts, though, and the ranking does not carry over. Serenity's case rests on exactly that mismatch.

By that account Taiyo Yuden holds under 4% of AI server MLCCs. It holds about 10% of the market as a whole, so most of its revenue still sits on the general-purpose side. This is where Serenity's pick comes from. Having been left outside the AI boom means still making a great deal of the product whose price is now rising. By the same logic Murata and Samsung Electro-Mechanics earn in the AI grade while shrinking their own general-purpose volume.

Taiyo Yuden's September 1 is the first checkpoint

There is already a scale on which to read whether prices really move. Samsung Electro-Mechanics lifted its entire lineup 30% from August 1, and Taiyo Yuden is reportedly weighing a September 1 increase, according to TrendForce. Serenity disclosed in the post that they hold Taiyo Yuden stock. The argument is best read as a case made by someone with a position rather than a neutral observation. The share gap it leans on, however, is confirmed by third-party data. What divides investors is how long the crowding-out lasts. In the 2018 MLCC shortage the book-to-bill ratio peaked at 1.25. At the end of this June all three suppliers were at or above that line. If Taiyo Yuden puts the September 1 increase through and general-purpose channel prices hold above August levels into the fourth quarter, Serenity's picture is filling in. If the increase is dropped or prices slip back below August, this tightness was a story that ended inside the AI grade.

In three lines
  • Lines turned over to AI server capacitors, and the ordinary ones inside phones and laptops began to run short.
  • Read one way, the winners are Murata and Samsung Electro-Mechanics, who supply most of the AI volume.
  • But the name Serenity picked is Taiyo Yuden, which holds under 4% of AI server parts.
