Oil crossed $100 again. The same war already took it to $126, then gave back 38% of that in ten weeks

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Brent crude has broken back above $100 a barrel, and the Kobeissi Letter says inflation expectations and interest rates are climbing sharply again as a result.

The move is real, but the pace is a rough estimate

Brent traded around $71.5-$72.4 a barrel in early July before rising to just above $100 by July 23, a move in the ballpark of the roughly 40% the original figure claimed, though no single sourced article pins down an exact '42% in 20 days' statistic, so that number is best treated as an approximation rather than a precise, independently verified figure.

This isn't oil's first trip to $100-plus in this war

What the original framing leaves out matters: this same Iran war already sent Brent to $126 a barrel, a four-year high, on April 30, 2026. Prices then fell more than 38% from that peak, dropping below $70 by early July on ceasefire and diplomacy optimism, before this latest escalation reversed the decline. The July move to $100 is the second leg of an ongoing rollercoaster tied to one conflict, not a fresh, first-of-its-kind milestone.

Oil prices fall to levels not seen since start of US-Israel war on Iran (Al Jazeera, 2026-07-02)
출처: aljazeera.com

Why the level still matters

$100 is a psychological line that traders and central banks watch closely, since oil feeds directly into gasoline, shipping, and nearly everything else. This latest leg up builds on a month of escalating headlines, Houthi tanker strikes, Iran threatening energy infrastructure, and warnings that the Strait of Hormuz will not return to pre-war shipping conditions, each adding its own premium on top of the last.

The question this history raises

If oil holds near $100, central banks leaning toward cutting rates face a harder case, and fuel-dependent companies face a fresh squeeze. But given that the same war already produced a much larger spike that fully reversed within ten weeks, the more useful question may not be how high oil goes if the war escalates further, but how much of this move survives if diplomacy makes any progress at all.

In three lines
  • Brent crude crossed $100 a barrel again around July 23, up roughly 40% from early July, as the Iran war escalated with Houthi tanker strikes.
  • This isn't oil's first spike in this war: the same conflict pushed Brent to $126, a four-year high, on April 30, 2026, before it fell back below $70 by early July.
  • Analysts warn further upside toward $120+ is possible if the war keeps escalating, but the April-to-July round trip is the stronger precedent for how fast $100 oil could reverse.
