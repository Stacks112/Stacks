The AI infrastructure and security software index is up 22% this year. Why is application software plunging?

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

US software stocks are splitting into clear winners and losers this year, and the divide runs along a line most investors haven't drawn yet, according to the Kobeissi Letter. Infrastructure and cybersecurity software indexes are up 22% year to date, while vertical (industry-specific) and general application software indexes are down 28% and 34%, pulling the broad US software index down 13% for the year.

Original post: The Kobeissi Letter (X, 2026-07-28) ↗

AI hype isn't the real dividing line, the pricing model is

Kobeissi frames this simply as AI dividing the industry into winners and losers. Deeper reporting suggests the actual mechanism is narrower and more mechanical: software sold per user seat is the one losing ground, regardless of what industry it serves. If an AI agent can do the work five employees used to do, a company now needs to buy one license instead of five. Industry coverage has tied that dynamic to a slowdown in vertical SaaS revenue growth, from about 14% in 2024 to roughly 12.2% by late 2025.

Not every 'vertical' product is actually exposed

A separate AllianceBernstein research note complicates the industry-vs-industry framing further. It argues the real fault line isn't infrastructure versus applications at all, it's how replaceable the software is. Products with low switching costs and thin proprietary data, mostly seat-priced interface tools, are easy for an AI agent to replace. Systems embedded deep in a business, even some vertical software running banking or retail operations, are harder to dislodge no matter the category label.

So what's the thing to actually watch

The question comes down to one thing: is this split really about what a company sells, or about how it charges for it? If it's mostly an industry story, infrastructure and security software should keep outperforming almost regardless of pricing model. If it's mostly a pricing-model story, some vertical software that has already moved away from flat per-seat licensing toward usage-based or outcome-based pricing should hold up better than this week's index split suggests. Some 'infrastructure' names still selling by the seat should eventually feel the same pressure. Next quarter's earnings season, when more companies disclose how their pricing models are shifting, will start to answer which read is closer to right. What that actually means is watching the pricing model, not the industry label. Software sold under flat per-seat licensing carries the same AI-substitution risk whether it calls itself infrastructure or application software, as long as switching costs stay low. An application-software name that has already moved to usage- or outcome-based pricing, on the other hand, may hold up better than this quarter's index drop implies. The one thing to check next earnings season is which application-software companies disclose a shift away from per-seat pricing, since those could be undervalued relative to this index gap. Don't read the infrastructure and security label itself as a safety signal, either. Some names in that category are still selling by the seat, and they carry the same exposure.

In three lines
  • The Kobeissi Letter says US AI infrastructure and security software indexes are up 22% year to date.
  • Workplace software indexes, vertical and application, are down 28% and 34% respectively, dragging the broad software index down 13%.
  • Forbes and AllianceBernstein research trace the real dividing line not to industry category but to per-seat pricing models.
