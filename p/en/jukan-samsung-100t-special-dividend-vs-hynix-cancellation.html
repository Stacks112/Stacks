Why is Samsung handing cash back as dividends while SK hynix cancels shares?

Samsung Electronics Formalizes an Era of Record Shareholder Returns Exceeding KRW 100 Trillion: “Even More Next Year”

According to Korean media reports, Samsung Electronics plans to convene a board meeting in August to approve major shareholder-return measures, including a…

August 20, 2026

Samsung Electronics is expected to hold a board meeting before the end of August to approve a shareholder return package that includes a special cash dividend, and the total is reported to exceed 100 trillion won. Reuters carried the same reporting as a return programme of more than $72 billion, and the shares rose more than 9% on August 20.

Samsung jumps 9% on record shareholder return hopes (Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-08-20)

None of this has been confirmed in a company filing yet. It is at the reporting stage. The size of the number is what makes the story legible.

The first box is what a year of rising memory prices left inside the company. It doubled in twelve months. Paying that out would not empty the account, but it comes out of the same wallet as the third box.

Retrieved 2026-08-20. Net cash per Korea Herald, 2026-08-11; operating profit from Samsung's second-quarter 2026 results, 2026-07-30; capital spending per Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-08-14.

Samsung has run its returns in three-year programmes. It returned 33.5 trillion won from 2015 to 2017, 39.6 trillion won from 2018 to 2020, and 29.4 trillion won from 2021 to 2023. All three together come to 102.5 trillion won. This would do that in one move.

What separates a dividend from a cancellation

There are two ways to hand money back. A cash dividend moves money from the company's account to the shareholder's account. A share cancellation has the company buy its own stock and destroy it, so fewer shares remain and each surviving share owns more. The difference that matters here is the ownership percentage. When the share count falls, a holder who kept the same number of shares sees their percentage rise on its own. A day earlier, on August 19, SK hynix said it would buy back and cancel 40 trillion won of its own stock, some 24.07 million shares, or about 3.3% of those outstanding.

SK hynix launches record $29b share buyback and cancellation after stock loses half its value (Korea JoongAng Daily, 2026-08-19) ↗

SK hynix's largest shareholder is SK Square, which holds 20.1%. Korean fair trade law requires a company under a holding structure to keep at least 20% of a listed subsidiary. That 20.1% leaves a tenth of a percentage point of room. Cancellation works in the direction of widening that room. Samsung has no such percentage constraint, which is why the original post reads dividends as the easier instrument for its governance structure.

The price of tripling the payout

The case for paying out

This cash piled up at the top of the memory cycle. A company that does not return it now will have nothing to return in the downturn.

VS
The case for holding

The same cash is what pays for the US fabs and the next process node. Take it out now and the next expansion has to be borrowed.

The split is whether the same cash is money to spend now or money already spoken for. First-half capital spending ran 28 trillion won, which points to roughly 60 trillion for the year. On the foundry side the second Taylor fab in Texas is set to break ground at year-end, and on the memory side the Pyeongtaek lines keep expanding. This payout shares an account with those plans. There is a policy backdrop as well. Korea moved dividend income tax to a progressive schedule in January, from 14% below 20 million won to 30% above 5 billion won. Larger dividends therefore carry a tax bill for controlling shareholders, so choosing dividends anyway says the governance reason outweighs the tax one.

What the August board meeting settles

A 9% move on an unconfirmed number means the board resolution either confirms the expectation or trims it. If the total clears 100 trillion won with a special dividend inside it, the reporting was right. If it lands below that, or if no resolution comes in August, the market ran ahead. After that, watch the mix. How that total splits between dividends and cancellations shows whether this company is returning cash for reasons that sit in the ownership register or in the cash flow statement.

In three lines
  • Samsung Electronics, whose net cash swelled to 167.59 trillion won by the end of June on the memory boom, is reported to be voting on a special cash dividend this month.
  • On the surface it reads as a memory boom being handed back to shareholders.
  • But its last three-year programme returned 29.4 trillion won. This would be more than three times that in one move, out of the same account that pays for the US fabs.
