US Data Centers Will Need 194 Gigawatts by 2035, Says a Forecast That's Already Tripled in 15 Months

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

US data center electricity demand will surge 253% from 2026 levels to 194 gigawatts by 2035, equal to roughly 194 nuclear reactors' worth of power, with data centers' share of total US electricity use rising from about 6% today to 12% by 2030 and 20% by 2035, Kobeissi reports.

Where does this number come from

The original source is BloombergNEF (BNEF), reported by Bloomberg on July 21. The 194 GW and 20% by 2035 figures match exactly. But that forecast is itself 83% above BNEF's own previous estimate from December 2025 (106 GW), which was itself a big jump from BNEF's April 2025 estimate of 78 GW. That's three different BNEF numbers in 15 months: 78, then 106, then 194 gigawatts. No secondary source reports the exact 253% figure verbatim. Tom's Hardware and DataCenterDynamics describe it as 83% above the prior forecast, while TechCrunch calls it four times today's level. Back-calculating from a roughly 55 GW 2026 baseline does get you to 194 divided by 55, or plus 253%, so the arithmetic checks out, but no secondary report states that baseline explicitly.

AI Boom to Drive Data Center Electricity Demand to 20% of US Total by 2035 (Bloomberg, 2026-07-21) ↗

What do other research houses say

EPRI puts the 2030 data center share at 9-17%. A World Resources Institute roundup of multiple forecasters shows a range from Lawrence Berkeley National Lab's 6.7-12% up to Boston Consulting Group's roughly 25%. BNEF's 12% (2030) and 20% (2035) sit toward the upper-middle of that wide range. This is one estimate among several, not a settled consensus.

US data center power demand surpasses 150 GW, with PJM and ERCOT accounting for 60% of the facilities (Enerdatics, 2025-08-04)
출처: enerdatics.com

Is the PJM concentration real

Yes. Of over 150 GW of announced US data center capacity tracked by Enerdatics, PJM holds roughly 45% (67 GW) and ERCOT in Texas about 13% (20 GW). BNEF projects PJM will send 34% of its electricity to data centers by 2035. Per an Introl blog post from February 2026, PJM's queue for large new interconnections holds over 60 GW pending, with average waits exceeding four years. The national average interconnection wait has grown from under two years in 2008 to about five years now.

So what's the thing to actually watch

The bigger picture, power replacing chips as the AI buildout's real bottleneck, is already showing up in utility earnings (Southern Company's data-center-driven demand up 42%, Duke Energy with 7.6 GW under contract). But the precise 2035 number should be read as the latest, most aggressive version of a forecast that's more than doubled in 15 months, not a fixed target.

In three lines
  • Kobeissi reports US data center power demand will surge 253% by 2035, to 194 gigawatts.
  • That reads like a settled future number.
  • But that 194 GW figure is BloombergNEF's own forecast, and BNEF's own number has already moved from 78 to 106 to 194 GW in 15 months, and no other outlet independently reports the exact 253% growth figure.

Why it matters · The 194-gigawatt figure isn't settled, even BNEF's own forecast has changed three times in 15 months, and the 253% growth rate isn't independently confirmed elsewhere. It's worth separating the bigger picture, power as the AI buildout's bottleneck, from the precision of the specific number.
