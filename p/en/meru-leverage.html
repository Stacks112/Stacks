A brake on 2x leverage: Korea's cash rule aims at the Samsung/Hynix retail crowd

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

From August, Korea is putting a brake on the 2x leveraged products that let retail investors double their bet on Samsung and SK Hynix. Meru walks through the rules, and the theme is one thing: raise the cash bar high enough to filter the small guy out.

How much cash do you actually need

From August 5, the base deposit jumps from 10 million won to 30 million, and the minimum trade unit goes from 1 share to 20. Brokers are also barred from the marketing trick of waiving or cutting that deposit for higher-tier clients. The real teeth come on August 19. Until now you could fill up to 70% of the deposit with stocks and bonds you already held, needing only a little actual cash; from that date, you need 30 million won in pure cash sitting in the account. Holding 300 million won of bank shares won't count: if your cash is under 30 million, you can't buy more. In plain terms, it gets much harder to average down when the price falls.

Why not just move to Hong Kong

And this applies not just to Korean names but to overseas leverage like 2x Tesla too. It is a signal not to just flee to the US or Hong Kong market. The Korea Herald says the same thing. The 30 million won cash requirement follows the buyer into single-stock leveraged products whether they are listed in Korea or overseas. What that piece watches more closely is the other side of it. Exchanges in the US, Hong Kong and Britain keep adding leveraged products tied to Korean stocks and indexes, and Korean investors' net buying of US stocks jumped to $2.6 billion in July from $633 million in June. Squeeze the cash and the demand does not vanish, it moves offshore.

Will this really cut retail demand

Meru's one-liner: with the deposit hike plus the no-collateral rule, the real hurdle feels north of 50 million won. It will cut retail demand somewhat, but whether that's enough remains to be seen. The regulator did not wait and see. The Investor reports that the Financial Services Commission pulled both measures, one set for August 5 and one for August 19, forward into a single date of July 31. Proceeds from a stock sale only count toward the deposit on T+2, once settlement is done. The same article adds that the FSC will discuss pulling forward the move to a 20-share trading unit as well, a step originally scheduled for November. The height Meru measured stands, and only the moment you hit it has moved forward.

What slipped through is written up in that earlier Korea Herald piece. Index-type leverage built from many names, like KOSPI 200 or Nasdaq 100, sits outside this tightening. The examples it gives are SOXL, which chases three times the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, and KORU, which chases three times the MSCI Korea Index. What the regulator wants to squeeze is money piled onto one single stock.

Why it matters · Emin Yurumazu's bear case named Korea's leveraged retail crowd as the detonator. This is the regulator reaching for that fuse: fewer forced buyers on the way up, and fewer forced sellers if it turns.
