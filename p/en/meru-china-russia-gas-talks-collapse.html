China's Gas Offer Is Below Russia's Own Delivery Cost, and Probably Not About a Bridge

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Gas pricing negotiations between Russia and China over the second Power of Siberia (POS2) pipeline have collapsed. China reportedly offered $50 per 1,000 cubic meters; Russia asked for $250, according to Korean market commentator Meru. The numbers check out. Asia Times and bne IntelliNews both independently confirm China's $50 offer and Russia's $250 ask. Russia's own delivery cost from its Yamal fields to China runs around $125 per 1,000 cubic meters. China currently pays $240-280 through the existing Power of Siberia 1 line. Gazprom charges other international buyers an average of around $420, and China's LNG import price runs around $370. Against those numbers, China's $50 doesn't even cover Russia's delivery cost, while Russia's $250 ask looks like a reasonable middle ground.

Meru points to a bridge dispute as the reason

Meru links the breakdown to an entirely different dispute: a rail bridge over the Tumen River, on the China-North Korea border. Completed in April 2026, the bridge could give China access to the Sea of Japan, but Russia's side has been slow finishing approach roads and customs facilities, and North Korea reportedly suspects Russia is deliberately dragging its feet out of deference to China. Meru's theory is that China's unusually low gas offer could be a signal of frustration over the bridge.

Tumen River bridge delay rattles North Korea (Daily NK, 2026-06-24)
출처: dailynk.com

The bridge dispute itself is real and independently reported. But no wire service or outlet, including this one, connects the bridge to the gas talks. Meru himself is careful to add that China's real motive can't be confirmed from outside.

Meru's read

China's unusually low offer could be a signal of frustration or retaliation over the delayed bridge.

VS
What other sources suggest

POS2 is still pre-construction and China is Russia's only realistic large buyer, so the lowball may just be a negotiating tactic from the side that isn't in a hurry.

The split is whether the price gap is a political message, or simply a calculation from the side with nothing to lose by waiting.

The real leverage looks like it's somewhere else

Analysis from the Carnegie Endowment shows POS2 is still stuck at a September 2025 agreement-in-principle stage, with construction likely taking at least five years and gas flows not expected before 2031. Both sides are negotiating over a pipeline that still exists only on paper. Over the same period, Russia's pipeline gas exports to Europe fell 44% in 2025 alone, hitting their lowest level since the mid-1970s. The Ukraine transit route shut down in January 2025, leaving only TurkStream. In Carnegie's own words, "China is the only buyer capable of taking the huge quantity of gas [Russia] has available."

Russia's Pipeline Gas Sales to Europe Plunge to 50-Year Low (OilPrice.com, 2025-12-30)
출처: oilprice.com

So what's the thing to actually watch

The question comes down to one thing: is China's $50 offer a political signal tied to the bridge, or just hardball from the side under no time pressure? If it's really a political message, watch whether the bridge's approach-road construction suddenly speeds up, or keeps dragging. If it's pure commercial leverage, expect construction to keep slipping while China announces new LNG contracts elsewhere first. A pipeline can't be rerouted to another buyer once it's built. Time isn't on Russia's side, and China has no reason to be in a hurry yet.
