Musk is building a chip fab, and he says he will not buy the light that draws the circuits

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

A signal came out of Terafab, the chip plant Musk is building in Texas, that he intends to make the light that draws the circuits himself. Only one company in the world can make that light today, ASML of the Netherlands.

Musk signals interest in FEL-EUV for Terafab, potentially cutting power versus ASML's current light source (TrendForce, 2026-08-12)
출처: trendforce.com

It started with a night rendering of Terafab that began circulating on X on August 6. A large ring runs under the building in the picture, and people asked whether that was a machine for pushing electrons to near the speed of light. Musk answered with three letters and one more: "FEL FTW". FEL stands for free electron laser. It is a machine that accelerates electrons very hard and then pulls light out of them. FTW is internet shorthand for "this is the way", so putting the two together says the light source is going to be an FEL.

How is EUV light made

Chip circuits are drawn with light. Drawing them finer takes light of a shorter wavelength, and what the industry uses now is extreme ultraviolet at 13.5 nanometres, EUV.

It burns metal to get light, which is why it drinks electricity. What Musk wants to replace is the first two steps.

An FEL has no metal to burn. Light comes out as long as there is force pushing the electrons, and the force given back by decelerating electrons can be returned to push the next ones. Meru writes that this is why Musk describes the route in terms of free energy.

Electricity is the resource that runs out first for anyone building AI chips right now. If the power behind the same light falls that far, the question of how many lithography tools fit inside one plant gets a different answer.

Elon Musk's Terafab enters the EUV lithography race with FEL technology (KuCoin News, 2026-08-11)
출처: kucoin.com

That piece frames the move as a challenge to ASML's monopoly while noting in the same breath that no one has ever run production lithography off an FEL.

Ten metres and one kilometre

Size is the price. An ASML EUV tool is a little over ten metres long, while a machine that accelerates electrons to pull light out of them runs past a kilometre when it is built for research. Korea has one, the fourth-generation light source accelerator run by the Pohang Accelerator Laboratory, and Meru notes that it too is more than a kilometre end to end. Meru's reading is that the arithmetic still has a chance. One FEL can feed light to dozens of lithography tools, and a machine built to make lithography light rather than research light can be far shorter.

The number 2028

Musk did not raise the idea of making EUV light with an FEL first. A US startup chaired by former Intel chief executive Pat Gelsinger is developing the same route, with the US government among its stakeholders. What that company has promised, though, is also one prototype in 2028. There is no FEL supplier anywhere that can feed light to volume production. So the thing being decided now is not whether an FEL can make the light. It already can. What is being decided is whether that light is even and steady enough to draw circuits on a wafer, and when that gets confirmed. If one FEL source clears production lithography inside 2028, ASML's monopoly gets a deadline attached to it for the first time; if the route is still a prototype by then, Terafab joins the queue at ASML like everyone else.

In three lines
  • A ring running under Terafab, Musk's chip plant, showed up in a rendering, and he confirmed it is a free electron laser.
  • Read plainly, that says he intends to make the lithography light source only ASML has been able to make.
  • The company that has been developing the same route has promised one prototype in 2028, so what is at stake now is the schedule rather than the physics.
