The semiconductor bear case hasn't changed in months. So why is the selling happening now?

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Jukan posted a short note on July 27. Nothing weighing on the market, he wrote, is a new negative. The peaking cycle, the capex squeeze on profitability, China's push for chip self-sufficiency: all of it has sat on the same list for months. The real reason for the selling, in his reading, is that investors want to bank what they have made.

Why does old bad news keep resurfacing

Jukan is asking readers to reverse the causality. People do not read a new negative and then sell. Those who have already decided to sell go hunting for a justification afterwards. So the same few stories keep getting summoned. Cycle, capex and China sit quietly while prices climb, then get pulled out the moment everyone wants to cash in.

Wanting to sell, or unable to buy

Jukan frames this as sentiment. Set another number from the same day beside it and a second explanation appears. By The Kobeissi Letter's count, equity allocation across US households, pensions, insurers and funds has reached a record 65%, up 31 points since 2008. That puts the starting point near 34%. At this level the money already invested dwarfs what is still waiting to come in. Before this is a question of who wants to sell, it may be one of who has room left to add.

The common read

The list of negatives is unchanged, so no new information appeared and only sentiment moved.

VS
Another angle

The list can stay the same while the money funding that spending changes hands. That never shows up on the list.

Was there really nothing new

Thirteen hours before the note, reports said Nvidia was in talks to backstop $250B of financing for OpenAI data centres, plus a separate discussion for OpenAI to buy up to $350B of chips. Neither is confirmed as a signed agreement. If it holds up, the capex argument changes shape. The debate was about who spends how much. Once the chip seller also underwrites the buyer's funding, where that money comes from becomes a second question. That was not on the list a few months ago.

What settles it

On earnings Jukan has the better argument. S&P 500 net margins head for a tenth straight rise at 15.7%, so the cycle peak has not shown up yet. Two things would settle it. If that 65% allocation rolls over, this drawdown was about capacity to buy rather than appetite to sell. If the Nvidia backstop becomes a signed deal, the premise that nothing new appeared loses a leg. The question underneath is single. Is this selling about a new negative, or about a market out of room to buy? Sentiment can reverse. A positioning ceiling means the money for a rebound is not there.

In three lines
  • Jukan wrote on July 27 that nothing weighing on the market is a new negative, and that people simply want to take profits.
  • He has a case: cycle peak, capex and China have sat on that list for months, while S&P 500 net margins head for a tenth straight rise.
  • But another number landed the same day. Equity allocation hit a record 65%, so there may be no room left to add rather than an urge to sell.
