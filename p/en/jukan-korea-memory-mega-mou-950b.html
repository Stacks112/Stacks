SK hynix's Deal Was Announced as $750 Billion: Jensen Huang Himself Said $500 Billion

Kim Yong-beom, South Korea’s Presidential Chief of Staff for Policy: “SK has also agreed to pursue long-term cooperation with global tech giants, including Nvidia, to supply advanced memory semiconductors worth $750 billion (KRW 1,085 trillion) over the next five years.”

July 25, 2026

SK hynix agreed to supply $750 billion (about 1,085 trillion won) worth of advanced memory chips to Nvidia and other US tech giants over the next five years. The same day, Samsung Electronics signed a separate memorandum of understanding with Broadcom covering $200 billion (about 290 trillion won) in memory supply and foundry cooperation, also over five years. Both were announced by Kim Yong-beom, South Korea's presidential chief of staff for policy, during President Lee Jae-myung's Silicon Valley trip, relayed by chip-industry commentator Jukan on X.

Samsung's $200 billion with Broadcom checks out

Samsung's own newsroom release confirms this almost word for word: "more than $200 billion across memory and foundry" over five years. Fortune independently confirmed the same figure through its own reporting.

Samsung Electronics and Broadcom Expand Strategic Collaboration Across Memory and Foundry Technologies (Samsung Newsroom, 2026-07-25)
출처: news.samsung.com

But SK hynix's $750 billion doesn't match Jensen Huang's own number

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said, the same day, in his own words: "SK Group and I are announcing today that our two companies will enter into business partnerships that will represent over $500 billion of business together." That $500 billion spans not just SK hynix's memory supply but SK Telecom's AI infrastructure, an investment in Naver, Hyundai robotics, LG, and an AI Frontier Lab, the whole SK Group conglomerate. The government's $750 billion figure appears to layer on additional pieces, including a separate SK hynix-Microsoft supply deal, under the broader "SK-Nvidia and others" framing. The two numbers are counting different things, so reading $750 billion as "the memory contract with Nvidia alone" overstates what's actually there.

Nvidia, SK to announce $500 billion partnership, Jensen Huang says (Korea Herald, 2026-07-25)

Even 'MOU or not' is contested

SK hynix's own newsroom release calls this a "Letter of Intent," meaning a framework requiring further negotiation, not a binding contract. But policy chief Kim Yong-beom pushed back directly on that framing in a Seoul Economic Daily interview, saying "this isn't simply a corporate MOU, expect quite large, meaningful numbers from investment attraction, strategic alliances, and long-term contracts." The people announcing the deal and the people drafting it are describing it differently.

The products involved are reasonably specific. The SK hynix-Nvidia side names HBM4 for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform; the Samsung-Broadcom side involves 2-nanometer-class foundry work and HBM4E. Around the same time, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei separately said the company had signed its own supply agreements with both Samsung and SK hynix, though no amount, product or duration was disclosed.

So what's the thing to actually watch

The question comes down to one thing: are these big announcements a real signal of order growth, or headline figures stretched as wide as possible to fit a presidential trip. If Samsung and SK hynix confirm real order backlogs or contract progress with hard numbers in this week's Q2 earnings, that leans toward the former. If these are still sitting at "letter of intent" stage five years from now, this $950 billion figure will be the part of the announcement worth revisiting first.

In three lines
  • The Korean government announced $750B (SK hynix) plus $200B (Samsung) memory deals, $950B combined.
  • Samsung's $200B deal with Broadcom is confirmed by Samsung's own newsroom release.
  • But Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang himself put the SK Group deal at $500 billion, the two figures appear to count different things.

What happened next

MissIn its Q2 2026 earnings conference call (7/28-29), SK hynix disclosed that it had signed long-term supply agreements (LTAs) with Nvidia and others, but did not disclose the contract value or order backlog, saying 'terms of individual customer contracts are difficult to disclose.' The deal sizes actually reported also split into two separate threads, the SK Group-Nvidia $500 billion AI partnership and a five-year, $750 billion SK-Big Tech semiconductor cooperation, an order of magnitude different from the '$75 billion vs $50 billion' dispute the prediction assumed, so a $75 billion order-backlog reflection was not confirmed.
