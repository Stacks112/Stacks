July 29: the SK Hynix ADR premium finally gets a closing mechanism

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

The same SK Hynix share costs 49% more in New York than in Seoul. The reason is simple: the channel for converting Seoul shares into US shares (ADRs) has been shut, so nobody could sell the expensive one and buy the cheap one.

Why was the channel shut

On July 15, Korea's securities depository confirmed it will open that channel after July 29. The blockage ran in exactly one direction. Newstomato reported that a retail holder of Seoul shares is electronically barred from turning those shares into ADRs through an MTS or HTS app. Only institutions able to handle the depository steps and the FX filing used the channel, and that is why the premium set hard.

How far can the gap close

What happens next is predictable: buy cheap in Seoul, convert, sell dear in New York. That flow pushes Seoul up and New York down until the gap shrinks. TSMC, which has a similar setup, trades at a steady 16-17% gap, so that's roughly the landing zone. The Newstomato piece puts finer numbers on TSMC. Its premium has held at 19.1% since 2024 and averaged 17.5% through 2026. Open the channel but leave the friction in place, and the gap does not go to zero.

So why not overnight

There are brakes, though: only a small slice of shares can convert for now, and regulators will loosen it slowly while watching the currency. The brakes have names. Financial Post reports that SK Hynix capped ADR issuance at 2.5%, and that turning ordinary shares into ADRs requires the issuer to run a regulatory filing that takes several weeks or more. Opening the application window and actually moving the shares are two different things. The premium that article measured was 32-33%. The direction was already set toward closing.

Meru's one-liner: good news if you hold in Seoul. Just not today.
