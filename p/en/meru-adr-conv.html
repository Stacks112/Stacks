July 29: the SK Hynix ADR premium finally gets a closing mechanism

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

The same SK Hynix share costs 49% more in New York than in Seoul. The reason is simple: the channel for converting Seoul shares into US shares (ADRs) has been shut, so nobody could sell the expensive one and buy the cheap one.

Why was the channel shut

On July 15, Korea's securities depository confirmed it will open that channel after July 29. The blockage ran in exactly one direction. Bloomingbit, the Korea Economic Daily group's English outlet, reported that turning Seoul shares into ADRs means filing a separate application with your broker and clearing foreign-exchange procedures, not the instant order an MTS or HTS app gives you for overseas stocks. Only institutions able to handle the depository steps and the FX filing used the channel, and that is why the premium set hard.

How far can the gap close

What happens next is predictable: buy cheap in Seoul, convert, sell dear in New York. That flow pushes Seoul up and New York down until the gap shrinks. TSMC, which has a similar setup, trades at a steady 16-17% gap, so that's roughly the landing zone. The same piece puts finer numbers on TSMC. Citing Shinhan Securities analyst Noh Dong-gil, it says the premium has held at 19.1% since 2024 and averaged 17.5% through 2026. Open the channel but leave the friction in place, and the gap does not go to zero.

So why not overnight

There are brakes, though: only a small slice of shares can convert for now, and regulators will loosen it slowly while watching the currency. The brakes have names. Bloomberg reports that SK Hynix capped the Seoul-listed shares convertible into ADRs at 2.5% of shares outstanding, and that the ADR books stayed closed for issuance and cancellation until July 29 because newly issued Korean shares cannot move until they are listed on the Korea Exchange. Opening the application window and actually moving the shares are two different things. The premium that article measured was 33% as of Wednesday, after touching 51% at its widest. That is a different snapshot from the 49% at the top of this card, but the direction was already set toward closing.

Meru's one-liner: good news if you hold in Seoul. Just not today.

Why it matters · The 49% premium was a gauge of blocked demand. From July 29 it becomes a trade with a mechanism, and the convergence flow buys Seoul.

Worth asking · After July 29, how far does the SK Hynix ADR premium compress?

What happened next

MissSK hynix's common-share/ADR mutual conversion opened on July 29-30, but the volume immediately eligible for conversion was only 2.5% of shares already issued, effectively limiting arbitrage. Right after the conversion opened (7/30) the premium, far from narrowing, jumped to 60.0% (widening from 36% on 7/13), and even the drop to 20.7% on 7/31 was assessed as being driven not by the conversion mechanism but by a rebound in the Korean stock market itself hitting its daily limit (+29.95%). So the prediction's core mechanism, that conversion would open an arbitrage channel, was not confirmed.
