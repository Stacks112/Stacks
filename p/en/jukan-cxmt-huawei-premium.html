China's CXMT now charges more than Samsung for memory, and pulled a Huawei-linked vendor's engineers in a pricing standoff

CXMT is now powerful enough to throw its weight around even with Huawei. When Huawei refused to accept the price increase, CXMT reportedly kicked Huawei’s engineers out of the fab.

July 24, 2026

Changxin Memory Technologies (CXMT), the Chinese state-linked memory chipmaker, has grown powerful enough to push back even on partners as large as Huawei, semiconductor analyst Jukan reports, citing Reuters.

Charging more than the industry's old price-setter

Reuters reports that CXMT has been charging more than Samsung Electronics's roughly $1,240 per unit for comparable 64GB DDR5 server memory modules in recent weeks, a figure sourced to people familiar with the matter rather than a public price list.

A pricing standoff pulled engineers out of the fab

When CXMT pushed for a price increase, engineers from SiCarrier, a Huawei-linked equipment and maintenance vendor working inside CXMT's Hefei facility, were reportedly told to leave in June after tension over the demand. Some coverage frames this loosely as CXMT versus Huawei directly, but the engineers involved worked for SiCarrier, not Huawei itself.

The US market is the goal, but capacity isn't there yet

Three sources tell Reuters that CXMT wants to eventually sell into the US market too, though for now its production is stretched just meeting Chinese demand. New fabs in Hefei and Shanghai could more than double its capacity to over 600,000 wafers a month by 2027, which is when a US push would become more realistic. CXMT makes DRAM, the everyday memory chip used in phones, PCs, and servers, and has been one of the forces squeezing global memory supply this year, a dynamic that has helped lift prices for Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron alike.

Is this one supplier's moment, or a real shift in leverage

That CXMT can now out-price Samsung and make a Huawei-linked vendor's engineers leave over a pricing dispute suggests the balance of power in memory chips may be moving faster than the industry's shared story, that scarcity lifts everyone's margins together, would suggest.

In three lines
  • CXMT now charges more than Samsung's roughly $1,240 per unit for comparable 64GB DDR5 server memory, Reuters reports.
  • When a Huawei-linked vendor's engineers pushed back on a CXMT price hike, CXMT had them leave its Hefei site.
  • CXMT wants into the US market eventually, but for now its new capacity barely keeps up with demand at home.
