Chip stocks fell together. Why did Kioxia fall harder than the rest?

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗ PAYWALLED · Original requires a subscription

Kioxia shares hit their daily limit down again on July 28, sinking to 44,550 yen. That's an 18% drop from the previous close, and over 60% below the peak the stock hit just a month earlier. The same day, Samsung Electronics fell 13.4%, SK hynix fell 14.7%, and the Kospi dropped more than 11% intraday, triggering Korea's eighth circuit breaker this year.

Kospi plunges 11% as Kioxia, Samsung, SK hynix tumble (TradingKey, 2026-07-28)
출처: tradingkey.com

Why did chip stocks fall together

The market points to two reasons. Doubts grew that the AI memory rally had run too hot, and reports that China's CXMT is accelerating DRAM capacity expansion revived worries about Samsung and SK hynix losing market share. South Korea's regulator is reportedly weighing tighter rules on retail investors piling into leveraged ETFs.

But why did Kioxia fall harder than the rest

That's the whole sector's story. But Kioxia's drop outpaced Samsung's and SK hynix's for a reason specific to the company. Kioxia is a NAND flash memory maker spun off from Toshiba. NAND is the chip that holds the photos on your phone and the files on your laptop, keeping them even with the power off.

On July 16, a jury at the US District Court for the Western District of Texas found Kioxia infringed a patent held by satellite communications company Viasat, and ordered it to pay about $228 million. The patent covered error-correction tech that cuts power use and extends NAND's lifespan and reliability. Kioxia called the verdict "completely unacceptable" and said it will appeal.

Kioxia ordered to pay $228 million in Viasat patent verdict (BigGo Finance, 2026-07-28)
출처: finance.biggo.com

The day after the ruling, on the 17th, Kioxia shares fell 16.1%, already near a limit down at 52,110 yen. This is the same stock's second sharp fall within ten days.

But aren't the earnings supposed to be good

Here's the paradox: Kioxia reports earnings on the 30th, and the guidance doesn't look weak. Last quarter's revenue crossed 1 trillion yen for the first time, with a 66% gross margin and free cash flow above 241 billion yen. This quarter's guidance calls for revenue of 1.75 trillion yen, up 74.5% from the prior quarter, operating profit of 1.3 trillion yen, and net income of 870 billion yen.

The sector-correction read

Chip stocks across the board are correcting, and Kioxia is simply caught in it. Once earnings hold up, it should recover along with the rest.

VS
The company-specific read

Kioxia fell harder than peers in the same sector. A patent payout and an appeal fight, unrelated to its actual business, sit on this one company alone.

Both readings look at the same drop, differing only on whether it's sector weather or an extra weight unique to Kioxia.

So what settles it

The question comes down to one thing. Is this just a sector-wide correction, or has Kioxia's own lawsuit risk been added on top? If the 30th's earnings come in as guided and Samsung and SK hynix bounce back together, the sector read holds. If Kioxia lags the recovery, the patent risk is still weighing on it. Whichever comes first, an appeal ruling or a settlement, will be the first real signal of how heavy that patent burden is.

In three lines
  • Kioxia hit limit-down again on July 28, sinking to 44,550 yen, over 60% below its peak from a month earlier. Samsung fell 13.4%, SK hynix 14.7%, and the Kospi dropped over 11% intraday.
  • The surface story is a sector-wide cooldown in AI memory stocks, compounded by fears over China's CXMT ramping up capacity.
  • But Kioxia fell hardest of all, because on July 16 a US court alone ordered it to pay $228 million for patent infringement.

What happened next

MissKioxia's July 31 earnings (revenue ¥1.7671 trillion, above the ¥1.75 trillion guidance) and two instances in July (7/9, 7/29-30) of Kioxia rebounding together with Samsung Electronics and SK hynix were both confirmed, but no news emerged of a Viasat patent lawsuit appeal ruling or settlement, and the stock reaction on the earnings-announcement day itself was inconsistent across outlets (+18% to -11%). As of July 30, Kioxia's drawdown from its peak (-63.66%) was still larger than SK hynix's (-54.68%), so the original question's premise cannot be said to have been clearly resolved.
