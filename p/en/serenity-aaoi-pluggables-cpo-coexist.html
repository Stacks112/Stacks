The 'Photonics CEO' Who Said Pluggables Have 10 Years Left Runs a Laser Supplier, Not AAOI

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

The debate over whether co-packaged optics (CPO) will soon make traditional pluggable optical transceivers obsolete in AI data centers got a reality check this week. Analyst Serenity relayed comments from a photonics-industry CEO, made on a podcast, arguing pluggables have roughly another decade of relevant commercial life left, not months.

Who is this CEO, actually

It turns out the comment didn't come from an AAOI executive at all. It came from Vickram Vathulya, CEO of Sivers Semiconductors, a Swedish company that makes continuous-wave (CW) lasers. The podcast was Laser Focus World's "Expert Insights: Photonics & AI," aired July 21, with Oriole CEO James Regan also on the call. Sivers sells laser components into both CPO and pluggable supply chains, worth noting since it has little to lose either way this debate resolves. The exchange started with a reader asking whether Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI) is in trouble as CPO gains traction. Serenity's answer: not exactly. AAOI is also developing CPO technology, just further behind on commercializing it than some peers.

The claim mostly checks out anyway

Independent of Vathulya's framing, real industry data leans toward pushing back on the idea that CPO is about to take over. SemiAnalysis, in a June report, pushed full CPO mass production out to 2028-2029, citing yield math: a single optical engine might yield 95%, but with 32 engines per switch, system-level yield drops to around 19%.

Lumentum CEO Michael Hurlston has said the company is "undershipping our customers' demand by somewhere around 30%." Nvidia locked in $2 billion each with Lumentum and Coherent in March, $4 billion total, to secure priority laser supply.

AAOI itself confirmed on its May earnings call that an industry-wide indium phosphide laser shortage is likely to persist, and said it plans to expand its own laser capacity by 350% by 2027.

A third option: near-package optics

The CEO's bigger point, as relayed, is that the industry is heading toward several coexisting architectures rather than one clean winner. Besides pluggables and CPO, there's a newer category called near-package optics (NPO), which sits closer to the chip than a pluggable without going as far as full co-packaging. Component makers including Credo, Marvell, Coherent and Lumentum are confirmed to be building in this space. It's less clear whether Nvidia or Broadcom use the NPO label in their own switch roadmaps, which suggests NPO is more a component-supplier category riding alongside the CPO platforms than a named hyperscaler initiative.

So what's the thing to actually watch

The question comes down to one thing: if pluggables, CPO and NPO all coexist for a decade, which optical suppliers actually win. What holds back all three paths isn't which architecture wins, it's laser and raw-material supply. The longer CPO slips, the longer pluggables stay relevant, and the bigger NPO's window gets too. This is secondhand color from a podcast relayed through a tweet, but its direction lines up with what the industry data actually shows.
