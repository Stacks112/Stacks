The S&P 500's Iran War Selloff Was Actually Three Different Days, and Iran Wasn't the Main Reason for Most of Them

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

The S&P 500 erased its intraday gains and closed lower as the Iran war escalated, with Intel's rally reversing the same day and the correlation between AI infrastructure stocks and the semiconductor index collapsing to a 4.5-year low, Kobeissi reports.

Which day is this actually describing

Lining up the underlying reporting by date shows this isn't one day, it's three different ones compressed together. On Thursday, July 23, the S&P 500 fell 0.9%. Per Yahoo Finance, that was driven by a Houthi attack on Red Sea tankers pushing Brent toward $97-100, capex fears from Wednesday's Alphabet and Tesla earnings, and a bond-yield spike. Intel hadn't reported yet. On Friday, July 24, the S&P 500 closed little changed, per TheStreet. That's the day Intel's rally actually reversed, and both TheStreet and The Detroit News point mainly to Tesla's 142% capex jump and chip-sector profit-taking, with Iran and tariffs cited only as secondary factors. Oil actually fell 3.5% that day. It's only on Monday, July 27, that the phrase Kobeissi uses, gains erased and chips falling, actually appears in the record, but Bloomberg and Fortune reported that day's Iran conflict had paused and oil was dropping, with the chip selloff (Nvidia -4.5%, Intel, AMD and Micron down 6-10%) attributed to fading confidence in the AI chip trade itself.

Does the SOX correlation statistic hold up

No independent report of a 30-day correlation between AI infrastructure stocks and the SOX collapsing toward zero, the lowest in 4.5 years, was found. This appears to be Kobeissi's own proprietary analysis. The day 145 count is also slightly off. Counting from the February 28 start, July 24 lands closer to day 147.

US stocks catch a bid as oil tumbles with Iran conflict pause (Bloomberg, 2026-07-27) ↗

So what's the thing to actually watch

Compressing a week's worth of separate events into the day Iran shook the market makes for a cleaner story, but the actual sequence was an oil shock, company-specific worries, and profit-taking, playing out across three separate days. Iran risk was real background noise all week. But before crediting it for any one day's specific drop, it's worth checking what that day's coverage actually cited.

In three lines
  • Kobeissi reports the S&P 500 erased its gains and tech stocks fell as the Iran war escalated.
  • That reads like a single day the war shook the entire market.
  • But the actual reporting spans three different days, a Thursday oil spike, a Friday driven by Intel and capex fears, and a Monday chip selloff, and Iran was the primary cited cause on only one of them.

Why it matters · Kobeissi's single-day S&P 500 Iran selloff was actually three different days, an oil shock, Intel and capex worries, and chip profit-taking. It's a reminder to check what each day's coverage actually cited before crediting Iran for a specific drop.

Worth asking · The single day blamed on the Iran selloff was actually three separate days, and Iran was cited as the main reason on only one of them. Does this kind of compressed framing overstate how dangerous the market looked, or does the bigger picture, Iran risk weighing on the whole week, still hold regardless?
