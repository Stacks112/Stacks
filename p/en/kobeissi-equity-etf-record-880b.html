US Equity ETFs Just Pulled In a Record $880 Billion. Is That Really Conviction, or Just the Machine Running?

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

US equity ETFs have pulled in $880 billion this year, the second-largest annual haul on record after 2025, and on pace to top $1.4 trillion by year-end, according to The Kobeissi Letter.

Does the 2025 record check out

TD Securities' 2025 ETF Recap puts 2025 equity ETF inflows at $923 billion, matching Kobeissi's approximately $920 billion almost exactly. ETF.com separately reported total 2025 ETF inflows across all asset classes at $1.49 trillion, meaning equity funds made up roughly 62% of that total.

Who confirms this year's $880 billion

No independent source published an exact $880 billion figure as of July 24. It appears to be Kobeissi's own running tally. The direction is plausible, though. State Street reported $441 billion into US equity ETFs specifically through June 30, 2026, with combined equity and bond inflows near $1 trillion. ETF Trends put combined H1 2026 flows at $995 billion. Both SSGA and ETF Trends project roughly $2.3 trillion in total 2026 ETF flows. Applying 2025's equity share of about 62% to that gets you to roughly $1.4 trillion, matching Kobeissi's year-end estimate.

That piece shows retail-heavy S&P 500 ETFs kept posting positive monthly inflows through a 26% market decline in February to April 2025, a similar pattern to what's being described now.

What kind of money is this

None of the flow reports break down how much of this is discretionary conviction buying versus money that arrives on autopilot. Payroll-deducted 401(k) contributions, robo-advisor rebalancing, and model-portfolio programs flow in on a fixed schedule regardless of what the market is doing. That money doesn't pull back during a selloff. Whether record inflows mean unshaken conviction or just business-as-usual mechanical buying is hard to say without knowing that split.

So what's the thing to actually watch

The 2025 figure checks out independently. This year's number and the year-end projection rest on taking Kobeissi's own tally at face value. What actually matters is how much of this money is discretionary versus mechanical, and whether that ratio holds if the Iran war drags on longer.

In three lines
  • The Kobeissi Letter reports US equity ETFs have pulled in a record $880 billion this year, on pace to beat 2025.
  • That reads like investor conviction holding firm even as the Iran war rattles markets.
  • But while 2025's ~$920B record checks out against independent data, this year's exact $880B figure is Kobeissi's own tally, and a large share of any such inflow is mechanical retirement-account money, not discretionary conviction.
