SpaceX's bonds crater weeks after the IPO: the credit market's warning to Musk

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Elon Musk's SpaceX went public on the Nasdaq in June (ticker SPCX), raising $85.7 billion from the listing alone. Right after, it sold another $25 billion in corporate bonds, five tranches maturing from 2031 to 2056.

Wasn't the sale a hit?

Demand was fierce: $89 billion of orders chased the $25 billion on offer, letting SpaceX shave 25 basis points off the spread (the 2056 bond priced at 6.65% instead of a planned 6.90%) and pay less interest. A textbook success, until the bonds hit the secondary market.

SpaceX draws $89 billion in demand for its debut bond sale, one of the largest US offerings this year (The Next Web, 2026-06-23)
출처: thenextweb.com

The $89 billion order book checks out, and so does the 25 basis point move on the 2056 tranche, which landed at 175 basis points over Treasuries. The article counts those 25 basis points as a spread over Treasuries rather than as the 6.90% to 6.65% yield the card describes. The proceeds were earmarked to repay a $20 billion bridge loan, itself raised to clear about $17.5 billion of high-cost debt at X and xAI. The xAI worry further down the card was already written into how the money was raised.

Why did prices slide so fast?

Within days, the yield on that 2056 bond jumped toward 7.3%, a 0.72-point rise driven partly by SpaceX's own risk (+0.56pt) and partly by higher US Treasury yields (+0.16pt). On a 30-year bond, a 0.72-point yield rise times a roughly 13-year duration works out to about a 9.3% price drop: a $100 bond became about $91 in a matter of days. To bond investors, a 9.3% loss in three weeks stings more than a stock hitting its daily limit-down.

The Bond Market Is Sending a Terrifying Message to SpaceX, but Is Anyone Listening? (The Motley Fool, 2026-07-10)
출처: fool.com

By the July 7 close the 2056 note had fallen from its 99.45 issue price to 94.52, and all five tranches were trading below issue. The card measures three weeks while this measures about two weeks from pricing, which is why the drop reads closer to 5% there. The direction is the same. All five tranches sinking shows the selling was not confined to one maturity.

Who was selling?

The culprit was hedge funds that parked short-term trading money betting the spread would tighten after the IPO hype, then dumped within days, fast money, not the pension-and-insurance kind that holds. Stocks run on dreams; bonds run on numbers. And the bond market is worried that heavy spending and xAI's mounting losses keep cash flow negative through 2029. Musk borrowed well, but bruised bondholders may hold a grudge.

Why it matters · The bond market is pricing SpaceX's cash-flow reality while the stock prices its dream, and the gap between them is an early warning about how the next round of financing will go.

Worth asking · Bonds flash caution, the stock still dreams, which one is right about SpaceX over the next few years?
