JGB DEMAND BACK
The Kobeissi Letter (@KobeissiLetter) · 2026-07-23 · original: EN

Japanese insurers just made their biggest bond purchase in three years. Is the buyer finally back?

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Japanese insurance companies bought $3.9 billion of long-term Japanese government bonds (JGBs, those maturing in 10 years or more) in June, their largest monthly purchase in three years, market commentary account The Kobeissi Letter reports.

A sharp reversal from May

That's a reversal from May, when insurers were net sellers to the tune of $1.2 billion, and it marks only the second month in the past 11 that insurers have been net buyers of long-term JGBs, according to Kobeissi's figures. Bloomberg independently ran a headline confirming Japanese insurers bought the most super-long bonds in three years in June, corroborating the broad direction of the claim, though the underlying article is paywalled and the specific $3.9 billion and $1.2 billion figures trace to Kobeissi's own data rather than something this outlet could verify line by line.

Japan Insurers Buy Most Super-Long Bonds in Three Years in June (Bloomberg, 2026-07-21) ↗

Why insurers matter so much here

Insurers are one of the largest domestic holders of long-dated Japanese debt, so their return as buyers could meaningfully support a bond market that's struggled with weak demand this year, a dynamic that has pushed Japanese yields higher and drawn comparisons to rising Treasury yields elsewhere.

Tokyo is also pulling other levers

The shift comes as Japanese authorities try to engineer more domestic demand for their own bonds. Japan's finance ministry has floated letting individuals hold JGBs inside a tax-free investment account, and separately, Goldman Sachs estimates Japan's public pension fund GPIF has roughly $75 billion of room to raise its allocation to Japanese assets.

One good month, or a real turn

This is the second buying month in eleven, not yet a confirmed trend, and it followed direct government encouragement rather than a clearly market-driven shift in appetite. Whether Japanese institutions are actually coming back to their own bond market, or this was a one-off month that reverses once the policy nudges fade, will show up in whether insurers post a third straight month of buying when July's data lands.

In three lines
  • Japanese insurers bought $3.9 billion of long-term JGBs in June, their largest monthly purchase in three years, Kobeissi Letter data show.
  • Bloomberg independently confirms the largest-in-three-years framing, though the exact dollar figures trace to Kobeissi's own numbers.
  • Japan is also nudging individuals and pension funds toward more JGBs, including a proposal to hold them in tax-free accounts.
