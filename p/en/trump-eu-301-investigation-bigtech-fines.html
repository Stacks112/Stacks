Trump's EU Tariff Threat Cited Four Big Tech Fines. Most of the Numbers Don't Check Out

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

President Trump announced a Section 301 investigation into what he calls the EU robbing American companies through antitrust fines, citing $15 billion against Apple, $3 billion against Meta, $2.5 billion against Amazon, and a new $1 billion against Google, for a claimed cumulative total above $18 billion, and threatened a substantial tariff in response.

Starting with Apple's $15 billion

The actual EU antitrust and Digital Markets Act fine against Apple in 2025-2026 was 500 million euros, about $571 million, issued in April 2025 over App Store anti-steering restrictions. The $15 billion figure is actually the 2016 EU ruling ordering Ireland to recover roughly 13.1 billion euros plus interest in back taxes from Apple. That ruling was upheld by the EU's top court in September 2024, and Ireland withdrew about 14.25 billion euros, roughly $15.5 billion, from escrow in 2025. It's a tax case from 2016, not an antitrust fine, and 9to5Mac fact-checked this exact conflation in Trump's own post.

What about Meta and Amazon

Meta's actual DMA fine was 200 million euros, about $220 million, issued in April 2025 over its pay or consent ad model. No fine matching $3 billion was found, roughly a 15x overstatement. No matching EU antitrust or DMA fine against Amazon was found at all. Amazon's actual live EU regulatory issue is a preliminary gatekeeper designation for AWS cloud services from June 2026, not a fine.

EU fines Meta and Apple under Digital Markets Act (NPR, 2025-04-23) ↗

Did Google really just get fined $1 billion

That part checks out. The EU fined Google 890 million euros, about $1 billion, on July 23 over Search and Play Store DMA breaches. But cumulative above $18 billion doesn't hold up precisely. Confirmed prior Google EU fines add up to roughly $13-15 billion: the 2017 Shopping case (2.42B euros), 2018 Android case (4.1-4.34B euros, upheld on appeal July 2, 2026), 2019 AdSense case (1.49B euros), and a September 2025 adtech fine (roughly $3.45B). That doesn't clearly clear $18 billion.

So what's the thing to actually watch

Section 301 really is the same legal tool Trump used to launch tariffs against China. But he's also threatened the EU with 50% and 100% tariffs before without following through. Three of the four dollar figures behind this threat don't check out or are inflated, and only Google's new fine is accurate. Whether that shaky foundation still ends in real tariffs, or another walked-back threat, is a separate question worth watching regardless.

In three lines
  • Trump opened a Section 301 probe over EU fines on Apple, Meta, Amazon and Google and threatened tariffs.
  • That sounds like all four Big Tech companies just took major antitrust hits.
  • But Apple's $15B is actually a 2016 tax case, not a 2025-2026 antitrust fine, Meta's $3B is inflated roughly 15x over the real fine, Amazon's $2.5B fine isn't found anywhere, and only Google's new $1B is confirmed.

Why it matters · Of the four EU fines Trump cited, Apple's conflates an unrelated 2016 tax case, Meta's is inflated 15x, Amazon's is unverifiable, and only Google's checks out. How shaky that foundation is matters for gauging whether this actually turns into tariffs.

Worth asking · Three of the four fine figures behind this were inflated or unverifiable, yet the 301 investigation is real and underway. Do you think shaky evidence like this still ends in actual tariffs, or does it fizzle out like Trump's past EU tariff threats?
