Japan's economy grew again in the second quarter, because imports fell

Japan’s Q2 GDP Growth Misses Expectations At an annualized rate of 1.1%, Japan's second-quarter GDP growth fell short of both the prior quarter’s 2.1% and the 2.0% consensus forecast. The policy implications of this miss are more consequential given the domestic measures required…

August 17, 2026

Japan's real GDP rose 0.3 percent on the quarter, which annualizes to 1.1 percent. That fell short of the 2.0 percent the market expected, and the components that produced it were not the ones expected either.

Japan Q2 growth misses forecasts on weaker spending, investment (Reuters, 2026-08-16)
출처: ca.investing.com

Domestic demand contributed minus 0.2 percentage points to growth. Private consumption did not rise for the first time in eight quarters, and business investment fell 1.2 percent. The headline still came out positive because net exports added 0.5 percentage points. Net exports are exports minus imports, so the figure widens when imports shrink. This quarter exports rose 0.5 percent and imports fell 1.5 percent. Put simply, the amount households and firms stopped buying from abroad showed up as growth.

The 2.1 percent the original post used as its benchmark is the first preliminary reading. That number was revised twice and stood at 1.8 to 1.9 percent by the time of this release, so the actual deceleration is gentler than the comparison suggests.

Checked 2026-08-17 · Cabinet Office ESRI first preliminary estimate for April to June 2026, released 2026-08-17; the revised prior quarter is as reported

The same day, the 10-year government bond yield climbed to 2.9 percent. That is the highest since September 1996. The 30-year sits near 4.1 percent and the 2-year at 1.7 percent. Why that matters is written in the budget. The fiscal 2026 budget totals 122.3 trillion yen, of which debt servicing takes 31.3 trillion yen, or 25.6 percent. And the budget was drawn up assuming a 3 percent bond interest rate.

What the intervention left, and what it erased

On August 1, Japan's Ministry of Finance and the Bank of Japan moved with the US Treasury to buy yen. It was the first time the two countries acted together since 2011. The yen briefly strengthened to 155.20 per dollar.

Japan and US confirm rare joint intervention to prop up yen (Al Jazeera, 2026-08-03)
출처: aljazeera.com

Two weeks later the rate is back around 159 per dollar. The intervention slowed the move rather than turned it, and that erosion is what the original post is pointing at. What is left is the interest rate.

Two readings of the same release

El-Erian's reading

A miss of this size raises the weight of the domestic measures needed to support the yen, which is what makes the policy implication larger.

VS
Japanese economists' reading

The consumption weakness owes much to one-offs such as pharmaceutical asset sales and changes to education policy and tobacco tax. The September timing does not move.

The split is over whether this quarter is structure or noise. Kazutaka Maeda of Meiji Yasuda Research Institute does not think the data point to weakness ahead or affect the timing of the Bank of Japan's next hike. Masato Koike of Sompo Institute Plus reads the soft consumption as a case for tightening at a more gradual pace, a disagreement about speed rather than direction.

September 17 and 18, when the Bank of Japan sits again

The policy rate has been 1.0 percent since the hike on June 16, the highest since 1995. The next Monetary Policy Meeting runs on September 17 and 18, and prediction markets and rate futures put the odds of a hike between 75 and 81 percent. So what this release actually changed will be visible that day. A hike in September means the market read the miss as noise; a pass means the minus 0.2 percentage points from domestic demand entered the Bank's arithmetic. Either way, a budget built on a 3 percent bond rate keeps diverging from a 30-year yield above 4 percent.

In three lines
  • Japan's real GDP grew at an annualized 1.1 percent in the second quarter, below the 2.0 percent consensus.
  • On the face of it, growth has halved, which is why people are asking whether the September rate rise still holds.
  • But what carried the positive print was net exports, and net exports widened not because exports rose but because imports fell 1.5 percent.
