On the morning of August 17, the joint exercise started exactly as scheduled

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

President Trump wrote on social media early on August 17 that he had instructed his war secretary to substantially reduce joint military exercises with South Korea. That same morning, the exercise began on schedule.

Trump orders Pentagon to 'substantially' reduce S. Korea-US military exercises (Korea Herald, 2026-08-17)
출처: koreaherald.com

The drill is Ulchi Freedom Shield, running eleven days from August 17 to 27 with about 18,000 South Korean troops taking part. Seoul's defense ministry said the exercise is proceeding on its originally planned schedule and scale, and that it had received no request from Washington to cut it back.

Neither earlier episode made the exercises disappear; both changed their size and their name. If this one sits in the same place, the result is a smaller next edition rather than a cancellation.

Retrieved 2026-08-17 · Defense News and AP, 2018-06-18 · The Diplomat, 2019-03-03 · Kyunghyang Shinmun, 2026-08-07

This form of announcement is not new. After the 2018 Singapore summit the Pentagon suspended all planning for that August's drill, and in 2019 Key Resolve and Foal Eagle were swapped out for a slimmed-down exercise called Dong Maeng. Neither ended the exercises; both changed their scale and their label.

The saving was $14 million

Trump wrote that the United States pays much of the cost of these exercises. When the 2018 drill was called off, the Pentagon put the American saving at $14 million. Against a defense budget then running near $700 billion, the share ends below the decimal point. The real cost-sharing sits somewhere else. South Korea's contribution to stationing US forces is 1.5192 trillion won for 2026, 8.3% above the 1.4028 trillion won paid in 2025. From 2027 through 2030 it is indexed to consumer inflation with the annual rise capped at 5%.

The exercise is not a ceremony; it is an input to a verification procedure. Shrink it and there is less evidence to verify with, which makes an earlier target year harder to defend.

Retrieved 2026-08-17

Wartime operational control is the authority to command South Korean forces in a war, and it currently rests with the commander of the combined forces. One condition for handing that authority to South Korea is a full operational capability verification confirming that its military can run combined operations. That verification happens only inside a joint exercise. Yet the two sides had already agreed that this year's Ulchi Freedom Shield would run without South Korea in the lead, with verification targeted for completion by the November alliance meeting. An order to shrink the exercise lands on top of that schedule.

The August 18 open is the first answer

Korean markets were shut on August 17 for a substitute Liberation Day holiday, so there is no price yet for this announcement. On August 14, the last session before it, the KOSPI had risen for five straight days and was approaching 7,000. The test is not the name of the drill but November. If the alliance meeting delivers both a completed capability verification and a target year for the command transfer, this order was a negotiating line. If the verification slips to a later date, the reduction reached the procedure itself, and cost-sharing talks and troop numbers come onto the same page. That is also where Korean defense shares split on this news. A thinner alliance reads as a bigger domestic procurement budget, but an actual troop cut puts regional risk back into the price.

In three lines
  • An order to shrink the drill and the start of that drill came hours apart on the same August 17.
  • On the surface that reads as exercises being stopped. Yet Seoul's defense ministry said the schedule and scale are unchanged and that no reduction request had arrived from Washington.
  • Where the order actually lands is not the exercise but the wartime command transfer process due for verification at November's alliance meeting.
