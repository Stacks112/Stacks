SK Hynix's Q2 Profit Could Hit ₩100 Trillion, but Analysts Say Much of It Isn't From Chips

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Korea's Meritz Securities estimates SK Hynix's Q2 (April-June 2026) pretax profit at 101.8 trillion won. That would be the first time a KOSPI-listed company's quarterly pretax profit has topped 100 trillion won. More than 40 trillion won of that is non-operating income from disposing investment assets, not from chip sales. Chip analyst Jukan relayed the estimate on July 27. English-language coverage of the same Meritz estimate carries it in dollars, at roughly $69.8 billion pretax and about $27.4 billion of non-operating income.

Where does this much money come from

In 2018, SK Hynix invested a combined 3.9 trillion won through special purpose companies (SPC1 and SPC2) alongside Bain Capital's acquisition of Toshiba Memory, now Kioxia. Now that Bain Capital has sold out its entire Kioxia stake, Meritz estimates SK Hynix's SPC1 holding was also liquidated, with the sale completing in June. An eight-year-old investment got cashed out in one shot.

According to Meritz, the convertible bonds SK Hynix still holds, convertible into roughly a 14% Kioxia stake, were booked at 13.61 trillion won at end-March and are now worth roughly 55 trillion won at Kioxia's current valuation. Add the June SPC1 sale on top of that, and Meritz's model has Q2's non-operating income clearing 40 trillion won.

Why is dividend talk suddenly in the mix

Jukan added one more detail. Meritz reportedly tied the story to SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won's divorce settlement, arguing it raises the odds of a bigger SK Hynix dividend flowing back through SK Square. A court ordered Chey to pay 944 billion won in the settlement. His SK Inc. stake is worth roughly 8 trillion won, and his dividend income last year was about 103.8 billion won. The Korea Herald values the same stake at 8.52 trillion won on the July 24 close, a slightly different count.

The Korea Herald also points to share-backed borrowing as the most likely funding route, with a dividend hike treated as the next option down. That's a cooler read than Meritz connecting it directly to SK Hynix's dividend. Still, the structure itself checks out. SK Square holds SK Hynix shares, so a bigger SK Hynix payout would flow into the group through that stake.

Why the headline number needs a second look

When Bain Capital acquired Toshiba Memory in 2018, the company was up for sale cheap amid an accounting scandal. The money SK Hynix put in back then has multiplied dozens of times over, riding the DRAM supercycle. None of that came from making or selling more memory. A single investment decision from eight years ago has simply grown large enough to reshape one quarter's earnings. To see whether the chip business itself improved, operating profit is the line to check separately.

Reading the headline

A record 101.8 trillion won pretax profit, first on the KOSPI past 100 trillion, gets read as proof the chip business is booming

VS
Looking one layer deeper

The 60.1 trillion won operating profit needs to be checked on its own; the other 40-plus trillion is a one-off from cashing out an eight-year-old stake

What settles this

The question comes down to one thing. Is a 100-trillion-won pretax profit proof of a chip boom, or an illusion built by a one-off stake sale. If operating profit alone lands around 60 trillion won as estimated, the core chip business really is strong, and the non-operating windfall is just stacked on top. If operating profit comes in meaningfully lower than that, this quarter's headline number was effectively a Kioxia disposal result. The dividend story splits the same way. If SK Hynix actually raises its payout by a wide margin, Meritz's read holds. If it keeps its usual level, this detail ends up as speculation that didn't pan out.

In three lines
  • Meritz Securities estimates SK Hynix's Q2 pretax profit at 101.8 trillion won, the first time a KOSPI company has topped 100 trillion won in a quarter.
  • On the surface, that reads as the DRAM supercycle producing a record quarter.
  • But over 40 trillion won of it is a one-off gain from cashing out an eight-year-old Kioxia stake, and Meritz has even tied it to chairman Chey Tae-won's divorce settlement and a possible dividend hike.
