The 'Equipment Lead Times Doubled' Story Traces to a Single Korean Trade Article

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Lead times for chip manufacturing equipment have stretched 1.5 to 2 times in recent months, according to reports. Tracing the origin leads to a July 23 article by Kwon Dong-jun in Electronic Times (Korea).

반도체 장비 납품기간 2배 늘어…제조사 '발등에 불' (Electronic Times, 2026-07-23)
출처: etnews.com

The direction got reconfirmed five days later

DigiTimes reported on July 28 that Samsung and SK hynix are placing orders early to get ahead of lead times that could reach a full year, reconfirming the broader trend.

Samsung, SK Hynix race to secure chipmaking tools before equipment lead times hit one year (DIGITIMES, 2026-07-28)
출처: digitimes.com

But the specific figures sit in one article alone

The claim that the top five, Applied Materials, ASML, Lam Research, Tokyo Electron and KLA, control about 70% of the global market, and that Korean suppliers feeding TSMC and Micron saw lead times stretch from three-four months to six-eight months, both trace only to that single Electronic Times piece. The suppliers are anonymized as "Company A" and "Company B," so they can't be identified.

Confirmed

The direction itself, lead times stretching (reported independently by two outlets)

VS
Unconfirmed

The 70% share figure and the 3-4-month-to-6-8-month range (from one article only)

Is there another explanation

Both articles frame this as demand overwhelming supply from simultaneous global fab investment, not as equipment makers prioritizing certain customers over others.

So how should these numbers be treated

The direction, lead times lengthening, is credible since two outlets reported it independently. But numbers as specific as 70% or "three-four months to six-eight months" rest on a single source and should be read that way. Whether Samsung and SK hynix's new fabs actually open late will be the real test of this story.

In three lines
  • The claim that chip equipment lead times doubled traces to a single Electronic Times article from July 23.
  • The 70% market-share figure for the top 5 makers and the 3-4-month-to-6-8-month Korean-supplier range both come from that same article.
  • DigiTimes confirmed the direction five days later, but the specific figures aren't independently verified beyond that one report.
