Huawei's AI chip shipments set to double this year to about 1.5 million units, WSJ reports

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Huawei is on track to ship around 1.5 million of its own AI accelerator chips this year, roughly double the volume it shipped in 2025, according to a Wall Street Journal report relayed by semiconductor analyst Jukan on X. The jump matters because it comes despite years of US export controls designed to cut China off from advanced chipmaking equipment and Nvidia's most powerful GPUs. Huawei's AI chips, sold under its Ascend brand, are the centerpiece of China's push to build a domestic alternative to Nvidia's dominant AI computing stack. Export controls, first imposed in 2019 and tightened repeatedly since, block Huawei from buying the most advanced lithography tools and from importing Nvidia's top-tier chips into China. The policy bet was that without those tools, Huawei's chip output would stay small and lag years behind the cutting edge.

Why doubling shipments changes the calculus

A doubling in shipment volume complicates that bet. It does not mean Huawei's chips match Nvidia's newest GPUs in raw performance, Huawei still relies on older manufacturing processes that produce lower yields and less efficient chips per wafer. But volume is its own kind of progress: more chips shipped means more Chinese AI labs and cloud providers can train and run models on domestic hardware instead of waiting for, or working around, Nvidia supply.

What the numbers show

Huawei's shipments are doubling year over year, even if per-chip performance still lags Nvidia's newest GPUs.

The report doesn't disclose yield rates, chip architecture, or which customers are absorbing the volume, so it's hard to know how much of this capacity goes to frontier model training versus lighter inference work. Still, for investors watching Nvidia's China exposure, and for policymakers debating whether to tighten or ease export rules, the shipment trajectory is one of the clearest signals available of how fast Huawei's alternative stack is actually scaling.
