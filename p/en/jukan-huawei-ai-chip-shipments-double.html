Huawei's AI Chip Shipments Are Said to Double: the Real Bottleneck Is HBM, Not Lithography

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Huawei is on track to ship around 1.5 million of its own AI accelerator chips this year, roughly double the volume it shipped in 2025, according to a Wall Street Journal report relayed by semiconductor analyst Jukan on X. The jump matters because it comes despite years of US export controls designed to cut China off from advanced chipmaking equipment and Nvidia's most powerful GPUs.

The exact 1.5 million figure doesn't hold up under scrutiny

The original WSJ article sits behind a paywall and couldn't be verified directly. Other outlets' own shipment estimates, gathered together, tell a messier story. Bloomberg put 2025 Ascend 910C shipments at roughly 300,000 units (up to 1 million dies across the whole Ascend line) and 2026 at 600,000 units (up to 1.6 million dies). SemiAnalysis put actual 2025 shipments at 805,000 units (653,000 of them 910C). JPMorgan, cited by Tom's Hardware, put SMIC-based output at 600,000-650,000 in 2025 and 800,000-850,000 in 2026. The US Commerce Department separately estimated Huawei's 2025 capacity at under 200,000 units, three to seven times lower than the industry estimates above. No single source independently confirms "exactly 1.5 million, exactly double."

The real bottleneck isn't lithography, it's HBM

Huawei's AI chips sell under the Ascend brand, and export controls first imposed in 2019 and tightened since have blocked Huawei from buying the most advanced lithography tools or importing Nvidia's top chips. But SemiAnalysis and Tom's Hardware independently point to a different constraint as the real one: high-bandwidth memory (HBM). Huawei's pre-restriction HBM stockpile, estimated at around 11.7 million stacks, was expected to run out by the end of 2025, and domestic substitute CXMT's annual capacity of roughly 2.2 million stacks supports only 250,000-400,000 910C packages. A July 2026 Bloomberg Intelligence survey still named HBM shortage as the biggest bottleneck.

What multiple analysts show

The real constraint on Huawei isn't lithography, it's the pre-restriction HBM stockpile running dry, and once it does, shipments could fall well short of 1.5 million.

Inside the same export-control story, analysts disagree on which specific component is actually doing the choking. DigiTimes reported Huawei's SMIC-made 7nm-class chip yield rising from around 20% in September 2024 to around 40% in early 2025, but no updated figure beyond that could be found. That's still well below TSMC's 90%-plus yields at comparable nodes.

Who's actually buying this volume

ByteDance reportedly planned around $5.6 billion in Ascend chip orders in December 2025, and a March 2026 Reuters report relayed by CNBC said ByteDance and Alibaba were considering orders for the newer, inference-focused Ascend 950PR. That suggests a meaningful share of what's shipping now is going to inference workloads, not frontier model training.

So what's the thing to actually watch

The question comes down to one thing: is Huawei's shipment jump proof that export controls failed to contain China's AI chips, or a number propped up by stockpiled memory that's about to run out. If Huawei's HBM stockpile actually empties by the end of 2025 and shipments keep climbing anyway, that's real self-sufficiency. If shipments stall right around then, this "doubling" report will have been a one-time number borrowed from inventory that's already spent.

In three lines
  • A WSJ-sourced report said Huawei will ship 1.5 million AI chips this year, double last year's total.
  • That exact 1.5-million figure doesn't independently show up in any other outlet's reporting.
  • Multiple independent analysts say the real bottleneck isn't lithography, it's Huawei's dwindling HBM stockpile.
