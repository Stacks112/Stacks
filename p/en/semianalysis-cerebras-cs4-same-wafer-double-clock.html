The chip did not change, but the speed doubled

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Cerebras unveiled CS-4 on August 19, and there is no new chip in it. The doubling comes from keeping the same 5nm wafer-scale engine, WSE-3, and pushing far more power into it to double the clock. A rack now holds three wafers instead of two, and a single rack draws 125 to 135 kilowatts.

Cerebras intros faster WSE-3 Turbo processor and first rack-scale CS-4 system (ServeTheHome, 2026-08-19)
출처: servethehome.com

A chipmaker usually cuts hundreds of rectangular dies out of one round 300mm wafer. Cerebras does not cut. One wafer is one chip.

This is the number Cerebras leads with. Yet the speed advantage the same company claims is up to 30x against GPUs. When two multiples diverge this far, it means bandwidth is not what sets the speed.

Checked 2026-08-19 · Cerebras CS-4 announcement materials (released 2026-08-18) · NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 product page, 22TB/s of HBM4 per GPU

ServeTheHome quotes 129.6 petabytes per second for a full rack, which is 43 multiplied by three wafers. The two figures agree.

The 44-gigabyte ceiling

The memory etched into the wafer is SRAM. It is far faster than the HBM stacked next to a conventional chip, and it holds far less. One WSE-3 carries 44 gigabytes of it, and that number did not move from CS-3 to CS-4. The number of storage cells is written into the silicon, so raising the clock does not add any. Frontier model weights today run from hundreds of gigabytes into terabytes. They do not fit in 44 gigabytes, so the model is sliced by layer and spread across several wafers.

Slicing by layer means every added wafer adds another leg to the journey. What caps tokens per second per user is not bandwidth but the number of those legs and the time each one costs.

Cerebras raised off-wafer I/O bandwidth this generation from 1.2Tb/s to 2.4Tb/s. Latency through the switched network fell from 5 microseconds to 3, and direct wafer-to-wafer paths get it to 2. SemiAnalysis still treats that 3 microseconds as a bottleneck. Spreading expert layers across multiple wafers stays impractical at that latency, which leaves slicing by layer as the only workable option.

The bill came due in watts

Power per wafer went from 23 kilowatts on CS-3 to roughly 42. SemiAnalysis therefore reads performance per watt as at best slightly better than CS-3. What offsets it is the bill of materials: fewer components and simpler assembly should land cost per wafer near where it was. The pitch to customers is not efficiency but double the token revenue for the same hardware spend.

This company sells chips and also sells inference run on its own chips. Cloud revenue nearly quadrupled in a year, and it has contracted 600 megawatts of datacenter capacity for delivery through the end of 2027. Put the 125 kilowatts per rack against that number and the reason for booking that much capacity in advance becomes visible.

The Next Platform puts the clock at roughly 2.8GHz against 1.4GHz before, with the 900,000 cores and the 44 gigabytes of SRAM unchanged. It says power and bandwidth figures are being held for a follow-up piece.

The number Hot Chips settles

Cerebras says fuller detail comes at Hot Chips, which opens August 23. The figure to watch there is not bandwidth but tokens per second per user. SemiAnalysis expects CS-4 to land near 4,000 on frontier models, against 2,000 for CS-3. Reach 4,000 and the company crossed a generation without new silicon; stall near 3,000 and we have seen the limit of what a clock can buy.

In three lines
  • Cerebras launched CS-4 without a new chip, keeping the 5nm wafer and doubling clock speed by pushing far more power into it.
  • On-chip memory bandwidth per wafer is 43 petabytes per second, about 1,950 times a single NVIDIA Rubin GPU.
  • Yet the speed advantage the company claims is 30x. The ceiling is not bandwidth but the 44 gigabytes of storage that did not move.
