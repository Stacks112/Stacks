BREAKING: The US 10Y Note Yield officially surges above 4.70% for the first time since January 2025.

This puts yields above the "Liberation Day" high in April 2025, set to drive interest rates to new 52-week highs.

The bond market is flashing red.

July 23, 2026

The US 10-Year Treasury yield officially broke above 4.70% on July 23, its highest level since January 2025, according to the Kobeissi Letter, surpassing even the roughly 4.5% high set during last year's tariff-driven Liberation Day selloff in April 2025.

What's confirmed

CNBC and CNN independently confirmed the 10-Year hit about 4.71% that day, its highest since January 2025, and that the 30-Year yield has spent its longest stretch above 5% since 2007. Both moves put the benchmark that anchors mortgages, corporate borrowing, and the US government's own debt costs on track for a fresh 52-week high.

More than one story is driving this

The Kobeissi framing credits the Iran war almost entirely, but coverage that same day describes a more layered picture. Oil-driven inflation fear is one thread, alongside markets repricing toward a possible rate hike rather than just fewer cuts, uncertainty around incoming Fed chair Kevin Warsh, and fiscal concerns as war costs add to Treasury issuance. One account went as far as calling the Fed-leadership question probably the biggest single driver.

The Liberation Day comparison holds up numerically

The April 2025 tariff-panic peak was around 4.5%, so July's 4.71% genuinely does surpass it, though no single article in the day's coverage states that comparison directly, it follows from combining the two data points rather than being a claim reporters made themselves.

Why the mix of causes matters

Rising long-term yields typically raise the discount rate applied to future corporate profits, a headwind that lands hardest on richly valued growth and AI stocks, while also raising Washington's own interest bill on new debt. Whether that pressure eases depends on which driver is actually dominant, an Iran war that could de-escalate, a Fed leadership question that resolves one way or another, or a fiscal supply problem that doesn't go away regardless of the war's outcome.

In three lines
  • The US 10-Year Treasury yield hit about 4.71% on July 23, its highest since January 2025, confirmed by CNBC and CNN alongside the Kobeissi Letter.
  • The 30-Year yield has stayed above 5% for its longest stretch since 2007, and Brent crude surged the same day on the Houthi tanker strike.
  • But press coverage points to more than just the Iran war: rate-hike repricing, Fed-leadership uncertainty around incoming chair Kevin Warsh, and fiscal/issuance concerns tied to war costs all get cited as drivers too.
