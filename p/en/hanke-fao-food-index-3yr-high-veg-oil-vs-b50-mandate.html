Did the war push world food prices to a three-year high, or did an Indonesian rule?

The FAO Food Price Index rose AGAIN in July, hitting its highest level in OVER 3 YEARS.

Vegetable oil prices are up over 17% from a year ago.

THE US-ISRAELI WAR ON IRAN = MASSIVE COLLATERAL DAMAGE.

August 17, 2026

The UN Food and Agriculture Organization's July food price index, published August 7, came in at 131.1, the highest reading since January 2023. Its vegetable oil index rose to 195.7, up 17.3% from a year earlier and the highest since June 2022.

World food prices hit 3-year high in July as weather and war weigh, FAO says (Reuters, 2026-08-07)
출처: whbl.com

Steve Hanke took those two numbers the same day and called them collateral damage from the US-Israeli war on Iran. The numbers match the FAO release. What FAO itself wrote down as the causes runs wider than that one line.

Oil rose, milk fell

The food price index bundles international trade prices for five groups: cereals, vegetable oils, meat, dairy and sugar. In July those five did not move together. Cereals rose 3.4% on the month and stand 6.9% above a year ago, and sugar rose 5.6% on the month. Meat went the other way, down 2.8%, and dairy fell 24.8% from a year earlier. So the headline index is only 1.0% higher than a year ago. Inside the same table, milk is giving back what cooking oil is adding.

The index reached a three-year high in the very month the mandate went up. With the dates in that order, policy and war have to be weighed separately.

Retrieved 2026-08-17 · FAO Food Price Index, 2026-08-07 · Argus Media, 2026-03-30 · Fuels & Lubes Asia

Biodiesel is a fuel made by treating a vegetable oil such as palm so it can stand in for diesel. Indonesia sets by law how much of it must be blended into diesel, and that share went from 40% to 50% on July 1. When the share goes up, dinner tables and fuel tanks compete for the same palm oil. Higher crude prices sharpen that competition, because when diesel is expensive, blending pays even without a subsidy.

Indonesia targets 50pc biodiesel blend in 2026 (Argus Media, 2026-03-30)
출처: argusmedia.com

Jakarta settled on holding at 40% in January and then reversed the decision in March. The reasons it gave were the narrowing spread between palm oil and diesel, and supply disruption in the Middle East. The war enters here as the force that pushed a policy along.

Why did sunflower oil fall

Read FAO's own wording and palm oil rose for a second month on firm Indonesian biodiesel demand and higher crude prices, while soy oil rose on steady feedstock demand from fuel. Sunflower and rapeseed oil, by contrast, fell in July. FAO notes only that renewed Black Sea tension limited the decline. If war were the force lifting cooking oil, the oil closest to a front line should have risen first. Instead that one fell, and the ones that rose were the tropical oils with a policy bid under them.

Hanke's reading

A three-year high and a 17% jump in cooking oil are collateral damage from the war on Iran.

VS
FAO's table's reading

Palm oil rose on Indonesia's mandate and crude; sunflower oil, nearest the Black Sea, fell.

Looking at the same index, one side sees a front line and the other sees a decree issued in Jakarta.

What $89 Brent leaves behind

The two forces can be sized against each other. Brent traded near $89 a barrel on August 17, 33.9% above a year ago, while the vegetable oil index is up 17.3% over the same span. If crude is the channel through which the war reaches food, only about half of crude's gain has come through. What is left to settle sits with Indonesia. If the September 4 release shows the oil component climbing again while Brent stays flat, the mandate set the price, not the war. If crude slides and cooking oil holds, the bid under it is demand fixed by law. Either way, the input costs of palm traders and of the food companies that fry with the stuff are tied to Jakarta's decision for longer than to the headlines from the war.

In three lines
  • World food prices are the highest in three and a half years, and of the five components only vegetable oil, cereals and sugar rose.
  • On the surface that reads as a war pushing up the price of dinner. Yet dairy fell 24.8% from a year ago, holding the whole index to a 1.0% annual gain.
  • The first driver FAO names for palm oil is Indonesia's 50% biodiesel blending mandate, in force since July 1.
