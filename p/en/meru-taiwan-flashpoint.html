The powder keg off Taiwan: a Japan-Philippines sea-boundary deal China reads as encirclement

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Meru pieces together a slow-building flashpoint just off Taiwan. On May 28, Japan and the Philippines opened formal talks to draw an EEZ boundary between Japan's southernmost Ryukyu islands (Yonaguni sits 110km from Taiwan) and the northern Philippines.

Why is Beijing furious?

Beijing is furious: it argues the water is also Taiwan's, Taiwan is China, so the deal tramples China's rights, and since early June it has pushed coast guard and government ships in as a show of force. The pushback is not coming from Beijing alone. Focus Taiwan reported that on June 2 Taiwan's foreign ministry asked both countries not to infringe on Taiwan's maritime rights, and that Japan's chief cabinet secretary said any deal would not legally bind 'third parties' like Taiwan. Taipei speaking for its own share cuts against the 'Taiwan is China' premise China builds its complaint on. The same stretch of water now draws objections for several different reasons.

Did the talks come out of nowhere?

But the talks aren't a bolt from the blue. They're one piece of a rapidly upgrading Japan-Philippines alliance: a 2024 reciprocal access pact, Japan scrapping its arms-export limits in April, Marcos's May state visit lifting ties to a strategic partnership, and Japan handing over five Abukuma-class frigates from 2027. China sees encirclement, not fishing rights.

What sits on Yonaguni?

The waters east of Taiwan are exactly the corridor US forces would use in a Taiwan contingency, and Japan is hardening the first island chain with missiles on Yonaguni. That hardening is not finished. IBTimes Singapore reported that two Chinese coast guard vessels entered Japan's EEZ around Yonaguni in late June and that China had launched a 'special maritime traffic law enforcement' operation on June 6, while the upgraded Type-03 medium-range surface-to-air missiles meant for Camp Yonaguni are slated for fiscal 2030. Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi said that deployment would reduce the possibility of armed attacks on Japan. The other side is moving before the shield is built.

Meru's one-liner: since PM Takaichi called a Taiwan-Strait conflict a 'survival-threatening situation' for Japan in November 2025, China has retaliated with a travel halt, a seafood-import ban, and, the market's real nerve, rare-earth and critical-metal export controls. Now the EEZ fight adds fresh fuel, and the odds of an armed clash tick up.

Why it matters · The first island chain is where semiconductor supply and Chinese leverage meet. Every notch of Taiwan-Strait tension is a tail risk investors quietly price into chips and defense, and China's rare-earth curbs show the lever is already being pulled.
