Kolon TissueGene's TG-C fails US Phase 3 trial, placebo outperforms on pain relief

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Kolon TissueGene's gene-therapy knee treatment TG-C, formerly sold in Korea as Invossa, missed its primary goal in a US Phase 3 trial. Topline results released July 20 showed patients who got TG-C saw their pain score (VAS) drop 38.6 points and function score (WOMAC) drop 26.34 points over two years. Patients who got a saline placebo injection did slightly better, dropping 39.1 and 27.57 points respectively.

Kolon TissueGene Reports Topline ACTiVION-II Phase 3 Clinical Trial Results for TissueGene-C (TG-C) Targeting Osteoarthritis (OA) of the Knee (GlobeNewswire, 2026-07-20)
출처: globenewswire.com

The company's own release that day says TG-C showed no statistically significant improvement over placebo on the co-primary endpoints of VAS pain and WOMAC total score at Month 12. It calls the trial ACTiVION-II (NCT03291470) and puts enrolment at 531 randomised subjects across 27 US sites. Those 531 do not add up to the 310 and 151 in the card. The company counted everyone randomised, and the card's figures are drawn on a different basis.

So why fewer surgeries?

There is one twist. Only 2 of 310 TG-C patients (0.6 percent) needed total knee replacement surgery during the trial, versus 8 of 151 placebo patients (5.3 percent). That gap hints TG-C may have slowed the underlying joint damage even though it did not beat placebo on subjective pain scores. The same release adds that the study missed every key secondary endpoint, and it never mentions knee replacement at all. So the surgery gap sits outside the scorecard the company chose to report on.

What does October change?

Kolon TissueGene ran two near-identical Phase 3 trials about five months apart, and the second one, trial 12301, reads out in October. The US Food and Drug Administration typically wants two independent pivotal trials to show consistent results before approving a drug, so a second miss would make approval even less likely.

[News Analysis] Kolon faces leadership test after TG-C's first US phase 3 setback (Korea Biomedical Review, 2026-07-24)
출처: koreabiomed.com

Korea Biomedical Review names the remaining study TGC-12301 and confirms the October readout. It also reports that four key secondary endpoints on pain and physical function failed to reach statistical significance in the first trial. The same piece expects the company's stated timeline, a 2027 filing and a 2028 US launch, to slip. The card asks whether October settles the picture, and the answer arriving early is that the calendar is already moving out.

Where have we seen this drug before?

TG-C is the same drug that triggered a scandal in 2019, when regulators discovered one of its two injected components was not the cartilage cell it was labeled as. The stock has been drifting lower since May, well before this readout.

Why it matters · The pain endpoint failed but the surgery-avoidance signal favors TG-C, making October's second trial the swing factor for FDA approval odds.

Worth asking · TG-C missed its main goal but beat placebo on surgery avoidance. Does the real verdict wait for October's second trial, or is the answer already in?
