Houthis declare a naval blockade of Saudi Arabia, threatening oil's last detour after Hormuz

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

The Houthis have declared a full naval blockade of Saudi Arabia, putting oil's last detour after Hormuz, the Red Sea, at risk. Meru updates a story he's been tracking since a Houthi delegation's plane was targeted on the runway at Sana'a airport, an attack the Houthis blame on Saudi Arabia. On July 20, the Houthis declared a full naval blockade of Saudi Arabia and ordered a national mobilization, echoing their 2023 campaign that hit 79 ships in the Red Sea.

What rides on one detour?

The stakes: since Hormuz was closed, Saudi Arabia has been rerouting about 70% of its exports, roughly 5 million barrels a day, through the Red Sea port of Yanbu and the 29km-wide Bab-el-Mandeb strait, which alone carries 10% of the world's seaborne crude.

How much does going around cost?

Even if tankers dare the strait, the costs pile up. Suez requires offloading crude via pipeline to lighten VLCCs (very large crude carriers), adding roughly $1 million in tolls and pipeline fees, while the Cape of Good Hope route stretches a 20-day trip to 50 days and adds $1.5 million in fuel. War-risk insurance has already spiked to 2.5% of hull value, about $2.5 million per supertanker.

How shipping insurance rates are rising, as Hormuz, Bab al-Mandeb shut down (Al Jazeera, 2026-07-23)
출처: aljazeera.com

Al Jazeera prices the water in segments. Hormuz went from 1 to 3 percent of hull value before the war to 7.5 to 10 percent, while Bab al-Mandeb sits at 0.5 percent and the Red Sea off western Saudi Arabia at 0.1 percent. The same piece works out that at a freight rate of $77.96 per tonne, cover for one 270,000-tonne tanker can run to about $21 million. That is a different clock and a different way of slicing the route than the card's 2.5%. The direction of travel matches, but on the date of that piece the Red Sea leg was still priced far below Hormuz, so the detour kept some of its cost advantage.

Why does this reach the pump?

Meanwhile Iran claims two tankers exploded near Hormuz, US bases in Jordan, Kuwait and Bahrain are under renewed attack, and US pump prices jumped from $3.87 to $4.10 a gallon in a week, crossing the psychologically loaded $4 mark ahead of midterms. US Central Command is set to resume operations against Iran once US markets close today.

Gas again tops $4 per gallon as Iran fighting persists (Axios, 2026-07-20)
출처: axios.com

Axios puts the US national average at $4.00 a gallon that same day. That is 86 cents above a year earlier, and it started 2026 at $2.8335 on January 1. The card's $3.87 and $4.10 come from a different series than that national average. The $4 line being crossed is confirmed by a story filed the same day, and that story blames thinning tanker traffic through Hormuz. Meru's read: Bab-el-Mandeb doesn't have to fully close for the damage to show up, longer routes and fatter insurance bills already raise the delivered cost of oil.

Why it matters · Hormuz risk was already priced in; a Red Sea blockade on top removes oil's last detour, so even rerouted supply gets structurally costlier through longer voyages and spiking war-risk insurance.

Worth asking · Is this Houthi blockade another bluff that fades, or the one that finally shuts oil's last detour?

What happened next

MissAs of Aug 1 reporting (The National, Al Jazeera), Bab-el-Mandeb transit was not actually cut off, vessels (around 25 commodity ships) kept transiting, and the Houthis denied plans to charge ships. The blockade showed up as higher insurance/route costs, not an actual transit stoppage, so the piece's bar was not met.
