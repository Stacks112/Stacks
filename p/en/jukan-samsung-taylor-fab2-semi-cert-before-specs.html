Samsung Electronics Accelerates Taylor Fab 2 Buildout, Asks Equipment Suppliers to Secure Certifications in Advance

Samsung Electronics is accelerating efforts to expand its advanced foundry capacity in the United States. Ahead of the planned groundbreaking for the second Taylor…

August 20, 2026

Samsung Electronics has asked the suppliers who will install equipment in its second foundry fab in Taylor, Texas to obtain SEMI certification ahead of time. Construction is due to start at the end of this year, with mass production targeted for 2030.

Samsung to start Taylor Fab 2 build by end-2026, targets 2030 mass production (TrendForce, 2026-07-31)
출처: trendforce.com

That report repeats the schedule the company gave on its second-quarter earnings call on July 30. A plan to secure one more fab for 1.4nm work came up at the same time, but that one carries a condition: it will be decided in phases, according to customer orders.

Why certification comes first

SEMI certification is an international industry standard that judges whether a piece of semiconductor equipment is safe for the people and the plant around it. It is paperwork that has to be in hand before a tool can be loaded onto a ship and sent to another country, and without it the tool does not clear the port. That puts the certification at the very front of the queue when a fab is being built. It is the kind of step that is already too late if you get to it after the building is up. What stands out here is the order. The detailed specifications for the second Taylor fab have not been finalized, an equipment industry official said. Samsung has asked suppliers to prepare the paperwork for shipping tools to a fab that has not yet decided which tools it wants.

The common reading

Asking for certification before the specs exist is a signal that groundbreaking is close. It reads as Samsung hardening its commitment to building in the United States.

VS
The equipment industry reading

Certification is required before any tool ships, whatever happens. Getting it early costs little, and little is lost if the plan changes.

The same act can be read as conviction or as a cheap option. Which one it is can be judged from what this company already did once on the same site.

The five years the first fab took

  • 2021-11-24

    Samsung announced the Taylor site and a $17 billion investment. The target was operation in the second half of 2024

  • 2024-12-20

    The US government finalized its CHIPS Act award at $4.745 billion, down from the $6.4 billion in the preliminary agreement

  • 2025-07-24

    Tesla signed a $16.5 billion contract manufacturing agreement with Samsung Foundry

  • 2026-04

    Equipment move-in began at the first fab

  • 2026-12

    Groundbreaking planned for the second fab, with mass production targeted for 2030

Four years and five months passed between the site announcement and the first tool going in. Over that span the operating target is reported to have slipped from 2024 to 2027.

Retrieved 2026-08-20 · Samsung Newsroom (2021-11-24) · US Commerce Department NIST (2024-12-20) · KED Global (2025-07-28) · TrendForce (2026-07-31)

What held the first fab up was not the building. Samsung failed to land a large customer in the early phase of construction, and as market conditions worsened the investment plan was pushed back several times.

Samsung clinches $16.5 billion chipmaking deal from Tesla (KED Global, 2025-07-28)
출처: kedglobal.com

The contract runs from July 24, 2025 through the end of 2033. Converted to annual revenue it comes to roughly 2.5 trillion won, and the chips are to be made on the 2nm process in Taylor. Construction picked up speed again only after that signature.

What the second Taylor fab needs before it can move

Whether to read early certification as conviction or as a cheap option is still open. No name has been attached to whoever will buy what the second fab makes. It could be filled by expanding Tesla volume, or by bringing in a new customer. Samsung's engineering and construction affiliate is also considering sending dozens of staff to the site, a semiconductor industry official said. The same official added that moving quickly from cleanroom construction to an actual mass-production line will require landing a major customer first. A leading-edge fab built in the United States is an asset whose value changes every time subsidy and tariff conditions move. So there needs to be a way to measure how much the company has really committed, and this request is the first notch on that scale. If the ground is broken by the end of the year, the schedule the company stated has held; if it slips into next year, Samsung is standing where the first fab stood.

In three lines
  • Samsung has asked the companies that make tools for its second Taylor fab to obtain safety certification in advance.
  • It reads as a signal that the fab is going up fast.
  • But the first fab sat still after breaking ground until a large customer appeared, and the second fab has no such name yet.
