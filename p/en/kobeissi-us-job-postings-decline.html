Indeed Postings Are Falling, But the Latest Official Jobs Data Just Hit a Two-Year High

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

US job postings on Indeed fell 3.5% year over year in the week ending July 10, landing near this year's lowest level, The Kobeissi Letter reports. New postings, a more forward-looking gauge, look weaker still, down 10.9% year over year to their lowest since early January.

The Kobeissi Letter, X post (2026-07-23) ↗

But the official data just moved the other way

Kobeissi frames the decline as an early warning that BLS's own June and July job-openings figures will likely show further weakening. But the most recent JOLTS release, for May, published June 30, actually showed openings essentially unchanged at 7.6 million (4.6% rate), beating consensus estimates of around 7.3 million and marking a two-year high.

Job Openings and Labor Turnover Summary (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2026-06-30)
출처: bls.gov

At the time of that release, June and July JOLTS data didn't exist yet. So the "likely show further weakening" framing is a prediction about data that hasn't been published, and the most recent confirmed data actually points the other way.

Does Indeed really lead BLS

There's no rigorous published analysis confirming Indeed data reliably leads official JOLTS figures, and this instance runs counter to the claim. While Indeed postings were falling, the most recent confirmed BLS reading was rising.

So how should this signal be read

The Indeed numbers are real data, but treating them as proof that official statistics will soon weaken is a forecast about figures that don't exist yet. Whether the June and July JOLTS reports, due in August and September, actually follow Indeed down, or extend May's strength, will be the real test.

In three lines
  • US job postings on Indeed fell 3.5% year over year in the week of July 10, The Kobeissi Letter reports.
  • But the most recent official BLS JOLTS data (May) actually came in above expectations at 7.6 million, a two-year high.
  • The idea that Indeed reliably leads BLS data is weakly supported, and this time it pointed the opposite way.
