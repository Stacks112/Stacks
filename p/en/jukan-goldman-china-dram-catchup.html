China's DRAM Prices Match Samsung's, But Only for Mainstream DDR5

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Goldman Sachs held an expert call with a Chinese DRAM industry insider, and semiconductor analyst Jukan relayed the key takeaways. The headline point: Chinese DRAM makers' prices have already caught up to global leaders like Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron.

How close has it actually gotten

A direct comparison by Tom's Hardware on July 26 found CXMT's 64GB DDR5-5600 RDIMM selling for 18,999 yuan on JD.com, versus 18,595 yuan for equivalent Samsung/SK hynix modules, a gap of just 2.2%. SemiAnalysis separately found CXMT's Q1 2026 average selling prices had narrowed to within 5-10% of the big three, and that the quarter's earnings improvement came mainly from pricing (up roughly 57% quarter over quarter), not volume growth.

The catch: this parity is mainstream-only

Per memory vendors WCCFTech spoke to at Computex in June, this price convergence is limited to entry-level and mainstream DDR5 modules running up to 8000 MT/s. CXMT has essentially no presence in premium formats like CUDIMM, CQDIMM or MRDIMM. So "Chinese DRAM matches Samsung" is true for a slice of the lineup, not the whole thing. "Yield" is the share of chips on a wafer that come out working. SemiAnalysis estimates CXMT's cost per bit runs more than 30% above the big three, partly because its die is around 40% larger than Samsung's, meaning fewer working chips per wafer. Charging within 2% of leader prices while carrying that much higher cost points to either eating losses to defend share, or state subsidies covering the gap. No report found actually documents subsidies covering that specific price gap, though. What is documented is capex-stage support, like a Hefei state investment vehicle funding around 80% of CXMT's Phase 1 buildout.

The bigger story may be industry-wide, not subsidy-driven

A larger reason prices have converged: Samsung, SK hynix and Micron have been redirecting wafer capacity toward AI-driven HBM, tightening the overall supply of conventional DRAM industry-wide. CXMT has no HBM pivot to make, so it kept its volume relatively intact. This looks less like CXMT winning on price and more like its rivals stepping out of the way. A technology roadmap came up too. Chinese DRAM makers are expected to make further progress on 3D-stacked DRAM in 2027, and capacity expansions in Hefei, Shanghai and Beijing are slated to be fully operational by 2028.

Is blocking lithography alone enough

Some fabs reportedly run mostly on domestically produced Chinese equipment, with one exception: high-end lithography, the machines that etch circuit patterns onto wafers. But that chokepoint isn't just EUV. Tom's Hardware reports China began delivering domestically made immersion DUV (ArFi) tools to SMIC, Hua Hong and CXMT in 2026, though commercial-scale self-sufficiency isn't expected until the mid-2030s. ASML still holds 98.7% of the immersion DUV market.

So what's the thing to actually watch

The question comes down to one thing: is Chinese DRAM matching leader prices despite lower yields a loss-leading share defense, subsidy power, or simply a windfall from the industry's own pivot to HBM. Once Samsung, SK hynix and Micron dial back HBM and return to conventional DRAM, CXMT's pricing power gets tested for real. If the gap stays around 2%, China's catch-up is real. If it widens again, this "price parity" will have been a temporary window opened by the industry's own capacity shift.

In three lines
  • A Goldman Sachs expert call said Chinese DRAM prices already rival Samsung, SK hynix and Micron.
  • CXMT's DDR5 pricing has in fact narrowed to within about 2% of Samsung's.
  • But that parity is limited to mainstream DDR5, and the real driver looks like industry-wide HBM reallocation, not subsidies.
