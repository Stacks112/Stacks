Nvidia halves Vera rack memory a second time, from 96GB to 64GB

To summarize GFHK's note:

Rubin's SOCAMM2 has been nerfed once again — down to 64GB.

But is that bearish? If anything, it's the opposite.

Working off a 192GB SOCAMM2 configuration, NVIDIA had asked the memory makers for a corresponding volume of SOCAMM2 — and the memory makers…

August 6, 2026

Nvidia's SOCAMM2 memory module, the card that plugs in beside its next-generation server CPU Vera, is set to shrink again, from 96 gigabytes (GB) to 64GB. The change is expected to be in place by the first quarter of 2027. The module was originally designed at 192GB, then already cut once to 96GB back in June and July. This is the second cut following that first one.

Nvidia reportedly halves Vera Rubin SOCAMM capacity as memory costs near 29% of system BOM (TrendForce, 2026-07-28)
출처: trendforce.com

TrendForce pointed to a specific reason for that first cut. Memory had climbed to 29% of total system cost, above the 20% target Nvidia had set. So total memory on the Vera CPU side fell from 55 terabytes (TB) to 28TB, while the GPU-side HBM4 memory stayed unchanged at 20.7TB.

Why SOCAMM2 keeps getting cut

SOCAMM2 is a low-power memory module that plugs in beside a server CPU. Unlike HBM, which stacks vertically beside the GPU, it feeds data the CPU needs from the side.

Lined up, the three dates show this isn't one decision but an ongoing trend.

TrendForce (2026-07-28) · Jukan (2026-08-06)

Jukan explains the second cut differently. Nvidia asked memory makers for enough SOCAMM2 to fill a 192GB configuration, and the makers said they could only supply 60 to 70 percent of that volume. That would mean the buyer didn't trim on its own. The makers simply couldn't fill the order.

Nvidia halves SOCAMM2 capacity from 192GB to 96GB amid LPDDR shortage (The Elec, 2026-06-10)
출처: thelec.net

The Elec pointed to the same cause for the first cut. Citing an industry source, it said the shortage was in low-power DRAM (LPDDR) supply, and that it actually signals LPDDR demand growing faster than expected. Two outlets, two points in time, point the same direction.

Memory's share of system cost before the first cut29%
Nvidia's target share20%

There's confirmation the cost share sat 9 points above target, but neither report says whether the same reasoning applies to this second cut.

TrendForce, retrieved 2026-07-28

The HBM 12-high fight, and capex that hasn't shrunk

In the same post, Jukan also touched on Rubin Ultra's HBM spec. One research house had written it was locked in at 8-high (8Hi) HBM4E only, but Jukan says a 12-high (12Hi) configuration is still under consideration. That extends the earlier 12Hi-versus-8Hi question: it isn't decided either way yet. A note from Jeff Pu, quoted in the same post, put 2026 capex plans for Amazon, Google and Meta at roughly $220 billion, $195 to 205 billion and $130 to 145 billion respectively. Memory content is shrinking even as cloud providers' spending plans are not. A single report of the memory spec shrinking looks, on its own, like a sign AI investment is cooling. But cloud providers' capex plans haven't shrunk over the same period, and now there's a specific number, only 60 to 70 percent of requested volume filled, that points the other way. The weight tips toward makers falling short of orders, not buyers pulling them back. Whether this 64GB spec holds through the first quarter of 2027, or gets raised again once more supply frees up, is what settles it.

In three lines
  • Nvidia is cutting SOCAMM2 memory on its Vera CPU again, from 96GB to 64GB by early 2027.
  • On the surface it looks like Nvidia trimming memory to save cost.
  • But memory makers filling only 60-70% of the requested volume says this is a shortage, not a saving.
