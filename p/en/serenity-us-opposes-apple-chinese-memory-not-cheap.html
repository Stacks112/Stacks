The Chinese memory Washington is blocking is not cheap memory

US Government urges $AAPL not to buy Chinese memory chips. $MU, $SKHY, and Samsung are happy to hear this...

Hilariously enough, CXMT/YMTC is price hiking too, so it's not exactly "cheap Chinese memory that floods the market".

Apple once upon a time actually had extreme…

August 15, 2026

The US Commerce Secretary said he had told Apple directly not to buy Chinese memory. Howard Lutnick, speaking after touring an Apple manufacturing site in Houston on August 13, said "the Trump administration is not in favor of that", and confirmed he had put that to Apple plainly.

Commerce Secretary says US opposes Apple buying Chinese memory chips (9to5Mac, 2026-08-14)
출처: 9to5mac.com

The Chinese memory in question comes from two firms, CXMT and YMTC. CXMT makes DRAM and YMTC makes NAND. Both are designated by the Pentagon as Chinese military companies and already sit under US export restrictions.

Apple chief operating officer Sabih Khan has said memory needs "not a lot of customization". That means it is an easy part to re-source, and that is exactly why politics has room to reach into it.

Price was never the reason

This is where the usual reading of the story turns over.

NAND and DRAM prices surge as China's YMTC and CXMT shake up the South Korea-dominated market (BigGo Finance, 2026-07-05)
출처: finance.biggo.com

Both firms widened their position and raised prices at the same time. That looks less like buying share with cheap parts and more like share opening up first in a market short of supply.

Cited from BigGo Finance reporting of 2026-07-05, accessed 2026-08-15

CXMT signed a contract with Tencent worth about $3 billion over three to five years. The piece above reads that deal as a move to secure volume rather than the result of a discount. A supplier with adequate technology, steady output and negotiable pricing beats a better but supply-constrained one in the competition that actually happens.

The common reading

China is flooding the market with cheap memory, and Apple wants the cheap parts.

VS
Serenity's reading

Chinese memory is raising prices too. What Apple is after is volume it can actually get, not a lower price.

The same facts split depending on whether Chinese memory is treated as a price question or a supply question.

The years when Apple held the whip are over

Serenity adds one line to that. Apple once held extreme leverage over its component suppliers, the argument runs, and that was before the AI build-out arrived. As memory capacity has been pulled toward high bandwidth memory for data centres, the volume left for PCs and phones has been pushed into the queue that takes what remains. In that queue the two Chinese firms were a card Apple could still play, and Washington is now telling it to drop that card.

What August 21 decides

A date is already fixed to this. On July 30 a bipartisan group of senators led by Chuck Schumer wrote to Apple demanding it commit to using no CXMT or YMTC memory in any Apple product sold anywhere in the world, with a reply due by August 21. If Apple gives that commitment on the date, Chinese memory loses one of its largest Western customers and Apple has to find replacement volume in a market where prices are climbing. If the date passes without one, the matter stays between Washington and Apple, and the share the two Chinese firms hold gets settled by supply rather than by rules.

In three lines
  • The administration has moved against the Chinese memory Apple wanted to buy, and the Senate has a reply due from Apple by August 21.
  • At face value that is a move to keep cheap Chinese parts out of the market.
  • But CXMT and YMTC have raised both their share and their prices over the past year, and what Apple was after was volume it could actually get rather than a lower price.
