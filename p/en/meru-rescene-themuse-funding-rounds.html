RESCENE's Viral Break Wasn't One Meme: It Was Two

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

A tiny K-pop label with just 10 million won ($7,000) in starting capital, The Muse, just closed its Series A funding round for girl group RESCENE at 2 billion won. Market commentator Meru used the case as a textbook example of how Korean startup fundraising actually plays out. Founded in Jeju in December 2020 by Berklee College of Music alumni including Lee Joo-heon and Kim Hye-soo, The Muse raised under 1 billion won in a Pre-Series A in May 2023 to train a five-member girl group. RESCENE, with an average age of 16.6, debuted in March 2024.

Why the company suddenly stopped raising

Starting in November 2025, The Muse pursued a 5 billion won Series A for a new album and operating costs, but by June 2026 had raised only 2 billion won, 40% of its target. And then it stopped there, on purpose. The surface story is that leader Wonyi's personal video went viral and ad inquiries poured in. What actually happened was two separate events overlapping. Member Minami appeared on Wonyi's channel and shouted "Geoje Yaho," a phrase tied to Wonyi's hometown, in videos posted March 6 and 20. The catchphrase spread as a meme through spring, dragging the group's 2024 debut track "Love Attack" back to No. 1 on Melon's real-time chart by July 8. Around the same time, an MBC producer's comment comparing Wonyi's use of a regional dialect ending to internet slang sparked a separate, unrelated national controversy that happened to land in the same season.

The Asia Business Daily's English edition reports that KNET Investment Partners and JB Investment joined existing backers in the round. Its June 9 write-up still had "Love Attack" only inside the chart top 10; the No. 1 finish on Melon came later, on July 8. The Muse's co-founder Kim Hye-soo said on MBC variety show "Point of Omniscient Interference" that the company got "97 missed calls in one to two hours, and more than 100 ad inquiries." The company's official reason for stopping: internal growth of the artist and company matters more right now than outside fundraising activity.

What the numbers show

2025 revenue came to 1.77 billion won, with a 5.69 billion won operating loss. One other outlet (Daum) attributes very similar revenue and loss figures to fiscal 2024 instead, so which year the numbers actually belong to isn't fully settled as of this writing. Either way, RESCENE remains the company's only revenue source. Measured against other 2026 K-pop-adjacent funding rounds, The Muse's raise looks small, well behind 23rd Century Girls' 4 billion won and Scon's 7.5 billion won, both closed in April 2026.

So what's the thing to actually watch

The question comes down to one thing: cash in now by selling a stake at a higher valuation, or keep betting everything on RESCENE alone. Meru calls this one-team risk, pointing to HYBE, which started as a single bet on BTS before acquiring Source Music (2019) and Pledis (2020) to spread the risk, as a model worth studying. That comparison is Meru's own framing; no other outlet made the same HYBE parallel independently. Memes can't be engineered on demand. Without the two events that broke in the same spring window, The Muse would very likely still be sitting on a Series A short of its target.

In three lines
  • Tiny K-pop label The Muse just closed a 2 billion won Series A around girl group RESCENE.
  • The surface story is that leader Wonyi's video went viral and ad inquiries flooded in.
  • Actually two separate events overlapped in the same weeks, member Minami's "Geoje Yaho" meme, and an unrelated dialect controversy.
