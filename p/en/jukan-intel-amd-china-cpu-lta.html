Intel and AMD are locking in multi-year CPU deals with China as prices jump 40%, a pattern that started in memory chips

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Intel and AMD are negotiating long-term supply agreements with major Chinese server customers, according to a Reuters report relayed by semiconductor analyst Jukan on X, responding to a real and well-documented price squeeze.

The deal structure and the numbers behind it

The agreements span roughly one to more than two years, locking in purchase volumes while letting prices float with the market, meaning buyers guarantee volume but stay exposed to further price increases rather than getting a fixed price. Server CPU prices in China have risen more than 40% since the start of 2026, with some months seeing increases of over 10%, consistently reported across Reuters, Tom's Hardware, and other outlets covering the story.

The six-month lead time figure needs a date stamp

The claim that some Intel lead times stretched to six months originates from a Reuters and Tom's Hardware report in February 2026; by March, industry coverage described lead times more conservatively as roughly 8 to 12 weeks. The six-month figure is real but reflects an earlier snapshot within this story, not necessarily the situation as of the July agreements.

Why China specifically

Server CPUs, unlike GPUs, are not covered by US export controls, making them one of the few AI-infrastructure components Chinese buyers can still purchase freely. That funnels demand toward Intel and AMD even as a broader AI-driven chip and memory shortage pulls fab capacity toward other products, compounded by reports of buyers stockpiling ahead of any further restrictions.

A pattern, or one investor's framing of several separate stories

Investor Serenity placed the CPU deals inside a wider narrative: long-term agreements spreading in 2026 from memory chips, where Micron, Samsung, and SK hynix signed multi-year DRAM and NAND deals, into photonics components, and now into CPUs and reportedly passive parts like MLCCs. Each of those individual shortages is separately documented, but no outlet ties them together as one unified industry-wide trend the way Serenity's framing does, so that synthesis is best read as her own interpretation rather than an established fact.

In three lines
  • Reuters reports Intel and AMD are signing 1-to-2-plus-year supply deals with major Chinese server customers, fixing purchase volumes while prices float, as China-specific CPU prices rise over 40% year to date and lead times stretch out.
  • The squeeze is real: Chinese server CPU prices are up more than 10% month over month in some periods and lead times reportedly reached six months earlier this year, though that figure traces to a February report and lead times were later described as shorter.
  • Investor Serenity frames this as part of a broader shift from memory to CPUs and passive parts, but that specific 'spreading pattern' is her own interpretation, not something independently documented as a single industry trend.
