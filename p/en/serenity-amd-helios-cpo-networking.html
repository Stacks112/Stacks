Is AMD's Helios really getting co-packaged optics, or is that still a generation away?

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

AMD's networking partners for its next-generation Helios AI server racks expect the company to move to co-packaged optics eventually, according to informal channel checks relayed by investor Serenity on X, though the specifics are worth separating carefully from what AMD has actually confirmed.

What Helios is, and what it isn't yet

Helios is AMD's first rack-scale AI system, a roughly 7,000-pound cabinet packing 72 of its newest Instinct MI455X GPUs alongside Venice CPUs, built to compete directly with Nvidia's NVL72 racks. Helios itself uses today's standard copper and pluggable-optic connections, not co-packaged optics, or CPO, the technique of building fiber-optic connections directly into a chip's package instead of using separate modules. AMD's own confirmed CPO work is tied to its later MI500 generation, code-named Verona, expected around 2027-28, through a reported silicon-photonics partnership with GlobalFoundries.

The 'no delays' pushback is about a different company's timeline

An unnamed AI infrastructure executive involved in a hyperscaler's CPO supply chain told Serenity there is no sign of the delays some third-party reports have recently flagged. Those delay reports appear to trace to a separate industry debate over Nvidia and Broadcom's switch-side CPO ramp, which one widely circulated analyst note argued could slip into 2027-2029, a claim that has itself been disputed, with other trackers reporting Nvidia's Spectrum-X CPO and Broadcom's Bailly switches already in volume ramp. AMD's Helios and its later CPO roadmap are a separate matter from that specific controversy.

Why the distinction matters

As AI clusters scale into the tens of thousands of chips, today's copper and pluggable-optic connections become a bottleneck on both power draw and bandwidth, and moving optics onto the chip package is one of the industry's clearest paths to keep scaling without a proportional jump in power use. That is the strategic logic partners are reportedly telling Serenity applies to AMD and the wider hyperscaler industry over time, but it describes a multi-year roadmap starting with Helios's successor, not a claim about what Helios itself ships with today.

An early signal, not a confirmed one

Serenity flagged the commentary as informal, not official guidance from AMD or its partners. Given it rests on a single anonymous executive and unnamed channel checks, and appears to conflate AMD's own later-stage CPO plans with a separate Nvidia and Broadcom timeline dispute, it is best read as an early, unverified signal about the direction of the industry rather than a confirmed AMD roadmap.

In three lines
  • AMD's Helios networking partners reportedly expect the company to move to co-packaged optics eventually, per informal channel checks relayed by investor Serenity on X.
  • Helios itself, AMD's current rack-scale AI system, is not confirmed to use CPO yet; AMD's publicly reported CPO work targets its later MI500/Verona platform around 2027-28 via a GlobalFoundries silicon-photonics partnership.
  • The 'delay' reports being pushed back on appear to trace to a separate industry debate over Nvidia and Broadcom's switch-side CPO timeline, not AMD specifically.
