Trump Says Xi and Putin Both Promised No Weapons to Iran. Neither Confirmed It

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

President Trump wrote on Truth Social that Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin both personally promised him they will not sell weapons to Iran. He said Xi's pledge extends to Chinese companies too, and that he is doing Xi "very big favors" in return.

What state is the Iran war actually in

The war is real. It began February 28, 2026, and Trump's July 23 statement lands exactly on day 145. The US and Israel are fighting Iran, with Hezbollah aligned to Tehran and the Houthis opening a second front by blockading Saudi shipping routes on July 20. Two ceasefires, in April and June, each collapsed. Trump declared the June memorandum "over" at the NATO Ankara summit on July 8. Iran did reject a US-backed ceasefire proposal put forward by Iraqi mediators. But CNBC reported on July 27 that the war has since paused to give peace talks "space," an ambiguous state that is neither a clean ceasefire nor open war.

Did China actually confirm anything

China's foreign ministry did not confirm the pledge itself. Instead it reaffirmed Iran as a "comprehensive strategic partner" and backed Iran's sovereignty, security and dignity, a generic statement that neither confirms nor denies Trump's framing.

What did Russia actually deny

Foreign Minister Lavrov, speaking after ASEAN talks in Manila, said Russia isn't supplying weapons to Iran and that Moscow wants de-escalation in the Strait of Hormuz. That's a separate denial, not a direct confirmation of the personal pledge Trump described. What matters more is a different US allegation: that Russia fed Iran targeting data and supplied upgraded Shahed drones. The Kremlin denied only "military transactions" on that point, not the intelligence-sharing allegation itself.

What's actually confirmed

China never confirmed the pledge, and Russia denied only arms sales, not the separate intelligence-sharing allegation. The war itself is paused, not over.

Whether to take the statement at face value or discount the unconfirmed half comes down to that gap.

What the "very big favors" refers to is also unclear. A May Beijing summit did produce a US-China trade deal covering tariff cuts, rare-earth exports and Chinese soybean purchases, but no reporting directly links that deal to the Iran arms question. That connection is Trump's own framing.

So what's the thing to actually watch

Only one thing actually settles this: whether Chinese or Russian weapons show up in Iran regardless of what was said. Trump's accounts of foreign leaders' promises have diverged from his own officials' descriptions before. Whether this pledge holds will show up in the war's next phase, specifically whether the Hormuz blockade eases or tightens further.

In three lines
  • Trump says both Xi Jinping and Putin personally promised him they won't sell weapons to Iran.
  • On its face that sounds like two of Iran's most capable arms suppliers stepping back mid-war.
  • But China only reaffirmed its "comprehensive strategic partnership" with Iran without confirming any pledge, and Russia denied only weapons sales, not the separate US allegation that it fed Iran targeting data.
