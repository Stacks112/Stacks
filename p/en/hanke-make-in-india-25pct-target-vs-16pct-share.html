India's factories kept growing for twelve years, yet manufacturing's share did not

#IndiaWatch🇮🇳: When PM Modi came to power in 2014, he promised to grow manufacturing and make it account for 25% of India’s GDP.

12 years later, manufacturing remains AT or BELOW 15% OF GDP.

PM MODI’S “MAKE IN INDIA” INITIATIVE HAS FAILED TO DELIVER.

August 17, 2026

Manufacturing's place in India's economy is roughly where it was twelve years ago. The target set in 2014 was a quarter of the economy, and no statistical series in use today puts it anywhere near that line.

Net assessment of India's manufacturing sector (Ideas for India, 2026-02-27)
출처: ideasforindia.in

That figure did not originate with the Modi government. The National Manufacturing Policy of 2011 had already written down a goal of lifting manufacturing to 25% of the economy within a decade while creating 100 million jobs, and the Make in India campaign of 2014 adopted that same number as its own.

Fifteen percent or sixteen, depending on the ruler

The "15% or below" in Hanke's post is a number that moves with the ruler you pick. Measured as gross value added, the series India's own statistics office publishes, manufacturing has drifted between 16% and 18%. Measured as a share of gross domestic product, the basis the World Bank uses so countries can be compared, it falls to around 12%. The two rulers differ over where taxes and subsidies are added or removed, and over which year is used as the price base.

2014 government target25%
India statistics office, GVA basis (2024-25)16.3%
World Bank, GDP basis (2024)12.5%

Neither ruler reaches two thirds of the target line. Hanke's "15% or below" holds only on the World Bank basis; on the one India's own government uses, the figure is in the sixteens.

Why the factories grew and the share did not

This is where the reading usually goes wrong. A flat share does not mean manufacturing failed to grow. Manufacturing output itself has expanded at roughly 6% a year since 2021. Services simply expanded at close to 8% over the same stretch. A share is a fraction. When the numerator grows and the denominator grows faster, the ratio falls. Not everything can be blamed on the denominator, though. Of 23 manufacturing sub-sectors, 11 produced less in 2024-25 than they did in 2018-19. The aggregate rose because a handful of sectors carried the rest.

The iPhones really were built in India

A stalled share does not mean the policy changed nothing. The Production Linked Incentive scheme, launched in 2021, committed 1.97 trillion rupees, about $22.8 billion, across 14 sectors. Investment actually realised through March 2025 was counted at 1.76 trillion rupees.

Apple's iPhone exports from India hit a record $10 billion in FY25 (Business Standard, 2025-10-08) ↗

According to that report, Apple's vendors assembled $22 billion worth of iPhones in India in fiscal 2025, and roughly 80% of that, some $17.5 billion, was shipped abroad. Foxconn and Tata Electronics divide the volume between them. The gap is in the size of the board. Incentives actually disbursed over the same period came to 215 billion rupees, about 11% of the money set aside. The promise was large and the execution was small.

What the early-2027 national accounts settle

Hanke's reading

Twelve years and the share has not moved a notch. It cleared barely half the target, so the policy failed.

VS
New Delhi's reading

Manufacturing output grew about 6% a year. The share stalled because services grew faster.

Both sides are looking at the same twelve years; one reads a numerator that would not grow, the other a denominator that grew faster. The dispute matters because it splits how an investor can own Indian manufacturing. One way assumes the target eventually gets filled and buys Indian industry broadly. The other ignores the share entirely and buys only the narrow boxes where volume genuinely rose, electronics and semiconductors. The record of the last twelve years favours the second. The point of decision is the next set of national accounts. If the provisional estimate for fiscal 2026-27, due early in 2027, puts manufacturing above 17.5%, the stall is over; below that line, it is a thirteenth year in the same place.

In three lines
  • Steve Hanke wrote that India's manufacturing has sat at or below 15% of GDP for twelve years, and called the 2014 target of 25% a failure.
  • Read one way, that says Indian manufacturing did not grow at all over those twelve years.
  • But output itself rose about 6% a year; the share stalled because services grew faster.
