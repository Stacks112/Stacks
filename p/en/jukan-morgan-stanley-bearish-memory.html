A Morgan Stanley note not meant to go public is reportedly behind Korea's sharpest chip selloff this year

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

A Morgan Stanley research note turning bearish on memory chips is real and has been circulating among investors, semiconductor analyst Jukan reports after confirming it with his own sources, and Korean financial media have tied it to a sharp KOSPI selloff.

The bear case, in Shawn Kim's numbers

The note is credited to Shawn Kim, Morgan Stanley's Asia-Pacific technology research head, who has made bearish semiconductor calls before. He argues NAND flash makers are holding roughly 13 weeks of unsold inventory, near the roughly 15-week peak seen during Covid, and that demand has evaporated. He expects Q4 price increases to slow sharply, to just 5% per quarter, which removes the incentive to buy early and stockpile.

Why buyers are pulling back

Big cloud companies say their own chip inventories are already healthy. Tencent has reportedly locked in 90% of what it needs, and China's CXMT is ramping supply fast, all pulling supply and demand back into balance. NAND spot prices have been falling for two months. The note extends the same logic to DRAM and caps HBM growth near 40%, citing TrendForce data.

It's not public, and it's already moving Korean stocks

The note itself hasn't been published anywhere Jukan or Korean outlets could point to directly, all of the corroboration comes from secondary financial press repeating its figures. Even so, Korean coverage reports a single-day KOSPI plunge sharp enough to trigger a circuit breaker around the time the note circulated, with SK hynix and Samsung Electronics both falling more than 8%, alongside other pressures like oil prices and CXMT's blockbuster IPO diverting capital.

A rival analyst sees it differently

Jukan adds that a rival analyst's more bullish channel checks lean heavily on conversations with US cloud firms, whom Kim's camp suspects may be overly optimistic about AI demand.

Inventory data can flip in a quarter. Does this one stick?

The bear case rests on inventory and pricing figures that could shift again within a quarter, while bulls point to structural AI demand this note doesn't fully capture. Whether Kim's numbers hold up when Q4 pricing actually comes in is the test that decides whether this was the start of a real downturn or a data blip.
