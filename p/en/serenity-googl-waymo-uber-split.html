The Uber-Waymo Split Isn't a Breakup. It's an Exclusivity Deal Ending in Two Cities, in 2028

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Uber and Google's self-driving unit Waymo may be headed for a split, the Financial Times reported, according to investor commentary account Serenity, who says this confirms a view he's held for over a year, that Waymo would eventually turn from partner to rival.

What does the split actually mean

Reuters, Bloomberg and CNBC, all citing the FT report, describe something more specific: Waymo has told Uber it will launch its own independent app in exactly two cities, Austin and Atlanta, starting January 2028, ending exclusivity there. That's not a company-wide breakup, it's an exclusivity deal ending in two markets. Under the existing contract, Waymo stays on Uber's platform in those cities through roughly May 2028. The two companies already ended their pilot in Phoenix in late June. Cited friction points include Waymo's complaints about vehicle cleanliness and routing, Uber's complaints about vehicle unavailability in bad weather, and terms both sides called unsustainable. Waymo still runs its own app independently in 11 metro areas with over 500,000 weekly paid rides, and continues working with Uber elsewhere.

Was Uber actually stuck with only Waymo

Serenity argues Uber should have chosen a neutral operator like Avride instead. But Uber already committed up to $375 million to Avride alongside Nebius back in October 2025. Uber also holds an 11.5% stake in Lucid, 5.82% in WeRide, and works with Pony.ai/Verne, Nuro, Zoox, Wayve-Nissan, May Mobility, Momenta, Baidu's Apollo Go, and Motional, more than a dozen AV partners. For the road not taken framing to hold, Uber would have had to actually pass on Avride. It didn't. It's already an investor.

Serenity's reading

Uber made a mistake choosing Waymo from the start, and is now paying for it.

VS
What's actually confirmed

Only a two-city exclusivity deal is ending, and Uber is already an Avride investor working with over a dozen AV companies simultaneously.

Whether to read this report as a clean breakup or a contract adjustment inside an already-diversified portfolio depends on that gap.

Uber, Nebius invest up to $375 million in autonomous vehicle firm Avride (Investing.com, 2025-10-22) ↗

So what's the thing to actually watch

Waymo can currently only reach riders without Uber's app in the 11 metros where it already runs independently. The Austin and Atlanta shift is still two years out. What actually matters is how fast Waymo's own app expands past those 11 cities, and how quickly Uber turns its dozen-plus AV partnerships into real, scaled service.

In three lines
  • Uber and Waymo may be headed for a split, the FT reports, per Serenity.
  • That sounds like a partner turning into Uber's biggest rival.
  • But the actual news is an exclusivity deal ending in two cities in 2028, and Uber is already an investor in the neutral alternative Avride and works with over a dozen other AV companies.

Why it matters · The Uber-Waymo split rumor turns out to be a two-city exclusivity ending, and Uber is already an Avride investor working with over a dozen AV companies. That gap is what determines whether Waymo's threat looks bigger than its actual scale.

Worth asking · What's actually ending is exclusivity in two cities, and Uber already works with several AV companies, yet this got framed as a split. Does that overstate Uber's risk, or do you think this is where things head eventually anyway?
