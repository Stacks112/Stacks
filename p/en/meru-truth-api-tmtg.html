Trump's Truth Social to sell a $100K/month 'Truth API', his posts, a millisecond early, for the algos

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Meru unpacks a strange new product from Trump Media & Technology Group (TMTG, ticker DJT), the company Trump founded and still owns 41% of. From August 1, 2026, its Truth Social platform will sell 'Truth API'.

Why pay for a thousandth of a second?

Truth API is a $100,000-a-month subscription, $60,000 on a three-year deal. It delivers posts from the platform's ten most influential accounts a thousandth of a second before they reach the public feed, in machine-readable JSON that trading algorithms can act on instantly. Those ten accounts are dominated by Trump himself, his sons Don Jr. and Eric, and close allies. FinanceFeeds reported that the list also carries broadcasters Dan Bongino and Sean Hannity, and counted Trump's 41% as 114.75 million shares held through the Donald J. Trump Revocable Trust. The stake is counted there in shares rather than as a percentage, but it points at the same holding. A roster filled with Trump family members and friendly broadcasters backs the claim that these accounts run with him.

출처: financefeeds.com

Did those posts really move markets?

TMTG's own pitch deck sells the edge with ten 'market-moving' Trump posts. An April 2025 'THIS IS A GREAT TIME TO BUY' landed hours before a 90-day tariff pause added $4 trillion to the S&P 500's value; an 'Iran blockade' post spiked oil more than 9% in a day, and a later 'productive talks' post sent it back down. Those are examples the seller picked itself. What carries a price is not the post but the head start on reaching it.

Is this insider trading?

Is it legal? US insider-trading law targets non-public information, and this data becomes public anyway, so TMTG argues it's clean. And while executive-branch officials are barred from such conflicts of interest, the President and Vice President are explicitly exempt from that rule (18 U.S.C. §208), so no penalty attaches. The buyers are high-frequency traders, who already place about 60% of US orders and live or die on being first. Fortune pushes straight back on that reasoning. Renée Jones, a Boston College Law School professor, said material nonpublic information belongs to the US government or the American people, not to Truth Social or President Trump. Tyler Gellasch of the Healthy Markets Association said milliseconds matter in modern markets and the president's social media posts move markets. The same piece noted the SEC is weighing a shift from quarterly to semiannual corporate disclosure, and that the two changes together could split the market into two tiers. The 'it becomes public anyway' defense does not touch the act of selling the moment of publication. The same information still lands on two clocks, one running ahead of the other.
