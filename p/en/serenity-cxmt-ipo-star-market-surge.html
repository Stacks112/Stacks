July 27, 2026

China's DRAM maker ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) surged 472% on its trading debut on Shanghai's STAR Market on July 27. Its market cap jumped from $85.5 billion to $489 billion in a single day. Market watchers, including the X account Serenity, reacted with disbelief to what was Asia's biggest IPO of the year.

How unusual was this listing

CXMT priced its IPO at 8.66 yuan and closed its first day at 49.50 yuan. It raised up to 66.6 billion yuan, beating the previous STAR Market record set by chipmaker SMIC in 2020 (53.2 billion yuan). Reports say the surge pushed CXMT's market cap past Intel's, making it China's largest listed company by that measure.

Was the 472% really about demand

Demand for AI server DRAM is running well ahead of supply, and that part is real. It's lifting every memory maker's valuation. But this specific spike has a mechanical cause too. Only 6.73% of CXMT's total shares were actually tradable at listing. The rest is locked up until January 27, 2027. When the sellable float is this thin, a rush of buyers moves the price much further than it would on a normal-sized float. That's not unique to CXMT. It's a common pattern in early STAR Market debuts.

Even the analysts can't agree on what this number means

According to chip analyst Jukan, Nomura initiated coverage on listing day with a price target of 116 yuan, a 20x P/E multiple built on CXMT's global DRAM share expanding from about 8% to 18% by the end of 2028. The same day, Morningstar put its fair value at 14.90 yuan, 87% below Nomura's number. Its reasoning: without EUV lithography, CXMT can't close its cost-per-bit gap with Samsung and SK Hynix, and that gap should stay above 30%.

Both numbers come from same-day published research, and they point in opposite directions. A gap this wide is close to an admission that analysts don't actually know what CXMT is worth yet

2026-07-27 · Tech Times, citing Nomura and Morningstar research

There's another variable that could work against CXMT: the United States. Starting December 23, 2027, US federal agencies will be barred from procuring CXMT products. Its HBM production is also targeted for 2027, roughly three years behind Samsung and SK Hynix, which are already shipping HBM in volume. Some regulators and market participants worried an offering this large would drain liquidity from other Chinese tech stocks, but state media dismissed the concern, arguing the broader market could absorb the capital.

What settles this

The question comes down to one thing. Was the 472% pop a read on real demand, or an illusion created by a razor-thin tradable float. If the price holds up when the lock-up expires on January 27, 2027, Nomura's read gains ground. If a flood of newly tradable shares sends it sharply lower, Morningstar's read gains ground. Until then, no one can say with confidence where the answer sits between Nomura's 116 yuan and Morningstar's 14.90 yuan. The one thing that's certain right now is that this listing has become the new reference point in China's chip self-sufficiency rally.

In three lines
  • China's CXMT surged 472% on its Shanghai listing debut, hitting a $489 billion market cap in a single day.
  • On the surface it looks like a clean win for China's chip self-sufficiency rally.
  • But only 6.73% of its shares were actually tradable, and Nomura's ¥116 target sits 678 percentage points above Morningstar's ¥14.90.
