AMD's Market Forecast Got Raised to $1.4 Trillion. The Number Traces Back to AMD's Own CEO

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

Chip-industry commentator Jukan relayed a note from analyst Jeff Pu raising the AI accelerator market forecast to $1.4 trillion by 2030, up from $1 trillion, alongside claims that AMD's MI455X delivers 34 times the throughput and an 18th of the cost per token of its predecessor, already beating Nvidia's B200 on some workloads.

Where does that number actually come from

According to Business Standard, the $1.4 trillion figure traces directly to AMD CEO Lisa Su's own keynote at AMD's Advancing AI event in San Jose on July 22. She told the audience compute infrastructure spending would reach $2 trillion by 2030 (up from $365 billion in 2025), with the AI accelerator market itself growing to roughly $1.4 trillion. Jeff Pu is a real analyst, a managing director at GF Securities in Hong Kong, but his background is Apple and hardware supply chains, not AI datacenter market modeling. Gate News reported GF Securities raised its AMD price target to $640 around the same event, consistent with a note relaying the company's own guidance rather than independent bottoms-up research.

Who actually checked the 34x and 18x numbers

No mainstream outlet independently reproduced or verified those specific figures. They circulate only through the Jukan account. SemiAnalysis's own technical teardown called the MI455X's real edge over Nvidia's Rubin relatively mild, and said AMD's low cost-per-token claims stem from equity-rebate deals AMD offers customers like OpenAI and Meta, not from raw silicon performance.

An independent analyst did the research and raised AMD's addressable market 40% on the merits.

What's actually confirmed

The number originates from AMD's own keynote, SemiAnalysis calls the MI455X's edge mild, and AMD's real market share sits in the mid-single digits.

Whether to read this as independent validation or repackaged company guidance depends on that gap.

So what's the thing to actually watch

Only one thing settles this: how MI455X and MI500 actually perform in independent benchmarks once they ship, and whether AMD's share moves out of the single digits. A note that just echoes a company's own event-day guidance deserves skepticism, especially when the price target and earnings estimates stayed unchanged even as the market forecast jumped.

  • On its face that reads like independent research validating AMD's competitiveness.
