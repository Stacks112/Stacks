The ECB can just buy, people say. The conditions for that were written down in 2022

The always-thoughtful Olivier Blanchard examines the multiple equilibria aspects of France’s fiscal situation. (This analytical framework is also applicable to some other economies.)#economy #markets @ojblanchard1 #debt

August 15, 2026

The expectation that the European Central Bank will buy your government bonds comes with conditions that are written down. The first one is not being subject to an excessive deficit procedure, or, if subject to one, not being assessed as having failed to take effective action in response to a Council recommendation. France has been subject to that procedure since July 2024.

The Transmission Protection Instrument (European Central Bank press release, 2022-07-21) ↗

The reason this comes up now is a post Olivier Blanchard wrote on August 15 about France's public finances, which Mohamed El-Erian passed along with the note that the framework fits some other economies too.

What the phrase multiple equilibria points at

Multiple equilibria describes a situation in which the same conditions can produce two different outcomes. If investors believe the debt is safe, rates stay low and it can indeed be serviced; if they believe it is unsafe, rates rise and servicing really does become hard. Belief produces the outcome. It works like a run on a bank. If everyone thinks the money is there, it is; if everyone goes to withdraw at once, it genuinely is not.

Safe zoneDebt is clearly serviceable and the central bank has nothing to do
→
Danger zoneA good outcome and a bad one sit side by side, and the bad one is avoided if the central bank buys when private investors sell
→
Default zoneDebt is clearly not serviceable, fiscal policy has to be adjusted first, and this is not the central bank's job

Each zone calls for a different response, so not knowing which one you are standing in means not being able to pick the response.

This is where Blanchard puts his finger. When investors demand a higher risk premium, the central bank does not know for sure whether this is the danger zone, where it should step in, or already the default zone, where absent an adjustment programme it should not. So it leans toward asking for the programme first, and counting on it to remove the bad equilibrium on its own, he wrote, is unwise.

The rating agencies have moved one way. Moody's cut in December 2024, Fitch followed in September 2025 and S&P in October 2025. France now carries A+ at S&P and Fitch, and one notch above that at Moody's, which is the only one attaching a negative outlook. That is not the rating of a country facing default, and it is not a rating that tells you the country is in the safe zone either. It is closest to the middle of Blanchard's three zones, and the middle is precisely the one where the answer is not settled.

The four lines written on July 21, 2022

When the ECB created a purchase tool called the Transmission Protection Instrument on July 21, 2022, it published four eligibility criteria alongside it. Compliance with the EU fiscal framework, the absence of severe macroeconomic imbalances, debt sustainability, and sound and sustainable macroeconomic policies. The first criterion reads as not being subject to an excessive deficit procedure, or not being assessed as having failed to take effective action in response to an EU Council recommendation. France entered that procedure on July 26, 2024, and on January 21, 2025 the Council adopted a recommendation to correct the deficit by 2029. The European Commission's compliance report of June 3, 2026 still lists France among the countries with an open procedure. The conjunction in that sentence carries weight. Being subject to the procedure does not by itself remove eligibility; eligibility survives if the country is assessed as taking effective action. France's standing therefore rests with the Commission's assessment rather than with the ECB. Where Blanchard expects the central bank to ask for an adjustment programme first, he is restating something already set down as a condition. No instance of the instrument actually being activated could be found. That it has stood unused for four years is itself part of what has held markets in place. How much of an expectation that the central bank will buy belongs in the price of the bonds is what splits here. Fitch reviews France again on August 28. If the rating holds, the ground stays where it is; one notch lower and the market starts reading the conditions instead.

In three lines
  • El-Erian passed along Blanchard's account of France's finances split into three zones: safe, danger, and default.
  • On the surface, in the danger zone a central bank that stands ready to buy when private investors sell keeps the crisis away.
  • But the conditions on which the ECB would buy were written down on July 21, 2022, in four lines, and France stands where the first line bites.
