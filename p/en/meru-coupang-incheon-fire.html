Coupang's Incheon warehouse fire brought under control after 61 hours, insurance structure eases the blow

Open in the Stacks app → Read the original ↗

A large fire at Coupang's Incheon Seongnam-dong fulfillment center was brought under primary control after 61 hours. The building and most of its contents are likely a near-total loss, according to Korean blogger Meru.

What was different in 2021

Meru compares it to Coupang's 2021 Deokpyeong warehouse fire, which was later traced to an electrical fault, took 55 hours to contain and 6 days to fully extinguish. That blaze cost Coupang 340 billion won in booked losses, and the company still collected 360 billion won in insurance, avoiding a major hit. The Incheon facility, more than twice the size of Deokpyeong, is insured for a combined 869 billion won across seven insurers.

The trade title InsuranceAsia News puts insured losses from the fire at up to $590 million, which is the 869 billion won of cover counted in dollars. It stops at the total and does not set out how the seven underwriters split it, with DB Insurance carrying 40 percent of the building policy, Meritz Fire 20 percent and Hana Insurance 15 percent, Samsung Fire 10 percent and Hanwha, KB and Hyundai Marine 5 percent each. At Deokpyeong, 360 billion won was paid against a 400 billion won policy limit. Roughly 90 percent of the limit came through, which fits the reading above that insurance absorbed most of the direct damage.

Why is the rent coverage thin

Coupang covers 428 billion won of inventory and equipment mainly through Meritz and DB. Landlord Igis Asset Management, which bought the building from original developer KKR, covers the structure and 60 billion won of rent-loss insurance separately.

Coupang warehouse fire triggers liability questions (Business Insurance, 2026-07-27)
출처: businessinsurance.com

Business Insurance reports the same split structure, noting that Coupang operates the center but does not own it, so landlord and tenant each hold and claim on their own policy. Coupang has been a tenant on a 10-year lease since 2024, and years of it still remain, so the longer the center stays dark, the more unpaid rent piles up. The same report puts what Igis can claim at up to 440 billion won ($299 million), the figure that roughly 380 billion won of property cover and roughly 60 billion won of business interruption cover add up to. What is called 60 billion won of rent-loss insurance above is filed as business interruption insurance, but it is the same money meant to bridge a period with no rent coming in. It counts Coupang's own side at 330 billion won for inventory and facilities, a different tally from the 428 billion won above, while the Igis side still lands in the 440 billion won range, so the rent portion alone stays thin. That rent-loss coverage is worth only about a year and a half of rent against an estimated 36 billion won of annual rent. If the center stays offline longer than that, Igis's fund could face a stretch it is not covered for.

What happens to deliveries

Deliveries are expected to hold up since six nearby Coupang centers can absorb the volume.

Why it matters · Coupang's 2021 fire showed insurance can absorb most of the direct damage, but the split ownership structure here means the landlord's fund carries thin rent-loss coverage, so how long the center stays offline determines whether this stays a manageable line item or turns into a real dispute over who eats the loss.

Worth asking · Coupang's inventory losses look well covered by insurance just like in 2021, but the landlord's rent-loss policy only stretches to about a year and a half: if the center stays dark longer than that, who ends up eating the gap?
